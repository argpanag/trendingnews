  3. Time for Europe to see how big Fenerbahce is - Kuytpublished at 17:02 GMT
    17:02 GMT

    Fenerbahce v Liverpool (17:45 GMT)

    Aadam Patel
    Football Reporter in Istanbul

    Dirk Kuyt insists that it will be a "special occasion" but he will have to park his emotions for Liverpool's Champions League game against Fenerbahce in Istanbul.

    Kuyt - who played for Liverpool between 2006 and 2012 and scored the winning penalty for the Reds in the 2007 Champions League semi-final - is now the interim Fenerbahce boss after Ismail Kartal resigned last week.

    The former Dutch international played for Fenerbahce between 2012 and 2015 and won the Turkish Super Lig. He was Kartal's assistant before stepping up to the role.

    Asked about his feelings pre-match, Kuyt said: "It's a really special achievement for Fenerbahce to be in the Champions League after 18 years and we're really excited. When you reach that stage, you know you'll be playing against very impressive teams.

    "As soon as the draw was done, I was really excited because everyone knows how much Fenerbahce and Liverpool mean to me and from that moment, you know it will be a special occasion.

    "Of course, tomorrow will be a very special occasion for me but at the stage we're in, I'm not here for myself. I'm here for the club and that's what is important - to show the fans who deserve much more, a reaction, positive energy and a good performance."

  7. From Derby to Champions League - Brown's journey to face Redspublished at 16:51 GMT
    16:51 GMT

    Fenerbahce v Liverpool (17:45 GMT)

    Aadam Patel
    Football Reporter in Istanbul

    For Archie Brown, playing for Fenerbahce against Liverpool in the Champions League on Wednesday will mark another step in the justification of his own "unorthodox" journey that began as a young boy in the Midlands.

    Brown, 24, was born in Birmingham and started his youth career at West Brom before joining Derby, where he helped them to their only youth top-flight title.

    He scored a hat-trick in a 5-2 win over an Arsenal U18s side that included Bukayo Saka in the final between the North and South division winners.

    Yet come 2021, the Englishman was frustrated by a lack of first-team opportunities and left to join Lausanne Sport in Switzerland at the age of 19.

    "I've had quite an unorthodox journey to get here. I won't say that was always the plan," Brown tells BBC Sport.

    "It was a mixture of two things. I'd seen so many talented players stay on the fringes of the first-team, playing with the U21s and then by 22, 23, they have no real first-team experience.

    "And they have to go down into the lower leagues or non-league to try and build their career up from there. I didn't want to fall victim to that.

    "Secondly, I felt it was important to get out of my environment, to learn a new language, experience a new culture, to help me grow up and mature as a person," Brown adds.

    The left-back - who also plays in central defence and midfield - joined Belgian side Gent in 2023, before moving to Fenerbahce in a £7m deal in 2025.

    The Turkish Super Lig side may be struggling for form - they are sixth and nine points adrift of leaders Galatasaray after 10 games - but Brown is adamant that Andoni Iraola's Liverpool will face an almighty challenge at the 48,000-capacity Sukru Saracoglu Stadium.
