Poisonous invasive cane toad 'size of a guinea pig' spotted in Tauranga

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:54 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Environment

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._v4.laWzzQVil69j0DGqQk4Ywh3JMAvc5jXoA1Fn8ldP8DY/environment/2801504%2Fpoisonous-invasive-cane-toad-size-of-a-guinea-pig-spotted-in-tauranga

An invasive cane toad "the size of a guinea pig" was spotted in Tauranga this week.

Volunteer firefighter Annie Henley was training by the Tauranga Model Powerboat Club in Tauriko on Tuesday night when she saw the poisonous toad.

Henley said at the time she had no idea what she was seeing was a cane toad and thought it was just a large frog.

"We were finishing up a training and pulling in some big hoses that were in the water and what I thought was a frog, jumped out of the water."

Henley said she and a friend went over to the toad and she began filming as she thought "it was the biggest frog I've ever seen".

She posted the video on Instagram and said a friend who was a conservationist messaged her and said she needed to contact the Ministry of Primary Industries (MPI).

"My friend got in touch and they [MPI] went to investigate. MPI called me the next day asking for exact coordinates."

Annie Henley is a volunteer firefighter.

AnnieGProNZ / SUPPLIED

Henley said the toad had jumped into long grass and had been about the size of a guinea pig.

MPI Biosecurity NZ's manager of surveillance and incursion Wendy McDonald confirmed there had been a report of a single exotic toad sighting in Tauriko.

"We've sent staff to investigate.

"Cane toads can occasionally arrive here as hitchhikers, most commonly from Australia. The risk to the public is low, provided people avoid handling the toad and wash thoroughly after any contact."

Cane toads weren't established in Aotearoa, McDonald said, possibly due to our climate being cooler than Australia's.

"We're going to check the area thoroughly. From the video, it appears to be adult male cane toad."

If anyone thought they had seen a cane toad McDonald said they should report it to Biosecurity NZ immediately on 0800 80 99 66 or by emailing info@mpi.govt.nz or report.mpi.govt.nz

McDonald said people should take photos and note the location if it was safe to do so and avoid touching the toad and keep pets away.
