Comedian and presenter PJ Gallagher dies aged 51

TheTools · 27.11.2026 22:13 · source · permalink

Comedian and radio presenter PJ Gallagher has died, aged 51.

The stand-up comedian and actor known for Naked Camera and The Young Offenders also worked on television documentaries, features and radio, including as part of the breakfast show team on Radio Nova with presenter Jim McCabe.

His family has appealed for privacy at this time.

Gallagher first rose to prominence on RTÉ with popular hidden-camera series Naked Camera in 2005.

As well as co-creating the show, Gallagher played the newspaper swinging Jake Stevens, among other iconic characters.

The hidden camera show involved Gallagher and his co-stars, such as Father Ted's Pat McDonnell and comedian Maeve Higgins, jovially accosting unsuspecting members of the public.

One encounter saw Gallagher, in character, taking RTÉ presenter Bill O'Herlihy on a prolonged taxi journey - at one point asking Mr O'Herlihy if he would "drop in" to his mother's house.

Later, Gallagher starred in sketch show Meet Your Neighbours, in which he played all the core roles.

More recently, he appeared in The Young Offenders TV series as principal Barry Walsh. He had played a drug dealer in the initial film version of the series.

His most recent television work included hosting The Random Driving Test, a series that premiered on RTÉ One in September.

Earlier this year, he also appeared in Uncharted with Ray Goggins when he joined actor Liam Cunningham and host Ray Goggins for the season two premiere on RTÉ One, embarking on an extreme motorbike expedition through the Himalayas.

Also a stand up comedian, Gallagher took the stage at the Iveagh Gardens for six editions of the Vodafone Comedy Festival, as it was then known.

He was a keen supporter of Dublin GAA and Bohemians Football Club.

Gallagher, who lived in Dublin, was the father of twins, son Milo and daughter Stevie, who he shared with his partner, Kelly Doolin.

Gallagher and co-host Jim McCabe did not appear on their Radio Nova show Morning Glory this week, with guest presenter Dee Woods filling in with snippets of pre-recorded material from the regular hosts. Woods had said the pair would return on Monday.

Radio Nova CEO Kevin Branigan said: "PJ was an extraordinary talent and an integral part of the family here at Nova.

"His warmth, humour, generosity of spirit and ability to connect with people brought joy to everyone and to all of us who had the privilege of working alongside him.

"His loss will be felt deeply by his colleagues, friends, listeners and the wider entertainment community.

"Our thoughts and deepest sympathies are with PJ's sister and children at this incredibly difficult time."

Gallagher had spoken openly about his struggles with severe depression and anxiety, including an 11-week inpatient stay at St Patrick's Mental Health Services.

In a radio interview with Brendan O'Connor in April, Gallagher described mental illness as a "liar" in your head.

It was part of a regular item in which guests talk about five songs that have had a profound impact on their lives.

Gallagher chose tracks by Damien Dempsey, Tom Waits, The Fat Lady Sings, a song from Christy Dignam's solo album, and the theme tune from TV series Minder, which he dedicated to his Nova co-host McCabe.

In May 2024, he featured in documentary PJ Gallagher: Changing My Mind, an RTÉ documentary exploring mental health in Ireland.
