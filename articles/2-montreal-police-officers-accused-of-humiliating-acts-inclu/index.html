2 Montreal police officers accused of humiliating acts, including urinating on homeless people | CBC News

TheTools · 16.10.2026 23:21 · source · permalink

Allegations 'undermine the dignity every person deserves,' says Montreal mayor

Montreal police have suspended two officers who work at a downtown precinct over alleged acts of humiliation directed at unhoused and racialized people.

One of the officers was suspended after colleagues alleged he urinated on unhoused people while in uniform, sources told Radio-Canada.

The 32-year-old officer is a former member of the military with three years of experience with Montreal police. Sources say he allegedly filmed himself over the past 18 months while on duty, humiliating people experiencing homelessness to the point of urinating on them while they slept in the street.

According to Radio-Canada, investigators searched the officer's locker in mid-August, and he has been suspended with pay ever since.

The internal probe is also reportedly targeting private social media groups where the officer allegedly shared his personal videos in recent months. Police had already infiltrated those online groups using investigative warrants.

The second officer, who has less than five years of service, allegedly took selfies with racialized individuals he was arresting, Radio-Canada reports. Other allegations of racist behaviour by that officer are also under investigation.

2 SPVM officers suspended for alleged acts of humiliation aimed at marginalized groups

The suspensions against the officers, both of whom are based at Station 21, were first reported Friday morning by La Presse.

In a statement to CBC News, the Service de police de la Ville de Montréal (SPVM) confirmed it has launched an internal investigation into the two officers.

"Be assured that the SPVM takes any allegation against one of its members seriously," the police service said. "We are determined to get to the bottom of things and take the required actions."

The SPVM declined to provide further details on the investigation, citing privacy obligations under provincial law.

In another statement Friday evening, police said while they cannot confirm the allegations, they acknowledged that "people experiencing homelessness are an integral part of our community and deserve, like everyone else, to be treated with humanity, respect, and dignity."

Advocate denounces 'degrading' act

The allegations come as Montreal prepares for La Nuit des sans-abri, an annual event held across Quebec to raise awareness about homelessness, and as the SPVM deals with the fallout of another scandal involving its officers.

A patrol unit at Station 39 in Montréal-Nord was disbanded last June due to serious allegations of racist and hateful acts allegedly committed by officers against Black and Arab people.

Annie Savage, executive director of the advocacy group RAPSIM (Réseau d'aide aux personnes seules et itinérantes de Montréal), said she was "extremely shocked" but not surprised by the new allegations, citing what she regularly hears from people living on the streets.

"It's hard to imagine a more degrading or violent act," Savage told Radio-Canada's Tout un matin.

Savage added that relations between police and unhoused residents have deteriorated as the city's homelessness crisis has deepened. She warned that police should not treat the incident as an isolated case.

An unhoused encampment is seen on Notre-Dame Street in Montreal. Homelessness has surged in the city since the pandemic. (Christinne Muschi/The Canadian Press)

A joint study by RAPSIM and the legal aid organization Clinique Droits Devant shed light on the heightened tension between unhoused Montrealers and law enforcement, including Montreal police officers and special constables patrolling the city's transit network.

The study, published last June, found that 40 per cent of unhoused individuals surveyed felt heavily ticketed or targeted, while 66 per cent reported rarely or never being treated like other members of the public.

None of the respondents expressed high confidence in police during an emergency, with many saying they avoid calling 911 out of fear of being treated as suspects.

Mayor, police union issue condemnations

In a statement to CBC, Montreal Mayor Soraya Martinez Ferrada said the allegations that surfaced on Friday were "extremely serious and undermine the dignity every person deserves."

She added: "I applaud the police officers who spoke up to report this situation. The investigation must now run its course so the full truth can come to light."

Mayor Soraya Martinez Ferrada condemned the alleged acts by Montreal police. (Ivanoh Demers/Radio-Canada)

The Montreal police brotherhood, the union representing SPVM officers, said it "firmly condemns" any behaviour that violates human dignity or breaches professional standards.

While declining to comment on the specific allegations, the brotherhood called the reported conduct contrary to the values of Montreal police officers.

Duane Mansfield, who works with unhoused people at the non-profit Maison du Père, said he feels the SPVM has generally tried to improve how it interacts with people experiencing homelessness.

But he said he finds it "very concerning" that an officer could commit such acts.

"It shouldn't happen to any citizen in Montreal," he said.
