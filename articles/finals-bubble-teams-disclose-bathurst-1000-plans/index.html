Finals bubble teams disclose Bathurst 1000 plans

TheTools · 08.10.2026 20:28 · source · permalink

COMPLEMENTING the battle for the Peter Brock Trophy this Sunday will be a crucial alternate storyline regarding the status of the 10th and last Supercars finals ticket.

Eight playoffs berths are already locked in, and that might as well be nine unless extraordinary circumstances conspire against Ryan Wood.

As for the 10th spot, it’s game on with just 87 points covering five contenders: Jack Le Brocq (Matt Stone Racing), André Heimgartner (Brad Jones Racing), Thomas Randle (Tickford Racing), Jayden Ojeda (PremiAir Racing) and James Golding (Blanchard Racing Team).

Victory in the Repco Bathurst 1000 is worth 300 points.

V8 Sleuth canvassed the relevant teams on how much they’re willing to prioritise one car over the other in the quest for finals.

The firmest stance arguably came from PremiAir team principal Roland Dane: “We’re not going to pass up an opportunity to be in there.

“We might not really be clear (on the situation) until there’s a lap or two to go. But we’re certainly not going to throw away an opportunity if there is one.”

MSR team manager Paul Forgie said there will “absolutely” be plenty of scoreboard watching going on as the 161-lap race pans out.

“All we can do is the best possible job, make no mistakes in the pits, no mistakes with strategy, and the drivers keep it on the track, and when we get to the last lap of the race hopefully we’re far enough up the road,” he said.

“If we have to play some team orders to change a couple of little points, I guess everybody will be trying to do something similar. Hopefully we’re winning the race and you can’t do anything better than that.”

Brad Jones noted it’s not something his team – which fields three cars – has discussed in great detail but that everyone is aware of what’s at stake.

“I think it’s important we have a car in the finals. Hopefully it won’t come to that, and I don’t know how that looks anyway,” he told V8 Sleuth of prioritising Car #8.

“Is one position going to make all the difference? Well, I’m not going to get a bloke to roll out if he’s leading the race. So we’ll just see what happens.”

That stance was largely shared by BRT’s Kate Harrington.

“We’ve briefly had a chat, and that’s something we will discuss later throughout the weekend as we progress and qualify and see where our cars are at,” she said.

“We’re not going to compromise one car over the other, for example if Car #3 is going for a podium result and #7 is fifth, I don’t think that’s fair to do that.

“We’ve got ourselves in the situation that we’re currently in but definitely it’s on our mind. If it does come down to it, we’ll work together to try to get the best result for the team and get Jimmy in finals.

“A lot of the other teams as you see play the team game quite well, especially those front teams, so that’s something we have watched and analysed since Tailem Bend to understand what we need to do and how we need to best manage that for both cars.”

Perhaps in the trickiest situation of all is Tickford.

Unlike the other four teams, its car not on the finals bubble is the already-qualified Cam Waters, who not only is a fair chance of fighting for Bathurst victory but is also trying to secure the best possible seeding for the Gold Coast.

“We spoke about it last week in pre-brief. Obviously the focus was to get Tommy in. It’s going to be tight, we know that,” team manager Matt Roberts told V8 Sleuth.

“Equally too, we want Cam to be in a good position going into Gold Coast as well.

“So the conversation was making sure we leave here with both cars in good positions. But in all honesty, our main focus has been on the 1000.”

It’s worth keeping in mind that the 10th seed last year, Kai Allen, ended up making it all the way through to the Adelaide Grand Final, getting within 83 points of winning the championship.
