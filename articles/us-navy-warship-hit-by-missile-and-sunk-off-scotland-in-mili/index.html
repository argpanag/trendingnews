US Navy warship hit by missile and sunk off Scotland in military exercise

TheTools · 28.09.2026 15:06 · source · permalink

A U.S Navy warship has been hit by a missile and sunk off the coast of Scotland as part of a joint military operation.

The USS Klakring was struck by a missile from the USS Paul Ignatius, with Royal Navy surface vessels and aircraft also involved.

Video posted by the American forces showed the frigate being completely destroyed as part of Atlantic Thunder 2026.

The exercise took place at the Ministry of Defence's Hebrides range.

The exercise was initially planned for May but was delayed by four months, and is officially described as a "multi-domain exercise designed to validate surface weapon kill chains, build resilient kill webs and deliver strategic messaging through kinetic demonstration".

The ship being hit (Image: US Navy)

The sinking of the Klakring by co-ordination between British and American forces was planned as the centrepiece of the exercise.

USS Klakring was decommissioned in 2013 and has been stored at the Philadelphia Naval Inactive Ship Maintenance Facility (NISMF) since. She was towed down the Delaware River on 12th August in preparation for the voyage across the Atlantic.

The frigate was initially towed to Campbeltown, and had all hazardous materials removed before being sent to the bottom of the ocean.

It is unclear why an American vessel was used, given there is an ongoing programme of towing British frigates to Turkey to be scrapped, with HMS Northumberland towed out of Plymouth earlier this month.
