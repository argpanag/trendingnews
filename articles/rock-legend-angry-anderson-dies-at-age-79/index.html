Rock legend Angry Anderson dies at age 79

Rock singer and Rose Tattoo frontman Gary “Angry” Anderson, who has died at the age of 79, had good reason to be angry.

Born into a household riven by violence, to a Mauritian mother and an Anglo-Australian father he later described as “a very explosive person”, from the age of five, young Gary was sexually abused by a friend of the family.

Finding little comfort at home, he turned to a music-playing, hard-living bikie uncle as a role model, and after a stint as a fitter and turner, he made his way to rock ’n’ roll – first with ambitions to be a guitarist, before switching to vocals, which is where he made his indelible mark on the Australian music scene, principally as frontman of Rose Tattoo.

Though he stood just 156 centimetres tall, Anderson presented as a hard-as-nails character, thanks to the shaven head, the permascowl, the tattoos and the denim-and-leather look he favoured throughout his career.

On screen, he played to type, with his turn as tough guy Ironbar in Mad Max: Beyond Thunderdome, the role for which he was perhaps best known. But the vast majority of his screen work was made with Paul Fenech, the leading auteur of Aussie bogan comedy.

Over three feature films and around 40 episodes of television, Anderson appeared in everything from Pizza and Fat Pizza (in which Rebel Wilson first came to prominence) to Housos to Dumb Criminals. Typically, he played a character called Angry.

“Of all the people I worked with on Housos, he was one of the few who became what I would call a dear friend,” Fenech told this masthead on Wednesday afternoon as he took stock of news that Anderson had died.

“We spent a lot of time together. He had these famous barbecues where legends of the ’70s and the ’80s would come to his place, guys from Rose Tattoo, AC/DC, etc. And we did a motorbike trip to Parkes together for the Elvis Festival.”

Fenech says his friend always struck him as “pretty bulletproof”, despite having “suffered a lot when he was younger”.

Anderson’s politics drifted rightwards in his later years. He stood for parliament for the Nationals twice, and ran as a Senate candidate for the Islamophobic Australian Liberty Alliance in 2016.

But Fenech, who reportedly has Maltese and Indigenous heritage, insists it would be wrong to characterise him as a bigot.

“He embraced me as a friend, and my father was a migrant. He had a plethora of people from all walks of life, backgrounds, religions. He was a friend to everybody, really.”

Some might dispute that on the basis of the views expressed by Anderson himself.

Describing his political position as “centre”, Anderson wrote on his blog in January about the curse of “Left-driven thinking”, which had led to “an out-of-control crime problem”. He also pointed the finger at immigration, lax parenting, the prevalence of female teachers in schools, and a lack of respect for war veterans – all symptoms, in his view, of the “correctness brigade” at work.

At the personal level, though, Fenech insists he saw a totally different person.

“Even though he had the nickname Angry he was – at least in the time I’ve known him, which has been about 20 years – probably the least angry person I ever met. He was just the quintessential good bloke.”

Anderson’s death was confirmed in a statement shared by his bandmates and family on Wednesday. Anderson joined the group in 1976, soon after it was founded by guitarist Pete Wells, and served as its lead vocalist until shortly before his death.

“It is with great sadness we announce the passing of Gary ‘Angry’ Anderson on the eve of Monday 21 September 2026, surrounded by his loved ones here in Australia,” the statement read.

“Angry was a devoted father, and much-loved bandmate, friend, and member of the wider music and entertainment community. His loss will be deeply felt by all those who knew and loved him both here in Australia and overseas.”

Anderson suffered a cardiac arrest in late August and was admitted to a hospital to undergo treatment.

On August 10, the band announced the cancellation of the European leg of its One Last Ride tour after Anderson was hospitalised due to a “medical issue”. It was set to be the band’s final tour before stepping away after more than five decades of performing live. The band had begun its European tour in July, and had nine more shows to go when the cancellation was announced.

Rose Tattoo were scheduled to return to Australia this month to perform several farewell gigs across the state capitals.

The veteran rocker’s music career first picked up major steam when Rose Tattoo released its self-titled debut album in 1978. The record was well received both at home and abroad, climbing to the top of UK heavy metal charts.

Anderson’s solo single, Suddenly, released in 1987, became a huge success after it was played during the wedding of Scott and Charlene (played by Jason Donovan and Kylie Minogue) on the TV soap Neighbours. The track shot to No.2 on the Australian charts at the time, and No.3 on the UK singles chart.

Rose Tattoo released a slew of other well-known tracks with Anderson as lead vocalist, including Bad Boy for Love (1977), Rock ‘N’ Roll Outlaw (1978) and We Can’t Be Beaten (1982). In 1978, the band released the song Nice Boys, which American rockers Guns N’ Roses famously went on to cover on their 1986 EP Live ?!*@ Like a Suicide (and again on their 1988 EP G N’ R Lies).

The group disbanded in 1987, though briefly reassembled in 1993 to open for Guns N’ Roses. In 1998, they officially regrouped and began touring again. Rose Tattoo were inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame in 2006.

In an Instagram post, legendary rock singer Jimmy Barnes farewelled his close friend and collaborator.

“We lost a good man this week. Angry Anderson sang from his heart with power and conviction,” Barnes wrote. “But don’t let that tough outside fool you. He was a caring soul who loved his family, cared for his friends and stood up for the downtrodden.”

“Rock in Peace my dear friend.”

David Campbell, son of Jimmy Barnes and co-host of Nine’s Today Extra, paid tribute to the veteran rocker who sang at his wedding to Lisa Hewitt in 2008.

“I got to share many a stage and sporting field with Angry Anderson,” Campbell wrote. “He was a great frontman with a wicked sense of humour and a huge heart. His generosity went above and beyond when he performed our bridal dance with his hit ballad, Suddenly. I can never repay him for that memory. My condolences to his family and the members and crew of Rose Tattoo.”

Singer Marcia Hines, paying tribute on social media, said: “I’m saddened to hear of the passing of Angry Anderson. He was one of the most kind and thoughtful guys in rock and roll.”

Liberal leader Angus Taylor described Anderson as “an Australian icon”.

“He looked tough, sounded tough and built a career as one of Australia’s great rockers. But behind it was a good bloke with a kind soul. A man who cared deeply about people, particularly young Australians doing it tough as he did.”

Anderson also appeared on stage as King Herod in the 1992 Australian arena concert Jesus Christ Superstar alongside actor Anthony Warlow and singers John Farnham and Jon Stevens. He became a prominent advocate for prostate cancer research after some of his bandmates died from the disease, including Wells. And in 1993, Anderson was made a Member of the Order of Australia for his role as a youth advocate.

In their statement on Wednesday, the rocker’s family and bandmates asked for privacy and thanked fans for their support. “We kindly ask that their wishes are respected as they come to terms with this devastating loss and take time they need to grieve. We thank everyone for their prayers and messages of love and continued support,” it read.

“Stay young, grow strong.”
