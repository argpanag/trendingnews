‘The dog has caught the car’: Why AOC is campaigning in Michigan’s Trump country

TheTools · 15.10.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, D-N.Y., is heading into deep-red territory on Thursday, campaigning in Michigan’s Upper Peninsula for Abdul El-Sayed in a Senate race that could determine control of the chamber.

It could also shape her own political future.

El-Sayed, a progressive, is running neck and neck with former Republican Rep. Mike Rogers. He won the Democratic nomination in August after narrowly defeating Rep. Haley Stevens, a moderate, in a bitter and bruising primary that became a proxy war over the direction of the party.

With El-Sayed at the top of the ticket, Democrats are betting that a liberal candidate with progressive policies can win in a purple state — a risky wager that will reverberate through the 2028 presidential race and the progressive movement.

A win, they argue, could help propel Ocasio-Cortez to mount a presidential run in 2028.

“The dog has caught the car here and we will soon find out if progressives are right when they say that a strong progressive candidate can compete in purple places,” a progressive House Democrat told MS NOW, requesting anonymity to speak candidly.

“I think AOC’s future is bright and it doesn’t hinge on this Michigan Senate race,” the lawmaker said, “but it would certainly help her a lot.”

Ocasio-Cortez appears to understand the stakes. At a fundraiser for El-Sayed in New York City earlier this month, she cast his race as proof in sweeping terms.

“What we do is that we break open the entire political calculus in America — period,” Ocasio-Cortez said, according to an audio recording of the event obtained by news outlets.

“Because if you can win, if Abdul can win in Michigan,” she said, “anybody can win anywhere.”

That anybody may be her.

Thursday’s swing will be Ocasio-Cortez’s first trip to Michigan since El-Sayed’s primary win. She will join him at town halls in Escanaba, Iron Mountain and Marquette, all stops on his “Shouldn’t Be This Hard” bus tour.

Turnout and excitement about those events could say a lot about El-Sayed’s chances in November and Ocasio-Cortez’s political future.

Michigan’s Upper Peninsula has for years been conservative territory. Michigan’s 1st Congressional District, which encompasses the area, broke for Trump by more than 20 percentage points in 2024, according to The Downballot, and it’s been represented by Republican Rep. Jack Bergman since 2017.

This won’t be El-Sayed’s first foray into conservative turf. On Monday, he posted a video on X from an event in Ionia County, which, he noted, is an R plus-32 area.

“We’re coming for ya, Mike,” El-Sayed said, taking aim at his Republican opponent.
