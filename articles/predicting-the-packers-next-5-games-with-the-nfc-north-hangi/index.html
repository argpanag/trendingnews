Predicting the Packers' next 5 games with the NFC North hanging in the balance

TheTools · 24.11.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Coming out of the bye week, the Green Bay Packers need to go on a run.

We haven't technically entered "must-win" territory for some of these games, but the NFC North race has heated up to the point that the Packers truly need to approach every week with a playoff mindset.

Every single game over the next month is going to have huge implications for the Packers' chances of not only contending for the NFC North, but even competing for a playoff spot in the loaded NFC. Let's take a look at the next five games for Green Bay, starting with a crucial Wednesday night matchup against the Los Angeles Rams on Thanksgiving Eve, and try to predict the outcome of each.

Predicting the Packers' next 5 games coming out of the bye week

Week 12: Packers at Rams (6-4)

Wednesday, November 25, 1 p.m. CT (Netflix)

There is simply no way the Packers are losing in the season debut of Micah Parsons.

Okay, that might be a little dramatic. Even though this game is being played in Los Angeles, we're not talking about a Hollywood script. I still wholeheartedly believe that the emotional jolt the Packers are going to get from having Parsons back in the lineup is going to be tangible.

This isn't just a regular player returning in season from an injury -- this is Micah Parsons. Parsons is a game-wrecker in every sense of the word, and the Packers are getting him back at just the right time, facing off against the reigning MVP Matthew Stafford in a gotta-have-it matchup on the road.

The Rams have been dealing with a lot of injuries of their own this season, and while their defensive front will be licking its chops at the chance to go against a struggling Packers offense, I trust that Jordan Love will bounce back in a big way after the loss the Packers took to the Vikings before the bye.

The return of Parsons will bring yet another presence to a Packers defense that has forced a ton of turnovers already this season. Parsons might even be the one to close out the game with a key sack or strip.

Prediction: Packers win 30-27 (7-4)

Week 13: Packers at Saints

Sunday, December 6, Noon CT

If you want the formula for beating the New Orleans Saints this year, it appears to be fairly simple: Get out to an early lead.

The Saints have been outscored 47-9 in the first quarter of games this season. They have struggled to get off the field on third downs (26th in third-down defense), and the Packers can take advantage of both of those things.

They are also minus-8 in turnover differential this season, and the Packers have been forcing takeaways unlike almost anyone else in the NFL. They have 20 so far this season, averaging two per game. How do you go on the road and avoid a "trap" after playing another NFC contender?

Force takeaways and jump out to an early lead. That's exactly what I see the Packers doing in this one against the Saints. They take care of business and win by 10.

Prediction: Packers win 27-17 (8-4)

Week 14: Packers vs. Bills

Sunday, December 13, 8:20 p.m. CT (SNF)

This is going to be a really great litmus test for the Packers and will rightfully be talked about as a possible Super Bowl preview.

This game will be played under the lights of Sunday Night Football, and it's an opportunity for the Packers to prove to the entire NFL world how serious a contender they are in the NFC.

Josh Allen was playing like an MVP earlier this season, but has dropped off in recent weeks. This game on the road against the Packers is going to be one of a number of huge tests Allen has upcoming (vs. Chiefs on Thanksgiving, at Patriots, at Packers, vs. Bears), and the Bills haven't had a ton of those this season.

It's also going to be a huge test for rookie head coach Joe Brady and defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard. Can they win the chess match with Matt LaFleur and Jonathan Gannon?

The Bills can run the ball, and Josh Allen is the best quarterback in the NFL at creating on broken plays. He's also capable of overcoming almost any number of mistakes in games, so this is a game where Jordan Love is going to have to put on his MVP cape.

As optimistic as this seems right now, I actually love how the Packers match up against the Bills with a QB like Love, a defense that causes takeaways, home-field advantage, and a coaching advantage.

Prediction: Packers win 34-29 (9-4)

Week 15: Packers vs. Dolphins

Sunday, December 20, Noon CT

After this next stretch of three games, the Packers will host old friend Jeff Hafley and the rebuilding Miami Dolphins.

Even though Hafley will be coming into this matchup with great knowledge of how Matt LaFleur and the Packers operate, there is simply too much of a talent gap between these two teams.

Right now, the Dolphins have Kyle McCord running the show at quarterback, and even picking up first downs has been hard for that offense. This isn't a game where you really need to overthink much. The Packers win this one easily, maybe even rest some starters if they are able to execute early on.

Prediction: Packers win 35-13 (10-4)

Week 16: @ Chicago Bears

Friday, December 25, Noon p.m. CT (Netflix)

Maybe one of the keys in the Dolphins game is not overlooking them, because the Packers have a short week before facing off against the Chicago Bears on Christmas Day.

Unfortunately, this game is being played on the road. The Bears have been one of the NFL's best offenses this season, even though they've hit a couple of bumps in the road over their last two games.

It's going to be fascinating to see where Chicago is at heading into this game, because depending on how the next four weeks go for them, this game could realistically determine the NFC North. That might seem far-fetched with both the Bears and Lions having just two losses heading into Thanksgiving, as well as the Vikings having just three losses, but you never know.

Either way, the Bears do a couple of things well that are hard to deal with on the road. They run the ball extremely well, and just like the Packers, they create takeaways.

Before hosting the Packers, the Bears play the Lions, Jaguars, Dolphins, and Bills. They are running a gauntlet.

The Packers absolutely can go on the road and beat Chicago, but the Bears have a disruptive defensive front and might be able to make Green Bay one-dimensional. They can also control time of possession.

Prediction: Bears win 24-20 (10-5)

If the Packers can find a way to go 4-1 in their next stretch of five games, it's going to set them up to potentially host a playoff game. The margin for error is slim with four losses already this season, but this team is good enough to go on a huge run with Parsons returning and Love looking like an MVP for most of this season.
