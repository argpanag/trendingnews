Joshua Van vs. Alexandre Pantoja 2: The Numbers Behind a Must-See UFC Rematch | Deadspin.com

TheTools · 18.09.2026 21:30 · source · permalink

Saturday's main event at Crypto.com Arena settles the argument that a freak injury started nine months ago, Joshua Van defends his flyweight title against Alexandre Pantoja in a rematch after their first fight lasted just 26 seconds, and the statistics behind this five-round championship rematch explain exactly why it's must-see television.

The 26-Second Number Everyone Remembers

Picture the entire first fight compressed into less time than it takes to read this paragraph: Van caught a Pantoja head kick, threw him to the canvas, and the former champion suffered an elbow injury on the landing that ended the bout before it truly began. That 26-second finish made Van, at 24, the second-youngest champion in UFC history, but it also left one of the sport's most decorated flyweights with real unfinished business.

Where Van's Volume Jumps Off the Page

Van's statistical profile is genuinely rare for the division. He lands 8.20 to 8.43 significant strikes per minute, nearly double the output of most flyweight contenders, at 55-57% accuracy. His three-round war with Brandon Royval set the UFC record for most combined significant strikes in a single fight at 419, and he became the youngest fighter in promotion history to eclipse 1,000 significant strikes landed. Visualize that pace over five rounds: Van's average fight time already sits at 13:15-13:37, meaning he's comfortable pushing deep into championship territory while still throwing at a suffocating rate.

Pantoja's Numbers Tell a Story

Pantoja's game is built differently. His 30-6 record includes 12 submission wins against just 8 by knockout, and he averages 2.20 takedowns per 15 minutes with a 0.97 submission attempt rate, numbers that reflect a fighter who wants to drag opponents into deep water. But the defensive gap is stark: Pantoja's takedown defense sits at 67%, and his significant striking defense is just 51%, both notably below Van's marks of 76-81% and 58-59%, respectively. That's the number that should worry Pantoja's corner most—against a fighter landing at Van's volume, a sub-60% striking defense could get exploited over 25 minutes rather than 26 seconds.

The Full Card's Firepower

The five-fight main card carries real stakes beyond the title fight. Arman Tsarukyan returns against Mauricio Ruffy in a lightweight five-rounder that could position the winner for a title shot, while Renato Moicano runs it back with Brian Ortega in a rematch of their own. Patricio Pitbull faces Doo Ho Choi at featherweight, and Alonzo Menifield takes on unbeaten prospect Iwo Baraniewski (9-0-0) at light heavyweight, rounding out a slate that leans heavily on rematches and unbeaten records.

What the Numbers Suggest for Round Two

With Van's output nearly double Pantoja's per-minute striking numbers and a takedown-defense gap of nearly 10 points in the champion's favor, the rematch numbers suggest Pantoja needs to change levels early and often rather than let this fight play out standing, because if it turns into another five-round track meet, Van's volume and durability numbers heavily favor a real title defense this time.
