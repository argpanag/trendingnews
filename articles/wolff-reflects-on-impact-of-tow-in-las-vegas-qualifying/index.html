Wolff reflects on impact of tow in Las Vegas Qualifying

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:58 · source · permalink

Toto Wolff reflected on the impact of the slipstream during Qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, after Mercedes topped the timesheets with Kimi Antonelli.

Toto Wolff has explained how Mercedes learned about the effectiveness of the slipstream during Qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with the Silver Arrows Team Principal believing George Russell had been ideally positioned to secure pole.

Mercedes enjoyed a strong session as Kimi Antonelli topped the timesheets ahead of Max Verstappen and Russell, but a five-place grid penalty for the Italian due to power unit changes means he will start sixth, promoting his team mate to the front row alongside the Dutchman.

The final stages of Qualifying saw drivers jostling for position to benefit from a tow along the circuit's lengthy straights, something Wolff explained had caught Mercedes by surprise.

"I've had 35 years in motorsport," Wolff told F1 TV. "I've never seen tows working when you're one and a half seconds behind each other like a train, because normally you should be penalised through the corners so much that the straights never compensate for it.

"But for whatever reason, here it seems to work. We found out later than some of the other teams, and I have no idea how these lap times came together."

Antonelli made the decision himself to drop to the back of the queue ahead of his final flying lap, with Mercedes initially concerned he wouldn't reach the line with enough time to start.

"We thought he wouldn't cross the line in time," Wolff explained. "But the truth is, even if he had, he would have been much too close to the car ahead of him, and so that lap for me was gone. And then, for whatever miracle reason, it was fast."

Speaking separately to Sky Sports F1, Wolff explained that he had expected Russell to be in contention for pole, although Mercedes still needed to investigate where the Briton had lost time.

"George, for me, had the perfect position. He was four seconds behind the car in front," Wolff said.

"We need to find out because I think there was something in Turns 7, 8 and 9, whether that was a mistake or something else happened. But it looked like the ideal position for a pole lap because he was quickest all the time."

Looking ahead to Russell starting alongside Verstappen, Wolff added: "Big cinema if they try to go through the first corners together. We shall be watching."
