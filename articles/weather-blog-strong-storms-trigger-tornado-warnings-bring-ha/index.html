Weather blog: Strong storms trigger tornado warnings, bring hail to NYC area

TheTools · 14.11.2026 09:56 · source · permalink

Early reports indicated vehicles had been stranded in floodwaters in New Jersey, while residents in Queens were bracing for a possible twister.

What to Know

  • Torrential rain and strong winds are pummeling the tri-state area, threatening flash floods, tornadoes, and hail.
  • Vehicles were said to be trapped in high waters in parts of NJ by early afternoon; in Queens, a tornado warning was in effect until 2 p.m. Watches were issued elsewhere.
  • The heaviest storms are expected to hit between about 1 p.m. and 7 p.m.,; they'll come from South Jersey and move northeast towards the city, Long Island and Connecticut.
  • Conditions should become less favorable for stronger storms by the late afternoon and evening hours; the overall threat remains low.

A developing storm system is bringing threats of severe weather, periods of heavy rain, and even accumulating snow to parts of the tri-state before a much colder air mass settles in.

Severe weather is expected throughout the afternoon Thursday, with flash floods, large hail, and tornadoes considered the most likely threats. Check severe weather alerts here.

Track the latest developments in our live blog below.

The weekend improves, but cold sticks around

Ready for some good news? Once the storm system pulls away, high pressure will build back in, bringing sunshine back for the weekend and into early next week.

However, don’t expect temperatures to bounce back right away. Highs this weekend will struggle to get out of the 40s, with overnight and early-morning temperatures falling into the 20s and 30s. Add in the chilly breezes, and it will feel even colder at times. The gradual warm-up won’t really get underway until the middle of next week, with temperatures potentially approaching 60 degrees toward the end of the week.

Friday's bigger story: a much colder blast of air

Perhaps the biggest change we’ll notice Friday will be in the temperatures.

As a much colder air mass settles in behind the departing storm, temperatures and wind chills will tumble. In fact, wind chills are expected to dip into the 30s Friday morning and remain in the 30s for much of the day across most of the region.

Even though no snow is expected for the immediate NYC area, it may still very much feel like a winter day.

After the milder conditions we’ve been experiencing, the change will be noticeable. This is the kind of day when a regular jacket may not be enough. You’ll want that extra-heavy winter coat, especially during the morning commute and any extended time outdoors.

So what comes next? The cold (and snow for some)

As that colder air filters into the region tonight, temperatures will drop enough to allow some of the rain to change over to wet snow, primarily across areas well north and west of New York City.

The higher elevations of the Hudson Valley are of particular concern, where accumulating snow is possible. Ulster County is under a winter weather advisory tonight into Friday morning, with up to 3 inches of snow possible in the higher elevations.

By Friday morning’s commute, snow showers could spread across other parts of the Hudson Valley and northern New Jersey. While most communities are not expecting significant accumulation, even a light coating could create locally slick roadways and reduced visibility, particularly in the higher elevations.

By midday, the precipitation will begin to exit from west to east, with just the chance of some scattered light showers or wet flurries lingering into early Saturday morning.

Tornado watches canceled for NJ

After canceling the tornado watch for Philadelphia and parts of New Jersey (including Hunterdon, Mercer, Middlesex and Somerset counties) around 5 p.m., the National Weather Service canceled the tornado watch for Monmouth and Ocean counties as well around 6 p.m.
