FIRST ALERT FORECAST: Here Comes A Cold Front

TheTools · 05.11.2026 23:08 · source · permalink

HAZARD, Ky. (WYMT) - After a mild and cloudy Thursday, we’re tracking a significant cold front that’ll sweep through the region Friday. This system isn’t going to bring any rain to speak of—just wind and colder air—but it’s the temperature drop that’s going to be the headline as we head into the weekend.

Tonight Into Friday Morning: Watch for Fog

Here’s what we’re dealing with this evening and overnight: fog is likely to develop, especially in the deeper valleys and low-lying areas. Some of that fog could be dense at times, so if you’re out early Friday morning for your commute, take it easy on the roads. Visibility could be reduced in spots. Temperatures will dip to the upper 30s to low 40s overnight, but by Friday afternoon as the cold front moves through, we’ll see increasing clouds and a few light showers or sprinkles are possible—mainly in the northwest portions of our area. Again, nothing significant for rainfall, but it’s the wind and the sharp temperature drop that we need to pay attention to.

The Big Story: Cold This Weekend

Here’s where it gets interesting. Behind this cold front, we’re going to see a dramatic shift in our airmass. Friday afternoon highs will struggle to reach the low to mid 60s, then by Saturday, we’re only looking at highs in the low to mid 50s. Sunday looks similar—hovering near 60 degrees at best. Combined with clear skies Saturday and Sunday nights, we’re setting ourselves up for cold conditions overnight.

This is where the freeze potential comes in. Saturday night into Sunday morning, lows are expected to drop into the mid to upper 30s for most areas, but in the deeper valleys and sheltered low-lying areas, we could see temperatures dip into the 20s. That means a widespread frost is likely both Saturday and Sunday nights, and a freeze is also possible, especially for our valley communities. If you’ve got any sensitive plants or outdoor water sources, you’ll want to take precautions.

Next Week: Back to Normal

The good news? High pressure builds in by early next week, which means we’ll stay dry and temperatures will moderate back closer to what’s normal for this time of year. We’re looking at mostly sunny skies and highs climbing back into the mid-60s by midweek.

There is some potential for more active weather to develop later next week, but there’s still some model uncertainty on the exact timing and details. We’ll keep you posted as we get closer.
