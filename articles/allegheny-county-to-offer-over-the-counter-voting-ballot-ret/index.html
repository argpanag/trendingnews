Allegheny County to offer over-the-counter voting, ballot return sites in lead-up to Election Day

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:57 · source · permalink

Amid rising confusion and concern about the conduct of mail-in balloting this election, Allegheny County will offer two in-person mail-in ballot return options this fall.

As the Trump administration seeks to overhaul the vote-by-mail system just weeks before the election, “We are still business as usual,” county director of administrative services Jessica Garofolo said at a Board of Elections meeting Friday.

Garafolo oversees the county’s elections division. “All methods that we previously had for the last six years are still in play.”

In Allegheny County, those options include the use of satellite voting locations — where voters can request, complete and return a mail-in ballot all in one visit — and drop-off locations, where people who have received and completed their ballots can hand them directly to county staff.

Both facilities allow citizens to have their votes counted without waiting to vote in person on Election Day, and without involving the post office in ballot return. And board members voted unanimously Friday to deploy five satellite centers and 12 drop-off sites in the lead up to the November election.

That’s a larger network than the county deployed in last year’s lower-turnout municipal elections, for which the county operated four satellite sites and 10 ballot return locations.

“I want to prepare for the worst, especially because of all the mixed messaging that’s happening in the media about, ‘Can I vote by mail? Will my vote be counted?’” said elections board and County Council member Bethany Hallam.

While election officials maintain that voting by mail will remain secure, there’s some evidence that voter trepidation may be having an effect: Applications for mail-in ballots have “slowed down significantly,” according to county Elections Manager David Voye.

The county has so far received around 120,000 applications — nearly 30,000 fewer than expected, though Voye noted “we still have time to bump those numbers up,” with the deadline for requesting a ballot still weeks away. Officials expect to begin mailing ballots to voters in the first week of October.

Voters can apply for and immediately cast their ballot at Soldiers and Sailors Memorial Hall from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Wednesday, Oct. 14 and Thursday, Oct. 15:

  • Soldiers and Sailors Memorial Hall – Auditorium; 4141 Fifth Avenue, Pittsburgh 15213

The following satellite locations will be open from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Saturday, Oct. 17, Sunday, Oct. 18, Saturday, Oct. 24 and Sunday, Oct. 25:

  • Community College of Allegheny County - Homewood; 701 N. Homewood Avenue, Pittsburgh 15208
  • North Park Ice Rink; 1200 Pearce Mill Road, Wexford 15090
  • South Park Ice Rink; 30 Corrigan Drive, Bethel Park 15102
  • County Office Building; 542 Forbes Avenue, Pittsburgh 15219

The hours and availability have been expanded since the spring primary in anticipation of greater demand this fall.

Voters who have already received a mail-in ballot can return it to one of 12 staffed drop-off locations around the county from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Friday, Oct. 30 and Monday, Nov. 2 or from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturday, Oct. 31 and Sunday, Nov. 1.

Nine of those locations have already been identified:

  • 911 Center; 150 Hookstown Grade Rd, Moon Twp 15108
  • Boyce Park Four Seasons Lodge; 901 Centerview Dr, Plum 15239
  • Carnegie Public Library; 5801 Forbes Ave, Pittsburgh 15217
  • North Park Ice Rink; 1200 Pearce Mill Rd, Wexford 15090
  • South Park Ice Rink; 30 Corrigan Dr, Bethel Park 15102
  • Dormont Pool; 1801 Dormont Ave., Pittsburgh, PA 15216
  • CCAC Homewood; 701 N. Homewood Ave., Pittsburgh, PA 15208
  • Greater Pittsburgh Food Bank; 1 N. Linden St., Duquesne, PA 15110
  • Avalon Public Library, 317 S. Home Avenue, Pittsburgh, PA 15202

The locations of three additional three drop-off centers, which the board voted to add on Friday, have not yet been determined.

In addition to those temporary locations, the offices of the county elections department also provide over-the-counter voting services as well as mail-in ballot collection in the lobby of the County Office Building. Voters can use those facilities during regular business hours, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Monday through Friday.

Voters must complete their own ballots, or fill out an authorization form for someone else to turn in a ballot if they are physically unable to do so.

Nearly two dozen people spoke Friday in support of additional satellite and ballot return locations, with more voicing their support in comments submitted online.

“Given voters' growing use of mail-in ballots, options to return those ballots should grow as well,” said Brett Scruton, an organizer with Common Cause Pennsylvania. “If the number of satellite election offices remains static, we are not realistically serving the needs of Allegheny County voters based on the data that we have.”

Satellite and drop-off locations have been used on and off since 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic scuttled typical voting procedures. County Executive Sara Innamorato, who chairs the Board of Elections, revived the practice in 2024 as part of an effort to expand voter access.

Though elections officials stressed that voting by mail remains a secure way to vote and said they believe the courts will block the Trump administration’s efforts to overhaul the system, officials are also preparing for what Hallam described as the “worst-case scenario.”

The debate over Trump’s proposed overhaul of mail-in voting,which would allow the post office to reject ballot mail it couldn’t match to a list of voters, is locked up in the federal courts. Voye said that if Trump’s changes went through, the county would need to reprint all the mail ballot envelopes to add an additional barcode. They’d also have to send a list of people eligible to vote by mail to the Postal Service. Still, he pledged, “We would still hold an election and we would still have mail-in ballots.”

Oct. 19. is the last day to register to vote before the election. The deadline to apply for a mail-in or absentee ballot is Oct. 27. Ballots must be received by 8 p.m. on Election Day, Nov. 3.
