Dozens more students come forward with concerns over qualifications from Portobello Institute

TheTools · 15.11.2026 20:00 · source · permalink

Since the 'Irish Examiner' first reported on the registration issues, recent students of Portobello Institute have continued to highlight their grievances. File picture

More than two dozen students who attended Portobello Institute have come forward to highlight concerns about the registration of their qualifications after spending thousands of euros on academic courses.

Since the Irish Examiner first reported on the registration issues, recent students of Portobello Institute have continued to highlight their grievances.

While the majority are students who enrolled on PE courses with the private college in recent years, the Irish Examiner has also heard from several students who took multiple courses with Portobello, spending upwards of €12,000, and as much as €18,000 in some cases, but have not been able to fully register as PE teachers with the Teaching Council as they expected.

As well as the graduate diploma in PE, there have also been concerns raised about a three-year BA in physical education, an MSc Physical Education, and the Post Graduate Certificate in Education International.

One student applied for a temporary Teaching Council number in 2022 while undertaking a PE course with the college.

In early 2023, off the back of covid and against the backdrop of teacher supply issues, the Teaching Council announced that, on a time-bound basis, it would accept applications to the register from teachers who qualified outside Ireland, but who had not completed their induction where they had qualified.

Instead, teachers were able to complete their induction period, called ‘Droichead’, in Ireland.

The student said: “At that time, Portobello were advertising another course called a Post Graduate Certificate in Education International (PGCEi). This was all online, and it was a Level 9.”

The course was awarded by Liverpool John Moores University, and led to a UK award, but the course was provided in Dublin.

This student, and others, believed both the PE course and the post grad together would allow them to apply to the Teaching Council and register successfully as PE teachers under this time-bound provision without having to complete an induction period abroad.

“We’re all annoyed that we never got anything in writing from them. It was always a phone call, that somebody indicated ‘yeah, there’s a fair chance this is going to work’.”

Prior to enrolling with Portobello, the student contacted the Teaching Council, but believes they did not get a straight answer about the status of courses offered by Portobello.

“If the Teaching Council had told me ‘You have to complete a PME, here’s one in Limerick, here’s one in Dublin, they are both full-time’, if I got told that from the get-go, I wouldn’t have gone down the route at all. I didn’t have the finances to do it.”

The course in Portobello “made more financial sense,” the student said, “because it meant I could work full-time, while paying for the course”.

They first learned of registration issues from other students.

“Some others were qualified teachers already, and they were applying to the Teaching Council to get PE added to their register, and it was coming back that the Teaching Council was not satisfied in terms of the credits, the modules, and the work that was done.

“I was being told everything is being gauged on a case-by-case basis, which led you to believe there was some wiggle room.”

A screenshot of the PGCEi course, provided by Portobello, from September 2023, described it as an “academic qualification, with a philosophy informed by the Teaching Council of Ireland and the Teachers’ Standards (DfE, UK)”.

In a timeline, the college provided in response to queries, Portobello said it became aware of issues with registration in April 2024, as it was notified by graduates. Between February and May 2025, it also “repeatedly requested a meeting” with the Teaching Council.

A second student, who also spent €12,000 on courses with Portobello, including the PGCE, has been working as a teacher for five years.

However, the student has been unable to register, as expected, with the Teaching Council. As a result, they have not been able to progress in their career.

The student could apply to the register as a lecturer due to having a Masters degree.

“I could lecture future PE teachers with no issues for the Teaching Council, but I can’t teach PE myself as a qualified teacher.”

How can a resolution be reached?

“I think it has to be some form of a collaboration between the Teaching Council and Portobello or whatever university is happy to cover it.

“I’m looking at how to get a mortgage. I only have academic term income. I would love to come out of this with a teaching number in PE.

“Whether it includes doing another course or not, as long as it has a guarantee from the Teaching Council, and Portobello is happy to cover the cost. I am not putting any more money in. I don’t have that money spare.

“It honestly broke me a little bit.”

A third student who also completed the PGCE at Portobello applied to the Teaching Council for route 2 registration five times.

“I’ve been rejected every time. We’ve been told by Portobello that we don’t have shortfalls. They have given us documents stating that. I was uploading those documents as evidence. The Teaching Council would just come back and reject it again.”

The student said he asked the council if he could address the shortfalls it identified in Ireland, but was told no.

“I can’t wrap my head around them not letting us do the shortfalls in Ireland. If I could do the shortfalls in UL, Hibernia, anywhere in Ireland, I’d do it in a heartbeat.

“They want us to go teach in England for two years. I’m into my sixth year now of teaching.”

The student “absolutely loves” their current school but is still stuck on a lower pay scale.

“In the eyes of the Department of Education, I’m not qualified.

“I can’t get paid for the holidays. I’m on substitute hourly pay, but teaching my classes, organising matches and trips, working with our ASD classes, I’m a nurture room teacher and I’m just getting paid hourly now.

“Because it’s my third year in this school, if I don’t have it sorted before May, I’m not allowed to teach in that school again next year, which would be devastating.”

A spokesperson for the Teaching Council said it has a statutory responsibility to uphold professional standards and to ensure that all applicants meet the published curricular subject requirements for registration.

“Registration requirements apply equally and fairly to all applicants and cannot be waived.”

The Irish Examiner asked Portobello Institute if it keeps records of phone calls between its education advisors and potential students.

In response, it said: “Yes. Portobello Institute records telephone calls between its education advisers and prospective students. These recordings are supported by established quality control procedures, documented operating practices, and ongoing training and assessment of advisers, drawing on both internal and external expertise.

“All recording and related quality assurance processes are conducted in accordance with applicable GDPR requirements.”

In a general statement regarding registration issues, the college said: “Central to this is how Ireland’s teacher registration framework accommodates graduates who pursue non-traditional educational pathways, compared with those who follow more traditional routes.

“There is an important distinction between maintaining rigorous professional standards, which nobody disputes, and ensuring that graduates who acquire academically validated qualifications meet registration requirements.”

  • Jess Casey is education correspondent
