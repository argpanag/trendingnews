Delhi Hosts BRICS Leaders Amid Improved Air Quality as AQI Drops to 62 - The CSR Journal

TheTools · 12.09.2026 11:59 · source · permalink

Delhi is hosting significant global leaders this weekend in connection with the BRICS Summit, and the capital is experiencing a notable improvement in air quality. On September 12 at approximately 4 pm, the Air Quality Index (AQI) in Delhi was recorded at 62, placing it in the moderate range according to AQI.in, a platform that monitors air quality and weather conditions.This positive development coincides with the arrival of prominent figures including Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin for the two-day summit. This drop in pollution is particularly remarkable, considering that Delhi had faced higher AQI levels earlier in the week, peaking at 133 on September 10 and even reaching 155 on the morning of September 11.The decrease in air pollution levels in Delhi has been attributed primarily to recent weather conditions. The city has witnessed rain and overcast skies in the days leading up to the summit, influenced by an active monsoon system affecting the region. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) had predicted generally cloudy conditions accompanied by moderate rainfall for September 12, with expectations of the wet weather continuing in the following days.Rain has a temporary effect on air quality by helping to clear suspended particles from the atmosphere. It can effectively pull down dust and pollutants as raindrops descend through the air. Additionally, changes in wind patterns and atmospheric conditions contribute to the dispersion of harmful substances, preventing their accumulation close to the ground. This dispersion is crucial for a city like Delhi, where continuous traffic, construction activities, and road dust significantly contribute to air pollution.Currently, this weather pattern aligns with a broader monsoon system driven by a low-pressure area located over the northwest and west-central Bay of Bengal. The impact of this system is being felt across parts of northern and central India, including the National Capital Territory, providing some respite from pollution during the high-profile summit.While the current air quality improvement is commendable, it should not be perceived as a permanent resolution to Delhi’s persistent pollution issues. Rain can temporarily reset the city’s air quality, but there is a high probability of pollution levels escalating again when conditions dry out, winds weaken, and air pollutants begin to accumulate once more.For the moment, however, the timing of the improved air quality is fortuitous. As Delhi welcomes participants to the 18th BRICS Summit on September 12 and 13, the capital experiences a short reprieve from the air pollution that has long characterised its environment. While the summit primarily focuses on matters of global diplomacy, trade, and geopolitical relations, participants and world leaders will benefit from a surprising bonus in the form of cleaner air during their engagements in the city.This temporary respite from pollution is particularly beneficial for both the attendees and the citizens of Delhi, who regularly contend with significant air quality challenges.
