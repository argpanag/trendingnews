Jody Scheckter, former F1 world champion, loses £1.5m buffalo farm tax case

TheTools · 15.09.2026 21:58 · source · permalink

HMRC demanding repayment from farm housing Britain’s largest herd of water buffalo, as annual financial losses disqualify it from tax relief

Jody Scheckter, the 1979 Formula One world champion, has lost a £1.5m court battle against HM Revenue and Customs connected to his organic buffalo farming business.

Scheckter, 76, was one of the biggest stars of Formula One in the 1970s. The South African won 10 grands prix and claimed 33 podium positions between 1972 and 1980, driving for McLaren, Tyrrell, Walter Wolf Racing and Ferrari, where he eventually won his title in 1979. He was the last driver to win a drivers’ championship for Ferrari until Michael Schumacher 21 years later in 2000.

After retiring from F1, Scheckter relocated to Atlanta, Georgia, making millions when he sold his firearms training business in America in the 1990s.

After moving to the UK with his wife, Clare, he ploughed an estimated £55m of that into the 2,500-acre Laverstoke Park, an organic and biodynamic farm in Overton, Hampshire.

The farm had Britain’s largest herd of water buffalo, supplying organic meat to Ocado and specialising in buffalo mozzarella. The farm, which also raised cattle, geese, turkeys, chickens, boar and pigs, and produced organic ale, was home to the CarFest festival for more than 10 years.

Following years of losses, Scheckter wound down the dairy and buffalo farming operations in 2024, citing “increased energy costs, a reduction in milk volumes, and the additional capital investment required for future-proofing”. Scheckter has since relocated to northern Italy.

The case with HMRC concerned a backdated demand for £1,457,634 after an inspector ruled that Scheckter’s farming partnership had wrongly offset losses against profits from other parts of his business between 2008 and 2010.

HMRC declared the arrangement invalid in 2019 and issued the demand for repayment. The relief is available to farmers, but only where the enterprise becomes profitable within five years of being established. The Laverstoke farming partnership registered a loss in almost every year since it was founded, meaning it failed to satisfy that requirement.

Dismissing his appeal against paying the tax bill, Judge Tony Beare shared his “sadness” at the failure of Scheckter’s buffalo passion project.

He said: “It is impossible to have been involved in this case without feeling considerable sadness.

“[Sadness] at how a project which was conceived with such imagination and originality by the appellant on which he expended so much financial capital and personal energy has turned out.

“He was…extremely passionate about the positive effects which healthier eating would engender for everyone and wanted the business to be a model for organic farming and healthier eating across the world.

“We would be remiss if we did not conclude this decision by acknowledging that and expressing our profound regret for the way that things developed.

“Regret which is compounded by the conclusions which we have felt compelled to reach in the present proceedings.”

Scheckter remains a member of Laverstoke Park Produce, and a director of both Laverstoke Park Mill Ltd and Laverstone Park Farm Ltd.
