THE BREAKDOWN: Drought Outlook Fall 2026

TheTools · 15.09.2026 19:15 · source · permalink

MEMPHIS, Tenn. (WMC) - As El Niño nears very strong “super” status this fall, we are likely to see changes in drought conditions across the United States.

From September through November 2026, wetter than average conditions are expected to be focused on parts of the Rockies and Southeast, with drier than average conditions in the far northern tier of the country.

This is why much needed drought improvement is expected to begin for most of the Rockies and Southwest this fall. A stretch from Washington to the Northern Plains is likely to see drought develop or get worse.

Most of the east coast will see drought improve this fall while parts of Maine and New Hampshire see additional drought development.

Middle parts of the country, including us here in the Mid-South, are likely to see drought get worse before it gets better, so make sure to appreciate any upcoming rain events as they come.

By winter, a wetter than average pattern solidified in the southern tier of the United States while drier conditions materialized farther north.

Here in Memphis, we are in between these two areas, so stay tuned for updates as we breakdown local winter El Niño impacts.
