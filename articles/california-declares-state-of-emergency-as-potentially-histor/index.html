California declared a state of emergency Monday to begin mobilizing state resources ahead of an anticipated historic El Niño storm season this winter.

The action also directs state agencies to lead the state’s flood-risk reduction efforts, stage flood-fighting supplies such as sandbags and pumps and pre-position other critical equipment.

The California Department of Transportation, known as Caltrans, will also stage traffic-control, road-closure and snow-removal equipment along highways most at risk.

In the San Francisco Bay Area a Coastal Flood Advisory was issued for high astronomical tides potentially causing  flooding for flood-prone, low-lying areas around the bay.

In addition, the proclamation prepares the California National Guard to support flood response, search and rescue, engineering and logistics missions. 

It also gives state environmental and natural resource agencies additional resources to support local efforts to prepare for flooding, landslides and debris flows by fast-tracking permits and identifying other ways to cut red tape.

WHAT IS COASTAL EROSION AND WHY IS IT DANGEROUS?

El Niño is a natural climate pattern in which trade winds are typically weaker, allowing warm water to build up in parts of the eastern and central Pacific, per the FOX Forecast Center.

This can lead to wetter and more intense winters in California.

In an aerial view, a Pacific Ocean wave crashes into a seawall protecting beachside homes on September 7, 2026 in Long Beach, California. According to the press release, federal forecasters said El Niño is strengthening, with a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event this fall and winter and a 75% chance it could reach historic strength.

This means much of California could experience repeated rounds of heavy rain, strong winds, mountain snow, flooding, landslides, debris flows, large waves and coastal flooding.
