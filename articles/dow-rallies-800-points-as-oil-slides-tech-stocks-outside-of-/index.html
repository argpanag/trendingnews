Dow rallies 800 points as oil slides, tech stocks outside of Nvidia gain: Live updates

TheTools · 08.11.2026 23:15 · source · permalink

Stocks surged on Monday, boosted by a decline in oil prices, as traders kept a close eye on technology shares after last week's volatility.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average popped 819 points, or 1.6%, supported by Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar. The S&P 500 rose 1.8%, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.1%.

U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures dropped 3% to around $87 per barrel. International benchmark Brent slid 4% to about $94 per barrel.

Friday's market partially rebounded from Thursday's brutal AI-driven sell-off, as chip stocks recovered on renewed buying. However, concerns linger in the wake of the Democrats' strong midterm results, with investors fearing that tighter regulations on data center construction, energy use, and development costs could slow the AI infrastructure boom.

The tech sector also focused on the bombshell announcement Sunday that Nvidia's board of directors is opening an investigation into co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a businesswoman who U.S. intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares were last down 6%.

Other chip stocks advanced, however, with Broadcom and Advanced Micro Devices each popping 5%. On Semiconductor also rose 3%. Additionally, other "Magnificent Seven" names were higher contrary to Nvidia. Amazon gained 5%, while Tesla and Alphabet climbed more than 3%. Microsoft added 2%.

Liz Ann Sonders, Schwab Center for Financial Research chief investment strategist, noted that the Nasdaq and Nasdaq-100 are oversold, with heavy de-risking by traders. Thursday marked the biggest day of retail selling since the Covid pandemic, according to Vanda Research. Retail traders haven't been taking advantage of the selloff, Sonders said.

"Retail has not proven to be the dip buyers that they have been in the past," she told CNBC's "Fast Money" on Friday. "You saw a really heavy amount of net selling … we may not fully be washed out yet, but we'll have to see whether that retail trader at some point decides to step back in."

Sonders suggested that the next short-term focus should be on companies' adjustments to capex plans and expectations for future profit margins.

This week, investors are looking ahead to key AI-related earnings — CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials are all on deck — as they assess the outlook for tech and whether the recent selloff has further to run.

They'll also watch for the releases of both the consumer price index on Tuesday and the producer price index on Friday, looking for clues on the Fed's next move.

Citi is flagging the rotational turbulence beneath the index-level calm that equity markets have seen in recent days, bracing for more potential downside in the short term. The bank tactically downgraded "the US, Global Tech, and European Tech to Neutral (from Overweight)" in a Monday note. 

"While fundamentals look attractive, positioning remains relatively neutral and could therefore adjust further into net short territory," Beata Manthey at Citi said. "We remain constructive on AI over the medium-term but would look for cleaner positioning and more stable AI sentiment (along with steady EPS revisions) before reestablishing our Overweight call."

— Tobias Burns

"We see AMD emerging as a durable supplier of AI compute, with multi-gen customer wins and Helios rack-scale solutions supporting a materially larger [data center] business."

"Annual Instinct product cadence and [AMD's open software stack] progress strengthen AMD's competitive positioning, while compute-heavy agentic AI drives incremental [graphics processing units] and … [central processing unit] demand," Schafer said.

European stocks got off to a mixed start on Monday, with the regional Stoxx 600 index opening lower before moving to a 0.4% gain by 8:20 a.m. in London.

Mining stocks popped 1.7% as gold and copper prices bounced back from last week's downturn.

Italian bank Unicredit was the top performer, up 4.7% after its third-quarter net profit rose by a record 24% year-on-year and as it prepares to take control of Germany's Commerzbank.

Luxury shares suffered, with LVMH, Moncler, Hermes, Kering, Burberry, Richemont and Christian Dior all trading slightly lower, as investors remained in risk-off mode and data showed mounting domestic inflation pressures in major market China.

Construction firm Hochtief fell 5.3% after Spanish group ACS said it had not considered a tender offer for shares in the company, which is its German subsidiary. Bloomberg had reported ACS was considering buying the remaining Hochtief shares it does not own or a potential merger of the firms.

Crédit Agricole has proposed merging its Italian operation with Banco BPM in a deal that would create Italy's third-largest banking group.

The French lender said in a statement Monday that the "friendly proposal" to engage its Italian peer in merger discussions is a "natural extension" of the long-term partnership between the pair.

Under the deal, Crédit Agricole would increase its current 29.6% stake in Banco BPM to approximately 45% of the new combined entity.

The proposal offers Banco BPM shareholders a 20% premium to the relative valuation of the businesses, based on estimated 2027 price-to-earnings multiples, and a 1 billion euro ($1.12 billion) special dividend, according to Crédit Agricole's announcement.

The Paris-headquartered bank said the deal — which has been submitted to Banco BPM's board of directors for evaluation — could deliver 800 million in annual synergies, with about 24% 2027 earnings-per-share accretion expected.

"This strategic project would create Italy's third-largest banking group, serving 6.5 million customers through more than 2,300 branches, by combining a strong local presence, financial strength, and business expertise," Crédit Agricole added.

— Hugh Leask

European bourses are heading for a weak open, according to IG futures data. France's CAC 40, Germany's DAX and Italy's FTSE MIB were all seen slipping, though the U.K.'s FTSE 100 was last seen slightly higher.

It follows last week's post-midterms markets rollercoaster, with strong gains on Nov. 4 followed by a selloff on Nov. 5 and 6. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index ended the week 0.24% higher.

— Jenni Reid

UniCredit's third-quarter net profit rose by a record 24% year-on-year to 3.3 billion euros ($3.68 billion), beating consensus estimates of 2.78 billion euros, as revenues reached 6.9 billion euros, up 12% year-on-year.

The Italian bank, which is set to take control of German lender Commerzbank, also hiked its full-year profit guidance to full-year profit guidance to "well above" 11.5 billion euros, from more than 11 billion euros.

Net interest income was up 5% over the quarter, and 8% year-on-year, while common equity tier (CET) 1 capital ratio — a measure of a bank's solvency — reached 15% during the period, up from 14.3% the previous quarter.

"UniCredit has delivered its best quarter ever, and the best in the sector, beating across all KPIs: highest net profit growth, best RoTE and cost/income ratio and organic capital generation supporting best-in- class shareholder returns while increasing capital," CEO Andrea Orcel said in a statement.

The Milan-headquartered lender said the Commerzbank acquisition would strengthen its pan-European footprint and boost earnings and shareholder distributions. It expects the remaining approvals to be greenlighted by European regulators later this month, with the deal's integration plan set to begin in the first quarter of 2027.

— Hugh Leask

Bitcoin fell below $74,000 over the weekend to its lowest level since August before clawing back some of the losses.

Ether, XRP, Hyperliquid and other cryptocurrencies also declined, according to pricing from Coingecko.

"Bitcoin's renewed weakness is a concern," said Matt Maley of Miller Tabak + Co., who flagged the the test of September levels and said "a break below $75k would give it a key lower-low."

However, he added that it would take a drop below the 200-day moving average around $73,000 "before it raised any serious warning flags."

— Joanna Ossinger

Producer prices in China grew at the fastest rate since June 2022, accelerating for the third month in a row.

The PPI for October came in at 5.7%, compared to the 5.3% expected by economists polled by Reuters. The increase was driven by a sharp rise in prices in the mining and raw materials industries.

Consumer inflation in China slowed to 0.7% in October, its first deceleration after two straight months of increases.

Core inflation, which strips out food and energy prices, came in at 0.8%, down from the 1% seen in the month before.

— Lim Hui Jie
