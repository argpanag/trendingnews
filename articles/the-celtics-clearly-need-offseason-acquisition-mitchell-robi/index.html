The Celtics clearly need offseason acquisition Mitchell Robinson to round his game into shape - The Boston Globe

TheTools · 07.11.2026 02:39 · source · permalink

What’s apparent is that Mitchell Robinson is taking his early-season struggles hard. After yet another listless performance in Houston, Robinson sat at his locker seemingly pondering why it’s been so difficult to adjust to his new team, to do the things he usually does, to stay in the game and be productive.

He was a minus-16 in his 15 minutes in the Celtics’ 109-93 loss to the Rockets on Monday. He sprained his left thumb and left the game briefly. He was not good on defense. His teammates are having major trouble throwing him his preferred alley-oops. He isn’t moving as well as expected. He isn’t scoring or nabbing offensive rebounds.

There’s a theory that Robinson reported to camp in less than optimal shape and has been playing himself back into condition, to the detriment of his production. There are also the limitations that were profound when he signed a three-year contract as a free agent: troubles with free throw shooting, lack of offensive arsenal, and a propensity for fouls.

But what the Celtics expected was a defensive difference maker, a player who can give his club additional possessions with his rebounds and as a rim protector. Robinson was so imposing two years ago when the Knicks faced the Celtics in the playoffs, Boston coach Joe Mazzulla fouled him intentionally in multiple games just to get him off the floor. The New York defense was considerably better with him on the court.

In an early sample size, the Celtics defense is markedly worse with Robinson on the floor. The Celtics have a plus-5.9 point differential in seven games this season — a number that sunk after the 16-point loss to Houston. The Celtics are a minus-7.0 when Robinson is on the floor. By comparison, the Celtics are plus-4.6 with Luka Garza on the floor and plus-8.1 with Neemias Queta in the game.

Robinson was a plus-2.6 last season with the Knicks and 1.7 during their championship playoff run. What’s more, Robinson averaged 7.7 offensive rebounds per 36 minutes last season, that number has dwindled to 3.4. Finally, his foul rate is the highest since his second NBA season, perhaps a byproduct of fatigue.

When asked if Robinson is still working himself into shape, Mazzulla didn’t reject that assertion.

“I mean, none of us have met the expectations, myself included,” Mazzulla said. “I have to be better, and it goes down the line. I like where Mitch is at from a mentality standpoint. I like his approach and his work ethic into doing the things that we’re asking to do. It’s obviously a little bit different, but none of us have met our own expectations, and we just have to keep working toward it. As long as we keep that mind-set, not be distracted by the result, we got a chance. He’s somebody that takes pride in that process of getting better, and, you know, I’ve enjoyed working with him.”

Robinson was visibly upset when he spoke to the Globe after a subpar opener against the Pistons. He was whisper quiet after speaking with the media briefly after the Rockets’ loss.

The plan for the Celtics is to encourage and empower Robinson. His returning to previous form could take time, and that means he’ll have to log minutes to improve and get into premium shape.

“I would say for his offensive rebounding impact, we have him a little bit further away from the rim, setting screens and playmaking for us on the perimeter as part of our offense,” Mazzulla said. “I think that has a little bit to do with it. But as far as the two-man game, it’s just something to work on.”

The Celtics are going to have to exercise patience. They’re going to need an effective Robinson to compete with the elite teams in the Eastern Conference. Garza has probably been the most impressive Celtic this season besides Paul George and deserves more minutes, especially since he’s proven to be a 3-point threat.

Queta has been strong defensively but also prone to mistakes and fouls, and still needs to polish his offensive game. The Celtics entered this season believing the center position could be a strength, but their three centers are so dramatically different that all three need to play well simultaneously for the club to thrive.

It’s uncertain whether Robinson was really prepared for the rigors of his first NBA season with added responsibility. He’s struggling with those assignments and the lack of conditioning isn’t helping. The Celtics’ next move is perhaps using Robinson in shorter stints, bringing him off the bench as he did in New York, and giving him opportunities to rebuild his confidence for the long term.

Robinson knows full well he’s not performing to the team’s expectations but benching him isn’t the solution. For now, there is a Robinson problem and it could take weeks to solve.
