Prince George is about to step into one of the most significant moments of his young life. But his aunt and uncle's return to Britain may be threatening to steal the spotlight. According to a royal commentator, Prince Harry and Meghan Markle's homecoming couldn't have come at a worse time. That's especially true for the Wales family.

Meghan Markle and Prince Harry threaten to overshadow Kate and William's 'steady, future-focused' brand, per expert

Expert warns Prince George's Eton debut could get buried under a Prince Harry and Meghan Markle 'feud' narrative. George reportedly plans to start at Eton College this month, following generations of royal men before him. Royal commentator Amanda Matta told Fox News Digital that this isn't just a typical first day of school. According to Matta, it marks the beginning of George's public path as heir to the throne, a narrative the Palace has spent years carefully building.

Matta explained that the Palace remains deeply protective of that image. She said any competing headline, especially one involving Harry and Meghan, risks pulling attention away from the "steady, future-focused" brand William and Kate have worked hard to establish for their family.

Her warning comes at a sensitive moment. Harry and Meghan reportedly relocated back to the UK last month with their children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet. That move ended six years spent in California. Their return has already generated near-constant coverage, from speculation about where they're living to renewed questions about their standing with King Charles.

According to Matta, the timing alone could turn George's Eton debut into an unwanted sideshow. She said it would reportedly be a shame if his first days at school got tangled up in a renewed feud narrative involving his uncle and aunt, rather than being remembered as a milestone in his own right.

For William and Kate, the stakes go beyond media coverage. The Wales family has spent years positioning itself as the monarchy's stable, forward-looking future, a deliberate contrast to the drama that's followed the Sussexes since 2020. Whether that carefully built image holds up may ultimately depend on how visible Harry and Meghan choose to be now that they're back on British soil.
