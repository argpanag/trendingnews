Anfield has become a problem for Liverpool - AOL

TheTools · 12.09.2026 16:53 · source · permalink

The game had meandered into stoppage time and Andoni Iraola got his first glimpse of a sight that had become prevalent last season.

Seats had emptied and supported flooded to the stairwells to make their way for home without looking over their shoulders. They had seen it all before from Liverpool, who huffed and puffed but never appeared capable of blowing up a balloon, let alone blowing Fulham’s house down.

This was the 12th Premier League contest Anfield has staged in 2026 and Liverpool, remarkably, have won only four, the last being against Crystal Palace in April. The stadium that was once a rich source of points has suddenly become a problem.

Not even their most staunch supporter could argue that they should have beaten Fulham here and the realists, of whom there are many, would recognise that on another day they would have finished without even a point on an afternoon that brought much relief for Alvaro Arbeloa.

The locals had turned up expecting one of those afternoons when Liverpool cut loose and win comfortably, but the grumbling at half-time gave a clear indication of how fitfully the hosts had played and how different everything had looked to Wednesday evening.

Advertisement

Really, Liverpool should have faced an uphill battle from the 11th minute. Alisson Becker inexplicably passed to Ryan Gravenberch on the edge of his penalty area, the Dutchman was mugged and Josh King gave Gonzalo Garcia a chance to silence the Kop, but Jeremy Jacquet cleared off the line.

What a lift that would have given Arbeloa, aware that he is already under scrutiny, but he would have taken heart from the organisation and determination his team showed when they were out of possession, barely giving Liverpool a clear sight of goal.

Alexander Isak whistled a drive across the six-yard box, Florian Wirtz crashed a shot into the side netting and Victor Muñoz had a header tipped on to the bar, but that was it from Liverpool as an attacking force before the interval.

It was a similar story in the second period and Fulham, with a little more ambition, would have pilfered victory – the closest they came was when King stung Alisson’s palms from 20 yards – but at least they were able to go home happy. You couldn’t say the same for Liverpool.
