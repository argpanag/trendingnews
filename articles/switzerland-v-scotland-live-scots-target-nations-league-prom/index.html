Switzerland v Scotland LIVE: Scots target Nations League promotion as Andy Robertson makes history

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:11 · source · permalink

There’s plenty at stake for Scotland as they take on Switzerland in their Nations League finale as a hopes of gaining promotion to the top-tier are still alive. A 2-1 win over North Macedonia affords Sebastien Pocognoli’s side an opportunity to clinch second place in Group B1 but they must win at the Sitterstadion in St Gallen tonight.

Switzerland have already secured top spot in the group and Scotland must take three points from tonight’s contest and hope that Slovenia fail to beat North Macedonia in the other group clash in Skopje. There is also a knock-on effect for Euro 2028, with second in the Nations League likely to hand the Tartan Army an easier qualifying group, despite being a co-host, with a pot two seeding before the draw.

It is also a historic night for captain Andy Robertson who has become Scotland's most-capped male player in history, beating Sir Kenny Dalglish’s record of 102 appearances, after leading out the team this evening.

Sebastien Pocognoli praises Andy Robertson ahead of historic 103rd Scotland cap

"I just told him that he has to enjoy it and after it's just a bonus for the future,” Pocognoli said when asked about Robertson becoming Scotland’s all-time record men’s appearance holder.

"So he needs to enjoy, to play with freedom. He has a lot of experience, so I don't need to tell him, but again, we are really proud of what we are living together with him. And the best way to have a good feeling for him is to have a good game."

Sebastien Pocognoli defends use of Scotland's back three

Sebastien Pocognoli bristled at a question from the BBC regarding his use of a back-three before facing Switzerland this evening. The Scotland coach has stuck with the same system following Friday’s unconvincing win over North Macedonia.

"I'm surprised the question comes before the game,” he said. "We create many chances against Slovenia away, North Macedonia away and in the last game at home, we play front-footed, we created chances.

"We keep two times a clean sheet, nearly three in the last game. We need to have some response against a strong opponent away from home, because if we want to be successful in the future we need to play against that type of team and I think we have the quality to try to make it difficult."

How does the Nations League impact Euro 2028 qualification?

Scotland are right on the cusp of falling into Pot 3 ahead of the Euro 2028 qualification draw next month. And yes, Scotland still have to qualify as there are only two spots ‘reserved’ for the four co-hosts.

Scotland finishing second in Nations League Group B1 would mean they remain in Pot 2 ahead of the qualifying draw. Only the 12 group winners, plus eight of the best second-place teams, qualify automatically.

If Scotland finish third, behind Switzerland Slovenia, they are likely to fall into Pot 3, giving Sebastien Pocognoli’s side a more difficult route if they are to clinch automatic qualification without the safety net of the reserved spots.

Switzerland team news: Scots to face much-changed side

Well, this is is a good an opportunity Scotland are going to get. Just four Switzerland players remain from the side that beat Scotland 3-0 at Hampden, with Granit Xhaka, Johan Manzambi, Dan Ndoye, Manuel Akanji and Ricardo Rodriguez all unavailable.

Samuel Ballet makes just his second Switzerland appearance, Adrian Bajrami his third, Winsley Boteli his fourth, Zachary Athekame his sixth, Isaac Schmidt and Alvyn Sanches their seventh, so this is a much-changed, inexperienced team indeed.

How Andy Robertson defied the odds to become Scotland’s greatest captain

Andy Robertson has worked to squeeze everything he could possibly get from his career.

Perhaps it was overcoming rejection that made him take nothing for granted, but Robertson has made history by turning the ordinary into extraordinary: by showing up camp after camp, for never letting his country down.

There is the work-ethic, the commitment, the desire to push for every cap as if it is his last. There are the personal touches, too, forming a spirit and a togetherness that took a Scotland squad further than they had done in a generation.

But above all, Robertson continued to play the game in the way he always vowed to: as if he was a fan who had just come down from the stands.

And in his relentless pursuit, he has made a nation proud along the way.

Who would Scotland play if they finish second in Nations League Group B1?

If the stars align and Scotland do finish as runners-up, they will advance to the two-legged Nations League promotion/relegation play-offs in March.

They would play one of the teams who finished third or fourth in League A, which could end up being someone like Wales, Croatia, Serbia, or Norway or Denmark.

What Scotland need to secure Nations League promotion play-off spot

Switzerland have already sealed automatic promotion to League A as group winners, with the runner-up in B1 advancing to a promotion play-off in March. A third place finish will keep Scotland in the B League, the competition’s second tier.

Switzerland top the table with an unassailable 13 points while Slovenia sit second on eight and Scotland are third on seven. Earlier in the group stages, Scotland secured a goalless draw with Slovenia before losing a crucial tie 2-1 at Hampden Park back in early October. That means Slovenia have a superior head-to-head record against Scotland.

Although a draw against Switzerland would be enough to move level on eight points with Slovenia (should they also lose to North Macedonia), Slovenia’s superior head-to-head record would win them promotion over Scotland. That means Scotland must win this evening and move to 10 points in the table to be in with a chance of getting promoted. Slovenia also have to drop points against North Macedonia.

Scotland's Nations League results

Scotland were able to get their first home victory under Sebastien Pocognoli on Friday: now the task is to beat someone not called North Macedonia. A 2-1 win at Hampden was not all that convincing, and it required a John McGinn penalty to ensure the three points. Scotland’s hopes of achieving promotion are still alive but they will look back on that costly double-header against Slovenia should they finish third.

What is the Switzerland team news?

Encouraging, from a Scotland perspective. Switzerland have already wrapped up top spot in Group B1, with four wins and a draw. And they will be without the services of captain Granit Xhaka, Aston Villa’s Johan Manzambi, Nottingham Forest’s Dan Ndoye, and Inter’s Manuel Akanji, who are all out. Head coach Murat Yakin said the World Cup quarter-finalists, who lost to Argentina on extra time, will offer some opportunities to players on the fringes of his squad.

What is the Scotland team news?

Sebastien Pocognoli could stick with largely the same side that started the 2-1 win over North Macedonia at Hampden on Friday, as he was able to recall the returning Scott McTominay and Che Adams. There have been a few question marks over the place of Ben Gannon-Doak, who has looked a little uneasy playing as the right wing-back in Pocognoli’s 3-4-3 formation. It’s set to be a history evening for Andy Robertson, too, as the Scotland captain prepares to win his 103rd cap, moving him clear of Sir Kenny Dalglish’s record and equalling it on Friday.

Good evening

Well, it may not have the drama and star-dust of England and Spain’s classic in Madrid last night, but there’s still plenty on the line for Scotland as they head to Switzerland with hopes of reaching the top-tier of the Nations League still alive.

For Scotland, the equation is simple: they must beat the Swiss and hope North Macedonia pick up their first point of the Nations League by denying Slovenia victory in Group B1.
