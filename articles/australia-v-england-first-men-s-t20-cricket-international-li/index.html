10th over: England 119-4 (Curran 1, Jacks 2) “Hi Rob,” says Simon McMahon. “Sorry, not been as up to date with the blogs recently, I had no idea you’d been unwell, sounds scary, glad to hear you’re on the mend. Let’s leave it to England for any collapses from now on, eh. Take it easy.”

WICKET! England 117-4 (Banton c Shaw b Dwarshuis 39)

Is it a bird? Is it a plane? No, it’s Lachlan Bloody Shaw! He has just taken a spectacular diving catch to get rid of Tom Banton, a delightful moment for a kid making his international debut. Banton walloped Dwarshuis flat and hard towards deep square leg, where Shaw ran round the boundary and swooped elegantly to his right to take the catch. Lovely stuff.

9th over: England 110-3 (Banton 34, Curran 0) That was another barnstorming innings from Brook: 65 from 28 balls with four fours and six sixes. In his last six T20 internationals – yes, that’s an entirely gratuitous sample size – Brook has an average of 136 and a strike rate of 220.

“Following along the OBO and great to see another fresh-faced bowler joining our ranks,” says Will Brookes. “If Henry Crocombe’s nickname isn’t Boosh then can we start a petition to change it? Niche baking references make the best nicknames.”

Tell that to ‘One Size’ Fitz Hall.

8th over: England 104-2 (Brook 64, Banton 29) Adam Zampa’s first ball is dumped behind square for four by Banton. His third ball is a lamentable – nay, despicable – long hop that Brook hoicks into the crowd at backward square leg. He has 64 from 26 balls, including 57 from the last 17.

Banton completes another big over with consecutive boundaries, a reverse-sweep over short third and a sadistic thwack down the ground. Try explaining that sentence to a Martian.

6th over: England 74-2 (Brook 53, Banton 10) Edwards has a big LBW shout turned down when Banton misses a heave at a slower ball. That looks really close, but Australia decide not review.

Good lord, Brook’s at it again. He launches Edwards into the crowd at midwicket, his fifth sixth in the last 10 balls. A ferocious four down the ground brings up a stunning fifty to 20 balls; that includes 45 runs from the last 11 deliveries. If only England produced players of such unfettered genius.

Banton clears the leaping fielder at long on, just, for his first six. Mercy.

5th over: England 56-2 (Brook 42, Banton 4) The fourth dropped catch in five overs. Banton smokes the ball back at Ben Dwarshuis, who can’t take a very difficult chance in his follow through. A tenth of a chance at best.

Four dropped catches – and now four sixes for Harry Brook, who slams a short ball from Dwarshuis over midwicket with disdain. What a player! He touches the parts that no England player since Kevin Pietersen has reached.

4th over: England 42-2 (Brook 30, Banton 2) It’s unadvisable to give Harry Brook one life, never mind two. He punishes Australia – and demonstrates his sheer genius – by hitting Meredith for three sixes in a row.

First he jumped inside the line to help a short ball round the corner. The next delivery was pretty much in the same spot, but this time Brook leaned back to uppercut it over the slips.

He charged the next ball, pulling it violently over midwicket, then threaded four past extra cover. Oh, the audacity!

Twenty-four from the over, and in the blink of an eye Brook is 30 not out from 14 balls.

“England’s batters are in the image of their coach and, I suspect, will continue to play a shot a ball until they run out of batters,” says James Matthews. “However, it’s worth looking at the other side of the fence: Australia have bowled superbly; mixing up pace and line to prevent the batters getting any sort of rhythm. If only their catching was up to the quality of their bowling, they’d have this game in the bag already.”

3rd over: England 18-2 (Brook 7, Banton 1) Blimey, Brook has been dropped again! This is bizarre. He whirled Edwards to short third, where Zampa got in a pickle and helped the ball over his left shoulder to the boundary. That was the easiest chance of the three that have been put down.

We know Brendon McCullum’s England like to run towards the danger. At the moment they’re re-enacting the final scene of Blackadder Goes Forth.

Cummins to continue as Test captain

Cricket Australia has thrown its “unanimous” support behind embattled Test captain Pat Cummins, saying the bowler will continue in his leadership role despite the intense pressure on him after the sandpapergate controversy resurfaced in recent weeks.

Cummins’ position as captain had been called into question after the publication of Usman Khawaja’s memoir, in which the former Australia opener claimed the entire team knew of the plot to tamper with the ball on the tour of South Africa in 2018.

But after the CA chair, David Boon, and the CEO, Todd Greenberg, met with Cummins in Sydney on Friday, they gave him their full backing in a statement on Saturday.

“We’ve expressed our support to Pat and our concern for everyone impacted by the events of the 2018 tour of South Africa,” Boon said.

“We have learned from that experience and are proud of the team and the culture Pat and other leaders in the team have fostered over the past eight years and unequivocally reject any implication that Pat’s integrity has been compromised.”

Preamble

We interrupt the sandpaper news cycle to bring you some live cricket: Australia v England at the MCG, the first in a five-match T20 series. It’s two years until the next T20 World Cup, so the contextometer isn’t exactly zinging, but this should be a fun contest between the teams who are third and first in the ICC rankings.

England’s recent T20 form puts the ‘cor’ in ‘coruscating’: they’ve won 23 out of 26 completed games since Harry Brook became full-time captain, and put the world champions India across their knee during the English summer.

Australia’s annus has been a little more horribilis. They lost 3-0 in Pakistan and left no trace at the T20 World Cup. A 3-0 win over Bangladesh in June provided a soupçon of hope for the future, particularly a World Cup on home soil in 2028. Victory over England would be a good way to build on that.
