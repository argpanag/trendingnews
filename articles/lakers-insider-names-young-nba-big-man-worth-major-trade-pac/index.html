Milwaukee Bucks center Kel'el Ware has emerged as a hypothetical Lakers trade fit who could provide size, rebounding, rim protection, and frontcourt versatility alongside Walker Kessler.

The Los Angeles Lakers' search for a better frontcourt solution could eventually lead them toward a younger and more ambitious option, with NBA reporter Jovan Buha making a strong case for Kel'el Ware as a hypothetical trade fit.

The LA insider was asked on Buha's Block about the possibility of pairing Ware with Walker Kessler, potentially using the 22-year-old as a starting power forward and backup center.

While the discussion was purely hypothetical, Buha revealed that Ware ranked highly among the centers he evaluated for Los Angeles during the offseason.

"You are speaking my language because I had a scouting report breakdown on Kel'el Ware earlier in the summer," Buha said. "He was there in my center rankings. I want to say he was third or fourth."

"He was third or fourth in my offseason center rankings for the Lakers. So, I'm on board. Would I empty the clip for him? Potentially," he continued.

"I would for sure give up the [pick] swap plus a second or two [second-round picks], and I would for sure give up the three seconds. Like, if either one of those was enough to get him, I would do it. Would I fully unload the clip? Maybe, maybe."

Part of the appeal is the possibility of playing Ware alongside Kessler rather than viewing him exclusively as a backup center.

"I think he can play with Kessler. You can also play with Mamu, and it does give you an element of size, rebounding, rim protection," Buha explained. "That backline of Ware and Kessler is scary."

Such a pairing would directly address one of the issues Buha has repeatedly highlighted with the current Lakers roster: a lack of size, rebounding, and rim protection once Kessler leaves the floor.

"I think that the interesting thing with Ware is how young he is and how talented he is. He could be more of the four or like the Jaren Jackson Jr. type in this scenario," Buha explained.

Miami selected Ware with the No. 15 overall pick in the 2024 NBA Draft.

Ware averaged 9.3 points, 7.4 rebounds, and 1.1 blocks over 64 games as a rookie and earned NBA All-Rookie Second Team honors.

His production increased during his second season. Ware appeared in 77 games for Miami in 2025-26, posting 11.1 points, 9.0 rebounds, and 1.1 blocks while shooting 53.0% from the field and 39.5% from deep.

After his time in Miami ended during the blockbuster Giannis Antetokounmpo trade this past summer, Ware has continued to produce in a reserve role, averaging 13.3 points and 8.5 rebounds through Milwaukee's first four games.
