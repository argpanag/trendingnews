A golden Clásico: Lamine and Mbappé grace the pitch before the red carpet

There are football matches, and then there is El Clásico. The fixture that splits Spain down the middle, brings half the planet to a standstill and, for ninety minutes, turns every other concern into something that can wait. Barcelona and Real Madrid meet again tonight (21:00, DAZN), and this time even the calendar has decided to dress for the occasion.

Because barely 24 hours later, the Ballon d’Or red carpet awaits. First, though, comes the green turf. Lamine Yamal and Kylian Mbappé, two of the leading contenders for world football’s biggest individual prize, must first settle their unfinished business on the pitch. Two stars going head-to-head, and a rivalry that has never lacked fuel.

OneFootball Videos

History also beckons for Barça. Hansi Flick’s side have won their first nine league games, matching the best-ever start, a record previously held outright by Real Madrid’s 1968/69 team. Winning El Clásico would mean an unprecedented ten wins out of ten and a place of their own in the record books.

They arrive flying high, but also smarting. PSG gave them a rough ride on Tuesday (4-2), a reminder that this Barcelona side still have another level to reach when they face Europe’s elite. Paris also left its scars: Dani Olmo will be out for around a month, joining Koundé, Eric García, Christensen and Bardghji on the sidelines.

Madrid arrive with less sparkle and more questions. They keep winning, picking up points and moving forward, but still do not quite look like the team Mourinho wants to build. The Portuguese coach is growing tired of being asked for a finished product when, he insists, he has barely laid the first bricks.

"We’ve been here three months," he pointed out on Saturday, before putting the actual working time, between pre-season and international breaks, at "a month and a half or two months". Flick, Mourinho insists, has a considerable head start. "I don’t think comparisons are fair." The problem is that El Clásico has never made allowances for settling-in periods.

The casualty list does not help either. Vinicius is serving the second game of his four-match ban and will watch the fixture he enjoys most from the sidelines. Mendy, Asencio and Rodrygo complete Madrid’s list of absentees. Without Vini, the spotlight falls even more squarely on Mbappé. And Lamine awaits on the other side.

On Monday, one of the two may leave the ceremony with a Ballon d’Or in his hands. Tonight, that matters rather less. Before the spotlights, tuxedos and speeches comes the match that leaves nowhere to hide: Madrid against Barça, Negreira against Real Madrid TV, Mbappé against Lamine, Mourinho against Flick, and a record waiting to be claimed. The latest mother of all battles. And this one comes with golden highlights, courtesy of the Ballon d’Or.
