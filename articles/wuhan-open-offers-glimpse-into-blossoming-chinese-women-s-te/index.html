When China's top player Zheng Qinwen announced to withdraw from the 2026 Wuhan Open due to a right shoulder injury, many home fans were left disappointed.

The 24-year-old had just lifted the China Open trophy and was expected to spearhead the home challenge in central China. Her absence, however, turned attention to the depth and resilience of Chinese women's tennis.

Guo Hanyu returns a shot during the women's sinlges round of 16 match between Guo Hanyu of China and Elina Svitolina of Ukraine at the 2026 Wuhan Open tennis tournament in Wuhan, capital of central China's Hubei Province, Oct. 15, 2026. (Xinhua/Xiao Yijiu)

SHI HAN: GROWING THROUGH THE LOWER TIERS

Ranked outside the world's top 200, 21-year-old Shi Han was hardly a household name when she arrived in Wuhan.

She earned her place in the main draw the hard way, qualifying by beating top-seeded American Peyton Stearns and top-100 player Kamilla Rakhimova.

Drawn against 13th seed and world No. 19 Solana Sierra in the first round, Shi admitted she was taken aback by the challenge.

"I had finally made it through qualifying, only to get such a tough draw," Shi recalled after the match.

With little to lose, she decided to give it everything she had. After dropping the opening set 6-4, Shi fought back to win the next two 6-3, 6-3. Her first serve remained reliable, with speeds still reaching around 180 kph deep into the decider. The win was Shi's first over a top-20 opponent and her first main-draw victory at a WTA 1000 event.

Shi then pushed former US Open finalist Leylah Fernandez in the second round, taking a 4-1 lead in the second set before Fernandez closed out the match in straight sets. Shi left Wuhan encouraged rather than disheartened.

"As everyone jokes, maybe I came here to take tuition," she smiled. "The players I've met in Wuhan are all ranked much higher than me. I'll give everything I have, then look back and see where the gaps are between me and them."

This season, Shi has spent much of her time competing on the ITF circuit, away from the WTA spotlight. Yet she has quietly compiled 64 match wins this year, eight more than world No. 1 Elena Rybakina, even though victories at lower-tier tournaments yield relatively few ranking points.

Starting the year outside top 400, Shi has climbed into the top 200 and secured a place in the 2027 Australian Open qualifying draw.

"I've played a lot of tournaments this season. I wanted to improve my ranking as quickly as possible and get back to the Grand Slams," Shi said. "I'm quite satisfied with the effort I've put in. I have nothing to lose. I hope to go further and play better tennis."

GUO HANYU: FIGHTING BACK AGAINST ADVERSITY

Wild card Guo Hanyu, ranked No. 240 in singles but fresh from Wimbledon doubles glory, arrived in Wuhan with modest expectations. Facing 12th seed and world No. 16 Sorana Cirstea in the opening round, Guo lost the first set 6-1 and trailed 0-4 in the second.

"Until the match was over, I didn't think I was going to win," Guo said. "I just wanted to stay on court a little longer and enjoy the atmosphere on the center court. I didn't expect my form to keep getting better."

Trailing 6-3 in the second-set tiebreak, Guo faced three set points. She then seized control, winning five straight points to take the tiebreak 8-6. Trailing 3-5 in the decider, she broke Cirstea when the Romanian served for the match and reeled off four consecutive games to complete a remarkable 1-6, 7-6(6), 7-5 comeback.

She followed that with another marathon win over American Katie Volynets, prevailing 6-4, 4-6, 7-6(3) after more than three hours on court. Guo's run ended against fifth seed Elina Svitolina in the third round, but reaching the last 16 was her best singles result at a WTA 1000 event and made her the only Chinese player to reach that stage in Wuhan.

Her achievement carried extra significance amid personal turmoil. Shortly after returning from Wimbledon, where she partnered Kristina Mladenovic to claim the women's doubles title, Guo learned her father had been admitted to intensive care. She has since struggled to focus and broke down at her post-match press conference when discussing him.

"I can't stand on court and enjoy the match happily like I used to. There has always been a feeling of sadness around me," she said. "I've tried very hard to pull myself out of it, but it's difficult."

"I keep telling myself to leave it to fate. If this is something I have to go through, I can't escape it. All I can do is play point by point, give my best and see where fate takes me."

Last year, Guo recorded her first WTA 1000 main-draw victory in Montreal. At the time, she described it as feeling as if she had "hit the jackpot" by suddenly producing an exceptional performance.

After winning two main-draw matches in Wuhan, she now carries greater confidence into future tournaments.

"At least against players ranked in the top 100, whoever I'm facing, I feel I have a way to compete," she said. "Maybe I won't win every time, but I believe every match brings progress and something to learn."

ZHANG SHUAI: DEFYING AGE TO DREAM BIG

On Oct. 13, 37-year-old Zhang Shuai walked onto center court to face 22-year-old American Ashlyn Krueger.

Zhang began her professional career in 2006, meaning her time on tour almost matches the age of her young opponent.

But on this night, fatigue was evident.

Zhang struggled with her movement, her shots lacked their usual power, and she repeatedly bent over her racket to catch her breath. She eventually lost 6-3, 6-0.

Zhang's heavy workload this year helps explain her fatigue. Since the US Open, she has played at the Billie Jean King Cup, won an Asian Games silver in Nagoya, reached the China Open doubles final and then arrived in Wuhan two days later. Counting singles, doubles and mixed doubles, she has played 128 matches this year.

"At this intensity and at my age, I think I must be a superhero," she said. "Even though I played like this today, I'm still very proud of myself."

As she left Wuhan, the veteran was already looking ahead.

"I'm not young anymore, but I'll give everything I have over the next year and try to come back to the Wuhan Open," Zhang said. "I hope I can perform better next year."
