Amazon refund payments are being sent: How to know if you'll be receiving one

TheTools · 06.10.2026 20:14 · source · permalink

Eligible Amazon customers are beginning to receive refunds as part of the company's $2.5 billion settlement with the Federal Trade Commission.

Related video above from 2025: Amazon settlement explained

The settlement, which was originally announced in 2025, was expanded last month to include more customers who could receive bigger refund payments.

Distribution of those expanded, accelerated payments – up to $200 for eligible customers – began on Oct. 1.

"The payments will be made via electronic payment (Venmo or PayPal) or mailed check without requiring consumers to submit claims, respond to notices or complete any forms," the FTC said last month.

The settlement was first announced in September 2025 over federal allegations that the company misled customers over Prime subscriptions. Amazon did not admit liability.

"The revised order will ensure more consumers who were harmed by Amazon's deceptive enrollment and cancellation practices benefit from the FTC's historic settlement," said Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, in a prepared statement.

What's the settlement for?

Amazon has sent out automatic payments as part of a $2.5 billion settlement over what the FTC described as "dark patterns," or design tricks that made it deceptively easy to enroll in Prime and frustratingly difficult to cancel. In its lawsuit, the FTC said Amazon buried the option to avoid Prime during checkout and required customers to navigate a lengthy unsubscribe path that employees reportedly called the "Iliad," a nod to the long Greek epic you probably read in school, for how many pages and clicks it took to actually cancel your subscription.

Amazon did not admit liability in the settlement but must pay a $1 billion civil penalty and issue $1.5 billion in refunds to customers affected by its enrollment and cancellation practices for Prime subscriptions.

Who is eligible?

Under the revised order, Amazon will be issuing refunds to those who used between 11 and 20 Prime benefits within a 12-month period.

Previously, automatic refunds covered U.S. consumers who signed up for Prime between June 23, 2019, and June 23, 2025, through what the FTC calls a "challenged enrollment flow" who used no more than three Prime benefits within a 12-month period to qualify. Those who used fewer than 10 Prime benefits within a 12-month period were also eligible for a refund in the second phase of the settlement.

What exactly counts as a "benefit?"

The National Consumer Unit previously asked the FTC directly about what qualifies as a "benefit."

The agency clarified that each Prime benefit refers to the total uses of Prime benefits in a year. For example, if you used two-day shipping when you otherwise wouldn't have received two-day shipping, each instance counts as one Prime benefit used.

If you're a Prime user who makes the most of your membership, this settlement probably isn't aimed at you. The FTC says the refund money is intended for consumers who were misled into signing up and then trapped in a cancellation maze they didn't expect.

How much money will eligible customers receive?

According to the FTC, under the revised settlement, eligible Prime customers can receive a refund of their Amazon Prime subscription fees up to $200. This amount was $51 in the original agreement, so those who previously received that amount could still be eligible for $149 more.

"If consumer-accepted payments do not reach the required threshold by February 2027, Amazon will provide additional automatic payments to consumers who previously received refunds under the settlement," according to the FTC. "As part of the revised order, consumers who have accepted payments will be eligible to receive an additional $149, for a total of $200. This last round of supplemental payments will start by April 2027 and will also be distributed automatically."
