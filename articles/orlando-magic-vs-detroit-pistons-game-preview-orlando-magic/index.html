Orlando Magic vs. Detroit Pistons: Game Preview | Orlando Magic

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:06 · source · permalink

ORLANDO – There is no shortage of storylines for the next game on the Orlando Magic’s docket.

Whether it’s the potential for Orlando to advance in the NBA Cup, revenge against the team that erased a 3-1 first-round series deficit to eliminate the Magic in the 2026 playoffs, or just looking to rebound after a tough loss, there’s plenty at stake.

The Magic (7-9) will attempt to achieve all three of those goals by earning a home win over the Detroit Pistons (10-6) on Tuesday at 7 p.m. ET.

“To be honest, we feel a sense to respond just based on the way our season is going,” said Magic guard Anthony Black. “We’re coming off a bad loss. So, we know we have to respond. Obviously, we’re playing the team that knocked us out (of the playoffs). It’s a cup game. There’s a chance to win the prize money, advance (in the tournament). So, there’s no reason for us to come out flat at all. Got a lot of things to play for today.”

The Magic, who are 3-0 in cup play, could clinch NBA Group A with a win over the Pistons. They can also clinch home-court advantage in the tournament quarterfinals with a win and a loss by either the Boston Celtics or Miami Heat.

“It’s huge,” said Desmond Bane. “You’re playing for something during the season. I don’t think you can take these games lightly. It’s a good measuring stick early in the season. We’ve played some good teams already, but anytime you get to a chance to play some good teams, it’s good.”

Orlando is determined to get off to a fast start against Detroit, after it was slow to get out of the gate in Washington, D.C.

“I'd like for us to show the competitiveness that we’ve been showing here,” said Magic head coach Sean Sweeney. “The other night was not quite what we’re looking for there, so I would like to see the competitiveness that we have been showing. We’ve done a really good job of attacking on both ends of the floor. So, we would like to see that come out tonight.”

As the Magic are well aware, points against the Pistons are always at a premium. Detroit possesses the league’s fifth-best defensive rating (109.2), is second in blocks (5.8) and fifth in steals (9.3) per game.

Offensively, the Pistons rely on Cade Cunningham to be their engine. The No. 1 overall pick in the 2021 NBA Draft is averaging 24.2 points, 5.1 rebounds and 8.3 assists per matchup this season. After scoring just five points in 33 minutes of action against the Hawks on Sunday, the two-time All-Star will certainly be searching for a bounce-back performance.

IN AND OUT: Paolo Banchero (illness / right high ankle sprain), Jonathan Isaac (left knee injury management), Izaiyah Nelson (left ankle injury recovery) and Noah Penda (right partially torn hamstring) are out for Orlando.

For Detroit, Cunningham (right knee soreness) is probable, while Jalen Duren (right hamstring strain), Ebuka Okorie (left oblique strain), Taurean Prince (right knee arthroscopy) and Duncan Robinson (left quadriceps strain) are out.

KEY STATS: Through 15 games, Bane is averaging 9.9 3-point attempts per game and 14.1 per 100 possessions. Those marks last year were 5.2 and 7.4, which were his lowest since his rookie season. He’s currently shooting 42.6 percent from deep. The only players averaging at least nine attempts and shooting over 40 percent from beyond the arc among qualified players are Bane, Steph Curry, Klay Thompson and Robinson. More on Bane

Franz Wagner is averaging 26.2 points per game in November, the highest mark of any month in his career. His prior high was 25.7 points per outing in Feb. 2025.

NBA CUP BREAKDOWN: The Emirates NBA Cup 2026 group play action continues tonight.

The Emirates NBA Cup will consist of two stages: group play and the knockout rounds.

All 30 teams have been randomly drawn into groups of five within their conference based on win-loss records from the 2025-26 regular season. In addition to Detroit, Milwaukee, Brooklyn and Toronto are also in Orlando’s group. The Magic are 3-0 in NBA Cup action after beating the Nets last week.

Continuing through Friday, Nov. 27, each team will play four designated group play games on “Cup Nights” – one game against each opponent in its group, with two games at home and two on the road.

Eight teams will advance to the knockout rounds: the team with the best standing in group play games in each of the six groups and two “wild cards” (the team from each conference with the best record in Group Play games that finished second in its group).

The knockout rounds will consist of single-elimination games in the quarterfinals, semifinals and championship. The quarterfinals (Friday, Dec. 4 and Saturday, Dec. 5) and semifinals (Tuesday, Dec. 8 and/or Wednesday, Dec. 9) will be played in NBA team markets, with each game at the home arena of the higher-seeded team. The teams in the knockout rounds will compete for a prize pool and the NBA Cup championship trophy.

All games across both stages of the Emirates NBA Cup will count toward the regular-season standings except the championship, which will take place on Friday, Dec. 11 at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis.
