What we learned as short-handed Warriors put up valiant effort, but fall to 0-3 with loss to Suns

TheTools · 29.10.2026 02:22 · source · permalink

Dalton Johnson delivers the takeaways from the Golden State Warriors' 131-122 loss to the Phoenix Suns on Saturday night at Mortgage Matchup Center.

  • BOX SCORE

The sting of the Warriors’ late collapse to the Memphis Grizzlies on Friday night cut deeper knowing how short-handed they could be the next night in Phoenix against the Suns in their first of 15 back-to-backs this season. 

Steph Curry didn’t suit up Saturday night. Neither did Draymond Green, Al Horford and Brandin Podziemski, among the other usuals for the Warriors. Their backs were against the wall, and the Warriors didn’t lack in fight. They did lack in experienced talent, though.

Devin Booker torched the Warriors for 43 points as they fell to the Suns 131-122 at Mortgage Matchup Center. He was the Suns’ only player to score 20 points in a game where the Warriors had three of them, and it didn’t matter. 

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg led the way with 26 points for his first 20-point game of his young NBA career. Lendeborg also had nine rebounds and five assists. De’Anthony Melton (25 points) and Brandon Williams (20 points) also reached the mark. 

Golden State’s defense in the first three games of the season has been a stroll in the park for its opponents. The Suns shot 58.1 percent from the field and tallied 60 points in the paint. 

Here are three takeaways from the Warriors’ third straight loss to open the 2026-27 NBA season.

Playing Through Yaxel 

The Warriors already are basically leaning on Lendeborg to be their No. 2 scorer. In their first game of the season without Curry, he became the Warriors’ top scoring option. Really, the entire offense needed to run through him when taking the absences of Green and Podziemski into account. 

And Lendeborg was first to get the Warriors on the scoreboard with a three just to the right of the arc. His next shot was another made three, connecting just to the left of the arc. Lendeborg’s first three made shots were 3-pointers after draining one from the right corner.

But his most impressive sequence might have been Lendeborg’s fourth made shot, sealing Dillon Brooks for a mismatch down and finishing through contact for a three-point play. Lendeborg went into halftime as the Warriors' leading scorer with 13 points, plus four rebounds and two assists.

Lendeborg proved the Warriors can play through him as a main option. The rookie made six 3-pointers and should have had many more than five assists if his teammates made shots off his dribble-drive passes. This was a Warriors loss, but a win in the development of Lendeborg.

Big Dubs Duo

Having Graham Ike and Charles Bassey as the Warriors’ centers set a tone. The Warriors were all over the glass and keeping possessions alive. Ike, who started the game at center, and Bassey, who made his season debut after missing the first two games to knee tendonitis, combined for nine points and eight rebounds in the first quarter.

As a team, the Warriors had 10 second-chance points, nine bench points and eight offensive rebounds in the first quarter to hold a three-point lead. But they also committed eight turnovers for 11 points. That number rose to 12 turnovers for 15 points with the Suns now leading by three.

While the Warriors did have 17 second-chance points in the first half, they continued to be too sloppy for a team that has such little margin for error. When it looked like the Suns were about to separate themselves with a 9-1 run during the third quarter, it was Bassey who carried the Warriors in answering with a 12-0 run to take the lead. 

Ike recorded a 12-point, 10-rebound double-double, and Bassey added 10 points and seven rebounds off the bench on a perfect 4-of-4 shooting. They accounted for 17 of the Warriors’ 44 rebounds and seven of their 19 offensive rebounds. The problem is, the Warriors allowed as many points off turnovers (26) as second-chance points scored (26). 

Richard's Role 

All signs pointed to the Warriors finding a steal late in the second round of the 2025 NBA Draft when they took Will Richard out of Florida. In just his seventh career game last year, Richard started and scored 30 points on a night where the Warriors didn’t have their regulars. He wasn’t close to the same player a little more than a year later in Phoenix.

Richard had the same amount of turnovers and fouls as shot attempts at halftime: One. He missed his one shot attempt, a three from the left corner. It often felt like the Warriors were playing 4-on-5 offensively having Richard on the floor. 

Then in the third quarter, Richard hit two threes that felt like they were worth triple that. The first was wide-open from the right wing where Richard looked around the court and then finally launched from deep. The second was in rhythm from the right corner, where Richard caught a bounce pass and in the same motion splashed a three, which is the exact shot the Warriors have to rely on him for. 

Richard made a smooth reverse layup cutting under the hoop early in the fourth quarter, showing more shades of what the Warriors expected. The player he was in the second half, scoring 11 points, was much more reminiscent of who the Warriors need going forward.
