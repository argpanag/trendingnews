No friendly fire as Creamline, Choco Mucho battle for F4 boost

TheTools · 12.11.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

Past records won’t matter when everything is on the line.

Creamline may hold a commanding 19-1 head-to-head record against Choco Mucho, but that overwhelming advantage will count for little when the two rivals collide in a high-stakes PVL All-Filipino Conference Siklab Series showdown tonight (Thursday) at the Smart Araneta Coliseum.

The Cool Smashers may draw confidence from their historical dominance, but the Flying Titans are determined to rewrite the narrative and score a victory when it matters most.

With the semifinal race reaching a critical stretch and every victory carrying enormous weight, the corporate allies will once again become fierce rivals, each eyeing a crucial win in their 6 p.m. encounter.

The Big Dome is expected to be split down the middle, with both teams boasting some of the league's most passionate and sizeable fan bases. And with both coming off painful defeats, the urgency surrounding this latest installment of their rivalry could hardly be greater.

Creamline, carrying a 4-1 record, remains in second place behind unbeaten Nxled in the scramble for Final Four spots. But the Cool Smashers are looking to regain their bearing after the surging PLDT High Speed Hitters handed them a four-set defeat last week, snapping their four-game winning streak.

Choco Mucho, meanwhile, suffered an equally frustrating setback, absorbing a five-set loss to Akari last Saturday to slide to 3-3.

Although the Flying Titans remain in third in points total, the pressure is mounting, with Farm Fresh and PLDT both carrying 3-2 records and Akari also in the thick of the race at 3-3.

With only five elimination-round playdates remaining, there is little room for missteps. Firepower will remain essential, but composure, consistency and timely adjustments could prove even more decisive, particularly in matches that go down-to-the-wire.

“The losses are very degrading, pero ayun na nga, it’s part of the game. It’s part of being a professional athlete. You win and you lose. It’s just how we, as a team, handle the loss and bounce back from that,” said Choco Mucho middle blocker Maddie Madayag.

“I think, give us a day to recover and think about what went wrong and what we can do to make it better. But again, we have to fight against Creamline,” she added.

Before the main attraction, Nxled shoots for the first semifinal berth when it takes on a resurgent Akari side at 1 p.m.

The Chameleons have swept their first five assignments and are determined to preserve their unbeaten record while sealing their place in the Final Four.

“I know that every single coach is studying the games deeply. I know everybody’s got their own system and strategy, et cetera. But I really think that now, the girls are really good at doing what I’m asking them to do,” said Nxled head coach Ettore Guidetti.

“So they know what I want. They don’t need a long speech or something like this. In two words, we organize our block and defense, and, at the same time, our block tactic,” he added.

Brooke Van Sickle will once again lead Nxled's charge, drawing support from Myla Pablo, MJ Phillips, Jackie Acuña and Jonah Escamillan, with ace playmaker Gel Cayuna expected to orchestrate the offense.

But Akari is hardly an opponent to be taken lightly.

The Chargers have breathed new life into their semis campaign, responding to the guidance of assistant coach Edison Orcullo with a win-loss-win run in a stretch of five-set battles. Their dramatic escape over the Flying Titans should also provide a timely confidence boost heading into the showdown.

Akari has enough firepower to challenge the league-leading Chameleons, with Eli Soyud, Ivy Lacsina, Faith Nisperos, Grethcel Soltones, Chenie Tagaod and Tin Tiamzon leading the attack.
Still, the spotlight will ultimately fall on the Creamline-Choco Mucho showdown.

The Cool Smashers, led by Jema Galanza, Bernadette Pons, Tots Carlos, Michele Gumabao and Alyssa Valdez, have largely had their way against the Flying Titans since their first meeting in 2019, winning 19 of their 20 encounters. Choco Mucho finally broke through in 2024, only for Creamline to reassert its dominance and claim the next seven meetings.

That history gives Creamline a psychological edge, but the stakes of tonight's encounter could make past results irrelevant.

For the Cool Smashers, it is an opportunity to bounce back, strengthen their hold on second place and keep their semifinal campaign on track.

For the Flying Titans, it is a chance for the likes of Eya Laure, Sisi Rosales, Kat Tolentino and Maddie Madayag to finally turn the tide against their corporate sister, gain valuable ground in the standings and make a statement when the pressure is at its highest.

One team seeks to reaffirm its mastery. The other is hungry to prove that the tide can finally turn.
Thursday night, the only number that will matter is the one on the scoreboard.
