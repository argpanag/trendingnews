Will Thanksgiving be cold, dry or wet in California? Latest NOAA outlook

TheTools · 12.11.2026 18:08 · source · permalink
  • California is likely to experience wetter-than-average conditions during the Thanksgiving period, according to NOAA.
  • The forecast also favors above-normal temperatures for much of the state from late November into early December.
  • This weather pattern is influenced by El Niño, which can affect storm tracks and moisture from the Pacific.
  • The outlook indicates broad weather trends over two weeks, not specific daily rainfall or temperature totals.

California could be in for a wetter-than-usual Thanksgiving period, according to the latest outlook from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center is forecasting an increased likelihood of above-average precipitation across much of California from Nov. 21 through Dec. 4. The outlook covers Thanksgiving week, including the Nov. 26 holiday, and the days immediately before and after it.

The forecast also favors above-normal temperatures across the West Coast, suggesting Californians could see a relatively mild but wetter-than-normal stretch heading into the holiday weekend.

However, the outlook does not predict specific storms, rainfall totals or temperatures for Thanksgiving Day. Instead, it identifies which broad weather patterns are more likely over a two-week period.

Will California have a wet Thanksgiving?

Rain chances are a standout feature of NOAA's latest outlook for California.

The Climate Prediction Center favors above-average precipitation across much of the state during the Nov. 21-Dec. 4 period. California has some of the strongest probabilities for wetter-than-normal conditions in the country, according to the agency's forecast discussion.

The outlook points to an increased flow of Pacific moisture into the western United States as one factor supporting the wetter pattern.

NOAA's models show differences in where the strongest precipitation signal could develop. Some dynamical models favor a stronger signal across the Northwest, while statistical guidance influenced by El Niño points toward higher probabilities across the Southwest.

The combination of those signals gives much of California a tilt toward above-average precipitation.

That does not mean rain will fall every day or that every part of the state will receive the same amount. The outlook also does not establish whether precipitation will arrive as rain or snow at higher elevations.

Will California be warmer or colder than normal for Thanksgiving?

Despite the wetter outlook, NOAA is not favoring an unusually cold pattern across California.

Above-normal temperatures are favored along the West Coast during the Nov. 21-Dec. 4 period. The agency attributes part of that signal to above-average sea surface temperatures along the coast.

The broader weather pattern also favors onshore flow of Pacific air into the western United States.

That means California has a greater likelihood of experiencing temperatures above its seasonal average rather than a prolonged period of unusually cold conditions.

Still, above-average temperatures do not rule out chilly nights, cold spells or mountain snow. NOAA's outlook describes the most likely overall temperature category across a two-week period, not the conditions residents will experience every day.

How could El Niño affect California's Thanksgiving weather?

El Niño is playing a major role in NOAA's extended-range outlook.

The climate pattern, characterized by warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures across the central and eastern equatorial Pacific, is currently the dominant influence on the broader atmospheric pattern, according to the Climate Prediction Center.

El Niño can influence the position and strength of the Pacific jet stream, helping shape storm tracks and moisture availability across the southern United States and West Coast.

For California, the latest outlook favors enhanced Pacific moisture and above-average precipitation probabilities.

However, El Niño does not guarantee a wet holiday or a rainy winter across every part of the state. Individual storm tracks and shorter-term weather patterns can still produce substantial differences in conditions from one region to another.

When will NOAA update its Thanksgiving forecast?

NOAA's Week 3-4 outlook is a long-range forecast, so confidence in specific Thanksgiving Day conditions remains limited.

For now, the broad signal is a potentially wetter-than-normal Thanksgiving period across California, with temperatures leaning warmer than average.
