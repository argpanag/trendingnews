How many more points Kimi Antonelli needs to win 2026 Formula 1 title

TheTools · 04.10.2026 10:55 · source · permalink
  • Kimi Antonelli strengthened his grip on the 2026 F1 title after finishing second at the Bahrain Grand Prix, extending his lead at the top of the standings
  • The Mercedes driver now sits 84 points clear of teammate George Russell with seven Grands Prix and a Sprint race remaining
  • A specific points target could seal Antonelli's maiden world championship well before the Abu Dhabi finale

Kimi Antonelli is closing in on what would be a remarkable maiden Formula 1 world championship, and the maths is beginning to look very favourable for the young Mercedes star.

The 2026 season has been a masterclass in consistency from Antonelli, who added another 18 points to his tally with a second-place finish at the Bahrain Grand Prix.

That result, combined with a retirement for Mercedes teammate George Russell, pushed Antonelli's championship lead to 84 points. He now sits on 320 points to Russell's 236.

How Many Wins Does Antonelli Need?

With seven Grands Prix remaining on the calendar, plus the Singapore Sprint, there are 183 points still up for grabs. The upcoming races take the season through Singapore, the United States, Mexico, Brazil, Las Vegas, Qatar, and finally Abu Dhabi.

In the most straightforward scenario, four Grand Prix victories would be enough to wrap things up. Four wins add 100 points to Antonelli's tally, bringing him to 420. Even if Russell were to win every remaining race and the Singapore Sprint, collecting the full 183 points available, he would only reach 419; one point short of Antonelli.

That said, four wins is not necessarily the minimum requirement. Because Antonelli already leads by such a comfortable margin, any race in which Russell fails to score further reduces what the Briton can realistically achieve. A string of poor results from Russell could see Antonelli crowned champion with far fewer additional points needed.

When Could the F1 Title Be Decided?

Singapore is technically the earliest the title could be settled, though that would require an exceptional set of circumstances. Before the Bahrain race, Formula 1's own projections had outlined a scenario where Antonelli could have clinched in Singapore, provided he had won in Azerbaijan, Bahrain, the Singapore Sprint and the Singapore Grand Prix itself while Russell scored nothing across all of those. That particular path closed when Antonelli settled for second in Bahrain rather than a win.

Despite missing out on victory in Bahrain, his second place combined with Russell's retirement has arguably left him in a stronger position than many expected heading into the final stretch of the season.

The number to focus on for now is simple: 100. If Antonelli outscores Russell by 100 points across the remaining seven rounds, he will clinch the championship regardless of what else happens. Given his current advantage, the momentum is firmly with the Italian-Finnish driver as the season enters its defining phase.

Why Bahrain Grand Prix was held in Malaysia

We also highlighted facts about why the 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix has moved to Malaysia’s Sepang International Circuit, while retaining its Bahrain identity and Gulf Air sponsorship.

Sepang last hosted a Formula 1 race in 2017, and its return comes amid security concerns in the Middle East. The unusual relocation sets the stage for a championship battle at a circuit absent from the calendar for nine years.
