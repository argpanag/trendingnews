Rajkumar Periasamy’s ‘O.M: Operation Mole’ adds Hollywood-style grit and texture to a Tamil-style melodrama

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:40 · source · permalink

Posted on November 19, 2026

Mammootty plays Karthikeyan, a smuggler who exports red sandalwood. Dhanush plays Thirukumaran aka Thiru, the man Karthikeyan regards as a son. (Note that both have names of Lord Muruga.) Their gang is infiltrated by a mole sent by a forest officer named Nithya, played by Sai Pallavi. This part of the film is shot and edited like a Martin Scorsese movie, and it’s filled with a huge number of characters, a huge amount of detailing. And underneath this crime drama lies the human story of what happens when someone you think of as a bad man is actually a good man. Or what happens when an enemy becomes a friend. The film has a solid first half with a brilliantly unexpected interval point, while the second half struggles to strike a balance between crime drama and melodrama. But overall, there’s lots to like, especially the three lead performances. That’s the basic storyline and the quick review. A longer analysis follows, and it may contain some minor spoilers.

From the start, writer-director Rajkumar Periasamy demonstrates a purity of purpose. He knows the things we need in a movie with a big star, and so we get an energetic group dance set to a Sai Abhyankkar song that sounds like firecrackers going off inside a tin can. We get “cute” scenes with Sreeleela, who plays Thiru’s wife Roja. We get a larger-than-life action sequence in a forest that positions the Dhanush character as some kind of saviour. This stretch is set to the rousing, anthem-like ‘Veezha veera’. We get many intercuts to religious proceedings, as though to ground the Hollywood-like happenings in a local zone. Many of these choices feel unnecessary from a purist point of view, and yet, you understand the need for these elements. Because without these compromises, the good stuff might not have been possible. And I include these compromises in what I call Rajkumar Periasamy’s purity of purpose. For instance, he lets quite a major part of the film play out in Telugu because Nithya is from that part of the country.
