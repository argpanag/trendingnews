Abate: "Torino, dirty match against Monza, we will need heart and bite"

TheTools · 23.10.2026 18:57 · source · permalink

Turin

On match eve manager warns his side: "Every match is a struggle against ourselves" 

Two consecutive wins, ten points taken from the last five matches. Tomorrow evening Torino face Monza again, and compared to the Coppa Italia clash on 1 September won 1-0 by the Brianzoli, they seem to have transformed themselves. Ignazio Abate is making sure they stay on course, on the eve of matchday eight of the league campaign to be tackled with great focus. "The desire to improve must never leave us," the manager stressed when previewing the match. "We have ample room for improvement; I say that seeing the work done during the week and what we do in matches. We must work and persevere. I have the utmost confidence in the group; I am always very positive about how they work. If we underestimated what awaits us after two positive rounds, we would be on the wrong path. The team are training with the right grit; we are on matchday eight and we must continue to grow day in, day out." 

The opponents

This is how Abate previewed Ivan Juric's Monza, who are fresh from beating Cagliari 4-0 and seem to have found a top-class focal point in Varela, who has scored six goals at the start of this league campaign. "It will be an extremely difficult match. They will make it about intensity, about duels. It will be a dirty match, we will have to get our hands dirty and add quality within that dirt. I don't think it is a test of personality for the team. If you face teams that go man-to-man, you cannot think of a match to dominate. We must get into their mindset, first and foremost by winning the duels. We will need all 11 defending well. We know what Varela's characteristics are: he made a very strong start, he has great physicality, burst, and quality inside the penalty area. But I also have strong players at the back, with personality, physicality and pace. I expect a great match from them, but also from the forwards who will have to protect the ball as early as possible. We will have to be good at varying our line of engagement at different points in the match. Monza have 4-5 top-level players; they play offensive, aggressive football. It will be a difficult match; they are coming off a very good spell, but tomorrow at our ground it must be tough for them. There will be a great crowd and I can't wait for tomorrow, because we must continue to have this connection with our people. They have always stood by us in difficult moments; now it is up to us to try to carry them with us as much as possible." 

Journey

Abate described Toro's journey at the start of this season as follows: "Every match is a struggle against ourselves. We would be mediocre if we settled for two decent performances. I believe we are hitting our first seasonal target: we are creating the soul of the group. It is not easy after changing 11-12 players, including many foreigners, but it is happening. I want to continue seeing that bite; at Parma the team knew how to enjoy the suffering. Then we should have managed the match, but the soul of the group came out. We are only on matchday eight. We must build more solid foundations; Monza will be a tough obstacle, but they too must find something tough. Then the next target will be to nourish this soul, because it has to be watered day by day and not neglected; in a football town like this it is an obligation. We must improve defensively; we need to have the desire and anger not to concede. And finally, as the last step, we must be as recognisable as possible. I believe in hard work, head down and pedalling hard, hard, hard." 

Simeone and Vlasic

The manager then spoke about individuals. Starting with Simeone, who broke his league drought at Parma with a brace. "Giovanni has always put the group ahead of everything, the crest before anything else. He has always fought for the good of the team. He is a team player, and when you think about the team and not about your ego, then your luck turns. We are convinced of his qualities. I coach positive, energetic lads who care about this football club and its fans. Running to hug him after his second goal came naturally to me. I am passionate, hot-blooded; I believe in human connection because it represents who I am. I played in teams where people fought for common objectives and then perhaps at the end of the match everyone went their own way. That is not who I am. I need to feel the pulse of the dressing room, the bond between the players. The sense of family and duty is fundamental for me; I believe in it very much." "Simeone and Vlasic have had a similar journey at the start of this season," Abate added, "and both are growing immensely. Nikola, unlike Giovanni, feels the weight of the great responsibility he carries on his shoulders very heavily. But instead he needs to stay calm, because the most important thing is what the team and the manager think of him. Nikola is an incredible worker, a perfectionist; he spends 10 hours a day at the training ground. He must stay relaxed; I am not the one discovering his qualities. I am sure he will give us great satisfaction. As for Simeone, I think a manager must pursue his footballing ideas, but always keeping in mind the characteristics of his players. We started the season with one forward and perhaps in doing so I made things a bit difficult for Giovanni. Switching to two forwards he seemed much more at ease; along the way we then had to find a certain balance between the players' characteristics and their fitness. It is a process; now I am beginning to know them better and understand what they can give you right away and where we can work. Simeone can read the game and understands it, as can Adams, Zapata, Kulenovic and Pellegri." 

The individuals

Against Monza, in addition to Ilkhan and Fortini, Abate will not have Rodriguez available. They return to the pitch just four days after Parma. "We will make our assessments, I will evaluate without overthinking and I will put out the team that gives me the greatest guarantees, thinking exclusively about tomorrow's match. Ismajli is a fighter, a strong personality, but at Parma he had, above all, a great match on the ball, in how he lost his marker and built play, in how he brought energy to the build-up. He works day by day, and when you are dealing with a 30-31-year-old player who is already fully formed, it is not easy to make an impact. He has always trained with great ferocity." "Belghali as a right-back is better than me," Abate added with a smile, after the player had explained how working with a coach who played in his same position is helping him. "By nature he has always had an attacking inclination. Together we are working on body shape, on losing markers, but also on the defensive phase, on switching from a back five to a back four, on transitions. He is improving, he has always been very humble, he joined us without a pre-season training camp and I am gradually giving him more minutes. He kept his head down and worked hard during the break and must continue to grow, without settling." Then a comment on Aboukhlal, who featured at Parma in place of Belghali: "I have words of praise for Zakaria. His summer was not easy; he could also have left, but instead he stayed, he worked every day with humility, during the three-week break he worked like a true professional. I see him as a wide midfielder on the left, not on the right. He is adapting to playing as a wing-back, he is improving a lot defensively. I wanted to reward him when Belghali asked to come off, because it was right to reward him. I expect a lot from him and I hope his chance comes."
