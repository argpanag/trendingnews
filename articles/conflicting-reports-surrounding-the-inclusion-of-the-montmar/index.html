Conflicting reports surrounding the inclusion of the Montmartre climb on the final stage of the 2027 Tour de France route

Editor's note: This article has been updated to reflect subsequent reports published in the French press.

Question marks have emerged concerning the future of the punchy Montmartre circuit that has shaken up the final stage of the Tour de France in the past two years, with conflicting reports in the French press surrounding its inclusion on the 2027 Tour de France route.

The route for the 2027 Tour de France will be unveiled in Paris next Thursday, and the newspaper Le Progrès reported this Thursday morning that the Montmartre climb is set to be excluded, meaning a return to a traditional flat-track sprint finish on the Champs Elysées.

However, this was later contradicted by separate reports from RMC Sport and Le Parisien, with both outlets reporting that the Classics-flavoured finale will be back for a third year of high drama and entertainment.

In the past two years, the Côte de la Butte Montmartre has shaken up the traditional format of the Tour's finale, measuring 1.1 km at an average gradient of 5.9%, mostly on cobblestones, and followed by a tricky little descent.

It has created drama on both occasions, with Wout van Aert dropping Tadej Pogačar and soloing to victory in 2025, before Mathieu van der Poel hung on from a small-group attack – also involving Pogačar – to hold off the chasing pack in a nail-biting finale this year.

'Paris is now perfect' – Conclusions from the 2026 Tour de France

The organisers had already tweaked the format, extending the distance from the top of the climb to the finish line from six kilometres to just over 10km, which resulted in the closer contest between the attackers and the sprinters.

This appeared to strike a better balance between tradition and the modern desire for action and entertainment.

However, both editions were somewhat awkward, given that general classification timings have been neutralised on both occasions, with the GC 'frozen' and therefore finalised upon entry to the final circuit.

In 2025, the wet weather made the cobblestones treacherous, and the neutralisation was enforced to allow the likes of Pogačar to race openly without fear of losing their overall placing in the event of an accident. This year, the weather was dry, but the riders pushed for a similar GC neutralisation and the organisers eventually agreed.

The final stage of the Tour has for many years been processional, with riders drinking champagne and posing for photographs in the early kilometres before racing in central Paris, but any situation where proper GC racing could see positions – or even the yellow jersey – change hands, would throw that celebratory atmosphere up in the air.

The last bunch sprint on the Champs Elysées came in 2023, with the 2024 edition moved from Paris entirely due to the Olympics taking place in the French capital that summer, and the Tour ending with a time trial in Nice.

It remains to be seen what the organisers do with stage 21 in the long-run, but more will become clear when we see what is officially confirmed for the final day of the 2027 Tour next Thursday.
