James Copley: Sunderland finally pack passports after decades of near misses

Sunderland embark on a first European away trip in 53 years, a moment to savour after a journey shaped by fine margins.

For years fans went almost everywhere but Europe, through Championship miles and four League One seasons after falling from the Premier League, then falling again. Now the passports are out and this feels more than a date in the diary.

OneFootball Videos

At Hillsborough in May 2022, Sheffield Wednesday levelled the play-off semi-final and, for a spell, looked likeliest to settle it. Then a break, a low cross, and Patrick Roberts in stoppage time changed the direction.

Three years later at Wembley, Sheffield United led the Championship play-off final and thought they had another when Harrison Burrows’ strike went in, apparently for 2-0. VAR judged Vinicius Souza had interfered with Anthony Patterson from an offside position, so the goal was removed.

Sunderland survived, turned defeat into promotion and completed a climb from League One to the Premier League in three years. Europe followed with a seventh-placed first campaign.

No one can sensibly know what happens if such moments flip. Strategies and budgets matter, yet sometimes everything pivots on a toe to a cross or a line on a screen.

Supporters endured record-low Premier League points twice, consecutive relegations and weekly indignities, yet kept turning up. Now come new cities and foreign squares, names once known only from television or Football Manager, and memories to make. These could even be halcyon days, if noticed before they pass.
