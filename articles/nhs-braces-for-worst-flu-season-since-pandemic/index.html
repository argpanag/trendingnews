NHS braces for worst flu season since pandemic

TheTools · 11.10.2026 15:26 · source · permalink

The NHS is braced for the worst flu season since the Covid pandemic.

Hospital admission rates from flu are at the highest level for this time of the year since 2022, health chiefs have said.

Some 1.97 people per 100,000 were admitted to hospital for flu in the week to Oct 4, figures from the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) show.

While still considered to be low, it is the highest for the time of year since the pandemic, with health officials warning of the possibility of an early flu season.

Figures from September also reveal that the amount of flu circulating in public is higher than in the same period for the past four years.

Trends have been rising since August, with increases being driven by young adults, officials said.

Prof Frankie Swords, the NHS medical director, said there were indications of looming winter pressure for the health service.

She said: “From an NHS perspective, it is very early in the season, but the number of admissions for flu are the highest that I can remember seeing at this time of year, and it’s coincided with just the start of the expected increase in Covid cases in our hospitals.

“But this is coming on the back of the busiest summer we’ve ever had in our emergency departments. We have got very, very busy hospitals, so slightly ominous early signs of winter pressure.”

There are three types of flu virus and, while it is too early to know which one may dominate this winter, the current vaccine protects against all three. Early data show there is an equal mix of subtypes A(H3N2) and A(H1N1) in England, the UKHSA said.

Prof Swords revealed that 3.9 million people had had their flu vaccinations since the start of last month.

The NHS started vaccinating pregnant women, toddlers and school-age children from Sept 1, with adults aged 65 and over and other eligible adults offered the protection from Oct 1.

“In September, on average, 95 per cent of our occupancy was used every day – and that’s September,” said Prof Swords.

“So although the weather’s still nice, and perhaps people aren’t thinking too much about flu at the moment, we are acting early and I’d really encourage people to act early. Winter is coming, and the pressure is going to build and rise as temperatures fall.”

Analysis suggests that the vaccination programme in England last winter prevented around 100,000 hospital admissions and 7,000 deaths.

Elsewhere, the UKHSA report shows that Covid activity is also increasing, but remains at low levels. This includes the so-called “American strain”, or XFG, which is closely related to strains that have emerged in the UK for more than a year.

Dr Alex Allen, a consultant epidemiologist at UKHSA, said: “That’s been circulating in some form or another in the UK for over a year now, and it’s only in the last couple of months we’ve actually seen this increased activity.

“There’s nothing particularly in the evidence or data that suggest that this American strain or [that] XFG is more severe or more likely to cause severe illness.

“The current autumn vaccination programme has vaccines that are specifically targeted against the XFG strain. We’re expecting that to have a significant protective effect.”

Meanwhile, the Government has announced a £33.2m investment to help protect against future pandemic threats.

The programme will target diseases such as Ebola and plague, amid overseas outbreaks, with eight projects set to advance promising vaccines, trials and technologies.

The scheme, backed by UK Vaccine Network and delivered through Innovate UK, previously supported the development of the Oxford/AstraZeneca Covid vaccine.

Separate data published on Thursday revealed that the NHS waiting list had risen by 40,000 to 7.4 million.
