Ferrari replace Lewis Hamilton for Las Vegas GP first practice

TheTools · 17.11.2026 10:24 · source · permalink

Ferrari will field a rookie in Las Vegas first practice as part of the mandatory rule of needing to field a rookie four times.

Ferrari has confirmed that Dino Beganovic will replace Lewis Hamilton for first practice at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, fulfilling the final mandatory rookie session of Ferrari's 2026 campaign.

The Scuderia has now completed all four of its required rookie outings for the season. 

Beganovic has already deputised for Hamilton in Spain and for Charles Leclerc in both Austria and Brazil, meaning this Las Vegas appearance closes out Ferrari's obligations under the expanded rookie rule.

Formula 1's sporting regulations, broadened from 2025, require every team to hand FP1 duties to a rookie, defined as a driver with no more than two grand prix starts, across at least two sessions per car each season. 

That adds up to four mandatory FP1 appearances per team over the year.

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit is not typically viewed as a forgiving venue for rookie running. Unforgiving concrete walls, low initial grip and a track surface that evolves sharply from a green first practice to a heavily rubbered, cooler nocturnal session make set-up work unrepresentative, and teams have generally been reluctant to hand such a session to an inexperienced driver.

Ferrari, however, had little choice given the timing of its remaining commitment. Beganovic, a Ferrari Academy member since 2020, has built up FP1 mileage across the year and was recently confirmed to be stepping up into a full-time reserve and development role with the Scuderia from 2027.

He will not be the only reserve on duty in Las Vegas. Haas has also opted to field Ryo Hirakawa in FP1, standing in for Oliver Bearman as it too works through its rookie-running requirements before the season concludes.
