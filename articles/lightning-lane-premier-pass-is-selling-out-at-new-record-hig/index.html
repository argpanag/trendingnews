Lightning Lane Premier Pass Is Selling Out at New Record High Prices, But Is It Worth It?

TheTools · 22.11.2026 14:17 · source · permalink

Walt Disney World’s top tier Lightning Lane Premier Pass has sold out at all 4 parks for a nearly week-long stretch ahead of Thanksgiving, and Single Pass is also unavailable. This post covers the sellouts despite LLPP hitting new record high pricing for Lightning Lanes at Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom, along with whether the paid (and pricey) FastPass is worth the sky-high cost.

Let’s start by distinguishing the various paid FastPass products at Walt Disney World. Lightning Lane Premier Pass (LLPP) is a top tier of line-skipping, intended for the top ~2% of spenders and priced accordingly. By contrast, Lightning Lane Multi-Pass (LLMP) and Lightning Lane Single Pass (LLSP) are the mass-market products aimed at the majority guests and also priced accordingly.

There’s a lot more to know about Lightning Lane Premier Pass, Multi-Pass and Single Pass, all of which is beyond the scope of this post. If you don’t already know about these line-skipping services, we’d encourage you to read our Guide to Lightning Lanes at Walt Disney World instead of this post.

Lightning Lane Premier Pass continues to increase in popularity. Although it didn’t sellout for a ~7 month stretch between last April and October, between Fall Break and today, LLPP has sold out on several occasions. Most of these timeframes are exactly what you’d expect, and correspond with higher crowd holiday weeks at Walt Disney World.

Looking back further, Lightning Lane Premier Pass has sold out for several week-plus stretches during Presidents’ Day/Ski Week, Spring Break, and around Christmas and New Year’s Eve in the last year. This occurs much more frequently than sellouts of Lightning Lane Multi Pass, which seldom sells out (although it is currently sold out for EPCOT tomorrow, November 23, 2026).

Whereas Lightning Lane Multi-Pass has one sellout on the horizon, Premier Pass has several. And this is despite the top tier line-skipping service reaching new all-time highs on Monday. Let’s start with that…

Lightning Lane Premier Pass New Record High Prices

Back at the start of the fiscal year, Walt Disney World announced that Lightning Lane Premier Pass peak prices would increase by $50 on the highest-demand days across all four parks. Here’s a look at price ranges for LLPP prior to that:

  • Disney’s Animal Kingdom: $119 to $199 per person
  • EPCOT: $169 to $249 per person
  • Disney’s Hollywood Studios: $269 to $349 per person
  • Magic Kingdom: $329 to $449 per person

While we have not yet seen an increase of $50 per park, each does reach a new all-time record high on Monday, November 23, 2026:

  • Disney’s Animal Kingdom: $219 per person (+$20)
  • EPCOT: $269 per person (+$20)
  • Disney’s Hollywood Studios: $369 per person (+$20)
  • Magic Kingdom: $479 per person (+$30)

Given that Lightning Lane Premier Pass is now sold out across the board for November 23, 2026 (and beyond) and also since prices are typically higher in the weeks leading up to Christmas and New Year’s Eve, it’s probably a safe bet that Walt Disney World will max out those $50 across-the-board price hikes starting around December 21, 2026 and continuing through early 2027.

Speaking of sold out dates, here’s a rundown of those…

Lightning Lane Premier Pass Sold Out Dates

Walt Disney World is completely sold out of Lightning Lane Premier Pass from now through Thanksgiving (November 26, 2026). The first date that sold out across the board was actually yesterday, making it almost a full week that LLPP will be totally unavailable.

Black Friday only has one park with availability: Animal Kingdom. Meanwhile, Magic Kingdom and Disney’s Hollywood Studios are also sold out on Saturday, with only EPCOT and DAK as options for purchase. All four parks have availability starting Sunday, November 29, 2026. At least for now!

Along with this, Lightning Lane Single Pass is sold out across the board between now and Thanksgiving. However, we won’t fixate on this one for a couple of reasons: 1) Lightning Lane Single Pass sellouts are incredibly common, and 2) so are ride reservation refills, meaning something that’s “sold out” on November 26th as I write this might become available later today (doubtful) or once Thanksgiving actually rolls around (much more likely).

Is Lightning Lane Premier Pass Worth It Right Now?

One of the big things that prompted this post in the first place, since we don’t cover every single Lightning Lane sellout, is shifting trends in Thanksgiving crowds. Just yesterday, we updated our list of the Least-Bad of the Worst Weeks to Visit Walt Disney World in 2027. What that awkwardly-titled post highlights is the optimal school breaks and holiday weeks to do WDW for families and others who are confined to those calendars.

We noted that Thanksgiving week was once wall-to-wall 10/10 crowd levels. By contrast, last year’s crowd levels were downright moderate, with only a couple of days at or above 8/10. While we do not expect that to be the new normal going forward, crowds have not been 9/10 or 10/10 terrible over Thanksgiving in the last few years. It’s actually one of the better weeks to visit Walt Disney World from a qualitative perspective if you’re limited to school breaks!

Not only that, but the week after Thanksgiving (starting Sunday) is one of the least busy weeks of the holiday season, and our #1 favorite week of the entire year to visit Walt Disney World! The upside of heading to Walt Disney World after Thanksgiving is that you’ll also hit the parks during the peak of the Christmas season, with decorations up at all hotels and Epcot’s entertainment having started.

We’re still in the early innings, but this is playing out again for the week leading up to Thanksgiving 2026. Per thrill-data.com, yesterday had an average wait time of 28 minutes across all of Walt Disney World, which they deem a 2/10 crowd level. If I were the one assigning numerical scores, I’d peg that as a 4/10, but even so, we’re talking below-average crowds. Despite that, Lightning Lane Premier Pass was sold out everywhere.

Thus far today, average Walt Disney World wait times are 35 minutes, which thrill-data calls a 6/10. If this holds, it’ll be the busiest day at Walt Disney World since Fall Break. There’s currently only one ride with triple-digit waits, Slinky Dog Dash at 160 minutes, but 16 are at an hour or more. This includes several we don’t normally see in that territory on truly slow days, and really, those are the biggest difference-maker between packed days and more moderate ones.

Based on historical wait times data, Monday and Tuesday will almost certainly be busier than today, and Wednesday probably will as well. As someone who is firmly not in the target audience for Lightning Lane Premier Pass, it’s difficult for me to assess when is and isn’t worth it, but assuming LLPP ever is, November 23-25, 2026 probably qualifies. At least at Magic Kingdom and Disney’s Hollywood Studios.

As for the other dates, probably not. If past precedent is any indication, Thanksgiving itself and Friday won’t be as bad as the dates leading up to it. Those days are back in above-average (but not peak) crowd territory. And crowd levels should drop like a rock starting on Saturday, with the decline continuing on Sunday.

It’s hard to recommend Lightning Lane Premier Pass anywhere on either Saturday or Sunday. And we never recommend LLPP at Animal Kingdom, including peak weeks, unless you’re otherwise going to throw that money on the ground and walk away from it.

Although we’re not fans of Lightning Lane Premier Pass anywhere, it’s at least a better option at Magic Kingdom and Hollywood Studios on dates with 7/10 or above crowd levels, even at $150 to $250 more. I would personally purchase Jollywood Nights or Mickey’s Very Merry Christmas Party tickets as opposed to LLPP for those parks, but to each their own. No advice is one-size-fits-all, and those hard ticket events are sold out at this point, anyway.

When Is Lightning Lane Premier Pass Actually Worth It?

Looking forward, as Walt Disney World gears up for the busiest 3-week stretch of the entire year from just before Christmas until early January 2027, prices for Lightning Lane Premier Pass will definitely reach record highs. Whether that means returning to these levels or maxing out that full $50 increase remains to be seen, but I’d bet on the latter.

In all likelihood, peak pricing will kick in from December 21, 2026 through January 2, 2027. On these dates, you’ll pay at least $219 at Animal Kingdom, $269 at EPCOT, $369 at Hollywood Studios, and $479 at Magic Kingdom. Our guess is you’ll actually pay $249, $299, $399, and $499. Per person and plus tax, of course.

These are the most expensive dates of the year for Lightning Lane Premier Pass, but for good reason! This stretch is far and away the time of year when LLPP is most “worth it.” Objectively speaking, LLPP makes more sense at those higher prices than it does at the lower rates on Sunday, November 29, 2026.

This is especially true from December 28-31, which will likely see the highest wait times by a wide margin of 2026. During these 3 days, Lightning Lane Premier Pass offers good value everywhere (except for Animal Kingdom).

If it were a thing we’d buy in the first place, we would hypothetically be more inclined to buy Lightning Lane Premier Pass for $499 on the Monday or Tuesday before New Year’s Eve than during Spring Break, when it might cost $399 to $449.

Even at its peak pricing, LLPP offers outsized bang for buck during the final few days of the year. If you’re ever going to splurge on it, that is the time. Lightning Lane Multi-Pass subsequent selections won’t be a sufficient alternative, and there are no hard ticket events to do instead. It’s Lightning Lane Premier Pass or bust!

Premier Pass Outside Peak Season

As for the rest of the year, the guests who should purchase Lightning Lane Premier Pass are primarily those for whom money is no object. Guests for whom time is their #1 resource, and who are willing to spend whatever it takes to buy themselves efficiency. In other words, the people who wouldn’t read over 1,000 words into a post like this.

Guests who are tremendously technology averse, want zero backtracking, to remove all friction, and have the resources to not care about cost. Parties that want a VIP experience, but either can’t make the math work on a VIP tour or don’t want to be accompanied by a stranger.

Same goes for guests of the Four Seasons Orlando or another luxury hotel who don’t have access to Extended Evening Hours or other on-site perks. Ditto people who didn’t plan ahead and are willing to buy their way out of lines at any cost. Again, guests who wouldn’t be reading a blog like this for money-saving tips & tricks.

Honestly, you should already know whether this is a product for you or not based on the description and price points. We’d reiterate our above analysis that it’s for the top 2% of spenders. If LLPP is a good fit, more power to you! It’s not for us, but I don’t begrudge others for buying it–different people understandably have different priorities, budgets, etc.

Who is Lightning Lane Premier Pass not right for and who should skip it? Pretty much everyone else. The vast majority of the year, LLPP is not a good splurge. If purchasing it is going to come at the expense of something–anything–else during your trip, you should absolutely spend the money on that other thing.

Unsurprisingly, the dates when Lightning Lane Premier Pass is cheapest are the dates when it’s least useful. Basically the opposite of the Christmas to New Year’s peak pricing recommendation above. Whereas LLPP is arguably “worth it” at $499 the Monday before NYE, it’s never worth it for the lowest price during the off-season. For that matter, I also would never buy LLPP during party-shortened days at Magic Kingdom, including in the lead-up to Thanksgiving and Christmas.

On days that are less busy, there’s also abundant availability for Lightning Lane Multi-Pass and Single Pass. Meaning that whenever crowds are sufficiently low to justify Walt Disney World selling LLPP at its lowest rates, you can scoop up most Lightning Lanes without too much trouble. This includes subsequent same-day selections.

In particular, Lightning Lane Premier Pass at Animal Kingdom is never worth it. Multi-Pass barely makes sense there for most of the year, but at least is well worth it around peak season dates. If given the option to choose Premier Pass or Multi-Pass plus Single Pass at Animal Kingdom without having to pay for either, I’d pick the latter most of the time. (Outside of Christmas, New Year’s, and Easter.)

The reason for that is simple: Multi-Pass includes Park Hopping at no additional charge, whereas Premier Pass doesn’t. Given that, I’d rather roll the dice on subsequent selections in a second park than be “guaranteed” a handful of pointless Lightning Lanes at DAK that offer only illusory value.

Starting the day at DAK with Multi-Pass, I’m pretty sure I could grab a couple of worthwhile Lightning Lanes at EPCOT in the afternoon and evening–perhaps all of them. At minimum, that would relieve the burden (and backtracking) of trying to score subsequent selections via Multi-Pass on my dedicated EPCOT day. (For what it’s worth, I generally wouldn’t buy LLMP at EPCOT, period, and am only doing so here for this thought exercise.)

Nevertheless, we’ve found that some planners are still on the fence about Lightning Lane Premier Pass. This is largely due to fear of missing out (FOMO) and fear, uncertainty and doubt (FUD), which are two of my favorite “f-acronyms.” We’ve ranted elsewhere at length about the WDW FOMO Machine.

It’s easy to fall into the FOMO/FUD traps, and drop an extra $5,000 or more on extras and upcharges to “guarantee” a great trip to Walt Disney World. You don’t want to miss out on something that’s “essential” to a great vacation, so you err on the side of caution and buy a bunch of upcharges that don’t actually guarantee that at all.

On the FUD side, it’s also likely that sellouts beget more sellouts, especially since people are purchasing Premier Pass before their trips, and they have a chance to feel crowds firsthand. This is one of the advantages for Disney in advance sales of Lightning Lanes, more generally.

Again, Lightning Lane Premier Pass does offer value to some guests–those for whom money is no object, grandparents not wanting to use phones or backtrack at all and having lots of disposable income, etc. I’m not arguing that. If you’re one of those demos, more power to you. But you should already know that one way or the other.

There are a lot of other people who are on the fence, debating which splurges are worth the money at Walt Disney World or having FOMO/FUD about line-skipping. If you’re in one of those camps and are contemplating whether it’s worth it to purchase Lightning Lane Premier Pass at its lowest price points, I can almost assure you that it is not.

It’s not like we’re simply cheapskates always looking to do WDW on a dime. We are perfectly willing to pay premium prices for premium products and experiences (hence being Disney fans in the first place). For a lengthy list of recommended upcharges, see Best Bang-for-Buck Splurges at Walt Disney World. It’s just that LLPP isn’t on that list, or anywhere even close to it. The bottom line is that Lightning Lane Premier Pass doesn’t make sense for ~98% of guests at all and about that much of the time, period.
