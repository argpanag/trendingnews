South West grandad dies after flight turbulence caused cardiac arrest

TheTools · 23.09.2026 17:18 · source · permalink

A 73-year-old grandfather from the south west died after a Singapore Airlines flight plunged through terrifying turbulence, an inquest has heard.

Geoff Kitchen, from Thornbury in south Gloucestershire, was travelling with his wife Linda on a dream six-week trip when their Boeing 777 was hit by severe turbulence over Myanmar on May 21, 2024. The retired insurance professional suffered a fatal cardiac arrest during the mid-air ordeal

Avon Coroners' Court heard Mr Kitchen was sitting next to his wife on the Singapore Airlines flight when the aircraft suddenly dropped, leaving passengers injured and the cabin in chaos, according to the Daily Star.

The plane was forced to make an emergency diversion to Bangkok, where he was pronounced dead. His wife was seriously injured and taken to hospital alongside dozens of other passengers. Dr Peter Harrowing, assistant coroner for Avon, gave a narrative conclusion at Wednesday's inquest into Mr Kitchen's death.

"On the morning of 21 May 2024, Geoff Kitchen, who had a history of coronary artery disease, was a passenger on a commercial flight from London to Singapore," Dr Harrowing said. "It encountered severe turbulence. Mr Kitchen was thrown from his seat and landed on an adjacent passenger.

"He was given cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). The aircraft was diverted and landed at an airport in Bangkok, where Mr Kitchen was confirmed deceased. Geoff Kitchen died of cardiac failure as a result of the aircraft he was a passenger on encountering severe turbulence."

Mr Kitchen, a retired insurance professional, and his wife, a former careers adviser, had set off from Heathrow airport for a six-week trip to Singapore, Indonesia and Australia.

In a statement read to the inquest, Mrs Kitchen, aged 74, described how she had been sitting in her assigned seat with her seatbelt fastened when the incident took place.

She said that her husband had been listening to a podcast when she began to feel turbulence, followed by a "huge drop or lunge" and she felt "incredible pain".

"I realised my husband was pressing me to the side and that's what caused the injury to my back," she told the hearing. "I carried on screaming. Eventually, two passengers lifted my husband off me. He was unconscious. I was in a lot of pain."

Mrs Kitchen described how she was "bleeding profusely" from a head wound and was helped across the aisle by other passengers. "My husband was moved to the front of the section and placed on the floor," she said.

"Many people helped to give my husband CPR. No-one told me that my husband had died. After landing, I was placed on a red stretcher."

Mrs Kitchen was not informed of her husband's death until two days afterwards. She remained in hospital for 10 days before being airlifted back to Bristol, where she spent a further week in hospital.

An initial post-mortem examination carried out in Thailand concluded that Mr Kitchen had died from congestive heart failure. The inquest heard that he had undergone a procedure to fit stents to his right coronary artery in April 2019.

However, a review conducted in October of that year established he had returned to "normal exercise capacity", according to a statement from his GP.

Dr Edward Sheffield, a consultant pathologist, conducted a post-mortem examination on Mr Kitchen's body in Bristol. He recorded the cause of death as cardiac failure resulting from coronary artery atheroma and the physiological effects of aeroplane turbulence.

A report into the incident, which Dr Harrowing partially read aloud, revealed that 56 passengers had sustained serious injuries, 23 had suffered minor injuries, and one individual, Mr Kitchen, had lost his life.

"The aircraft was cruising at 37,000ft when it was approaching the west coast of south-western Myanmar," Dr Harrowing read. "At 7.49am, the aircraft flew past a waypoint when the aircraft experienced severe turbulence. By 7.53am, the aircraft had stabilised."

The crew reported the severe turbulence, with a Mayday call issued at 8.01am alongside a request to divert to Bangkok. At 8.45am, the aircraft touched down at Suvarnabhumi Airport, where medical personnel were waiting.

Mr Kitchen was subsequently pronounced dead.

The coroner concluded: "I am satisfied that it was the physiological effect of the aircraft turbulence which precipitated the cardiac arrest and ultimately Geoff's demise."

Mrs Kitchen – who suffered spinal fractures during the incident – has appointed solicitors at Irwin Mitchell, which has launched High Court proceedings for compensation against Singapore Airlines. In a statement released through the law firm, she described how "absolutely devastating" her husband's death had been for her, their two children and four grandchildren.

"Losing Geoff was a crushing blow to the whole family as we all thought he would be around to see his grandchildren grow up," she said. "We had a happy and fulfilled marriage and now I've lost my soulmate."

Anthe Korelidou, the specialist aviation solicitor at Irwin Mitchell, described the ordeal endured by Mrs Kitchen and fellow passengers throughout the flight as "extremely traumatic".

"While it's more than two years since Geoff's death, Linda and the rest of her family continue to have a number of questions and concerns about what happened and if more could have been done to prevent their injuries," she said.

"All Linda wants is for the most thorough investigations to be conducted. The issuing of High Court proceedings is a major milestone in being able to provide Geoff's loved ones with the answers they deserve."
