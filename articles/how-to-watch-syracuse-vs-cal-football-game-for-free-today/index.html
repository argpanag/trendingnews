How do I watch Cal vs. Syracuse for free?

Here three storylines to watch for today’s game:

1. Steve Angeli has the Orange offense looking strong

Quarterback Steve Angeli’s start Saturday was his first since he tore his Achilles four games into the 2025 season. He completed 20 of 29 passes for 267 yards and three touchdowns in the 66-3 win. While it was against an FCS opponent in UNH, the offense looked promising compared to the way the Orange’s offense deteriorated after Angeli’s injury.

2. A good problem to have at running back

Freshman running back Shavane Anderson Jr. capitalized on his starting opportunity against UNH, running for 102 yards and three touchdowns on 10 carries. He became the first true freshman ACC running back to have 100 rushing yards and three touchdowns in a debut in at least 20 seasons.

Running backs Ju’Juan Johnson and Ahmad Miller both missed the first game, but will be active Saturday. After Anderson’s record-breaking debut, the Orange have no choice but to mix him in with the veterans going forward.

3. Syracuse’s wide receiver room gets closer to full strength

The offense looked great in the season opener, and the Orange should continue adding reinforcements at wide receiver in the weeks to come.

Umari Hatcher shined in the opener, catching eight passes for 147 yards and two touchdowns. But competition is going to get much tougher, and Angeli will need more options he can rely on consistently.

Fifth-year receiver Justus-Ross Simmons returns to the lineup this week. He had five touchdowns in the four games Angeli played last season (Rickie Collins threw him one of those), and should be a big-play threat almost immediately.

Freshman Calvin Russell III could be back “in a few weeks” according to Syracuse coach Fran Brown. The five-star recruit tore his Achilles in March, but has recovered impressively fast. The 6-foot-5 wideout adds a size and athleticism element to Syracuse’s offense that it does not have.
