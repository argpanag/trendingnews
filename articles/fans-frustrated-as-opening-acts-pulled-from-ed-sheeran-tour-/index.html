Fans frustrated as opening acts pulled from Ed Sheeran tour amid controversy

TheTools · 19.09.2026 02:21 · source · permalink

PHILADELPHIA (WPVI) -- Lincoln Financial Field will be the first stop on Ed Sheeran's concert tour since the controversy started surrounding rapper Macklemore's pro-Palestine remarks.

With less than 24 hours until the show on Saturday, many fans are trying to sell their tickets due to the concert changes.

Some are in support of the rapper, while others are concerned about safety due to planned protests.

According to TicketData.com, ticket prices dropped more than 50% this week.

Nicole Tedesco of Pittston said she feels "disappointed."

"You want the whole experience," she said.

The concert will proceed without any opening acts, and the start time has been moved from 5:30 p.m. to 8 p.m. Tedesco said she had been looking forward to seeing Macklemore and Lukas Graham.

The changes follow Macklemore's "Free Palestine" speech while opening for Sheeran at MetLife Stadium.

Soon after, Robert Kraft - owner of Gillette Stadium - banned the rapper from his venue. Six other stadium owners followed, including Lincoln Financial Field.

Sheeran said in a statement that the tour promoter decided to remove Macklemore.

He added, "I am appalled by the conflict between Israel and Palestine, I am not complicit. I have my personal views on this devastating conflict."

Other opening acts dropped out in solidarity with Macklemore.

RELATED: Protest planned outside Ed Sheeran concert at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia

Pro-Palestine groups say they plan to protest outside the stadium.

That has raised concerns for fans like Caitlin Smith of Hatboro, who planned to attend with her husband. Both use power wheelchairs.

"The Ed Sheeran song 'Perfect' was our wedding song," Smith said. "My ultimate dream would have been for us both to meet him, but going to this concert was essentially the next best thing. Because of everything being so unsure, it kinda sucks."

Despite Philadelphia police saying they will adjust staffing to maintain safety, Smith said she plans to stay home.

"With us both being in chairs trying to get into the city, we don't want to take that risk," she said.

Refunds have also become a major concern.

Smith said she was unable to get her money back from StubHub, though some fans have received refunds through Ticketmaster.

Ticketmaster told Action News the refund policy is up to event organizers, Messina Touring Group.

Action News reached out to them and the Linc regarding their policy, but haven't heard back.
