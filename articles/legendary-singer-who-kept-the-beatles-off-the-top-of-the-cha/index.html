'Legendary' singer who kept The Beatles off the top of the charts dies at age 76

TheTools · 02.11.2026 18:06 · source · permalink

Pop singer Steve Ellis, frontman of 1960s group Love Affair, has died aged 76.

Ellis, who lived in Portslade and made Brighton his adopted home town, died on the evening of October 30 following complications from a chronic illness.

A family statement confirmed the musician passed away peacefully in the presence of his loved ones.

The East End-born singer shot to national fame in 1968 at the age of 16 when Love Affair's cover of Everlasting Love topped the UK singles chart.

The track famously kept The Beatles off the number one spot in 1968, keeping the Fab Four classic Hello, Goodbye from the top of the UK chart.

Steve Ellis, front and centre in the red shirt, with his Love Affair bandmates (Alamy/PA)

Rock musician Paul Weller led tributes to his long-time friend, sharing: "Our dear mate Stevie Ellis passed on last week.

"God bless ya Stevie - safe travels. Everlasting love for sure."

Blues musician Norman Beaker also paid tribute, describing Ellis as "a legend with the mod movement" and "a great character" who possessed "a great voice".

Organisers at Kent-based BCM Festivals, where Ellis appeared in his later touring years, said he was a "familiar and much-loved face" whose music "provided the soundtrack to so many memories".

Ellis formed his first band Soul Survivors at the age of 15 before forming Love Affair alongside Rex Brayley, Mick Jackson, Lynton Guest and Maurice Bacon.

The group scored a string of chart hits including Rainbow Valley, A Day Without Love, and Bringing On Back The Good Times before Ellis left in 1969 to pursue a solo career and front bands including Ellis and Widowmaker.

Steve Ellis performing in 2022 (Image: Wikimedia)

Despite his London upbringing, Ellis settled in Sussex for decades, embracing coastal life and becoming a prominent figure in the area's music heritage.

In 2003, he returned to the stage at the Concorde 2 in Brighton to launch his solo album A Sort Of Innocence after years away from touring.

Dressed in jeans and a checked shirt as if he had stepped straight from a North Laine boutique, he delighted a packed crowd with two renditions of his signature track.

Sipping a cup of tea backstage after the Concorde 2 show, he told The Argus: "You've got to do it haven't you? Everyone wants to hear it."

The following year, The Argus spoke to Ellis at his home in Drove Crescent, Portslade, after jazz artist Jamie Cullum recorded a fresh version of Everlasting Love for the movie Bridget Jones: The Edge of Reason.

Ellis was gracious about the cover, saying: "People are trying to get me to slate him and I am not going to do it. I have heard the song and it is all right."

He added: "Good luck to him. He is a young man starting out and he has talent."

Love Affair pictured in the Netherlands in 1968, with Steve Ellis on the left (Image: Wikimedia)

While recording his own solo album, Ellis included a reimagined acoustic version of the song with cellos, alongside a live recording performed alongside Paul Weller at a concert in Crawley.

He insisted he had no desire to reissue the hit as a commercial single, explaining: "I would not want to reissue the same track again and again. I make music, that's what I do."

He told The Argus: "When that happens it just looks like you have got a history of one song in your life and I like to think my music is a bit more than that."

His lasting contribution to Sussex culture was permanently cemented in 2018 when he was inducted onto the Brighton Music Walk of Fame on Brighton Palace Pier.

Producer David Courtney included Ellis among 30 celebrated musicians tied to the city, honouring him as a "hit-maker and soul singer" alongside The Who, The Cure and Leo Sayer.

Fellow musicians and fans from across the county and beyond have continued to share memories online following his passing.
