South Africa stamp authority with the bat in Centurion | ICC World Test Championship | ICC

TheTools · 24.11.2026 16:57 · source · permalink

Marques Ackerman stood out for South Africa with a fiery knock of 150, as the Proteas extended their advantage on the second day of the Centurion Test.

South Africa extended their lead on Day 2 of the Centurion Test courtesy of a stellar batting effort led by Marques Ackerman, who smashed a 150 off merely 138 balls. Wiaan Mulder (100) and Tony de Zorzi (66) along with Marco Jansen (67*) played supporting roles.

For Ackerman, this was the second consecutive hundred for him in the Test format after a 157 in the first innings of the previous game. The Test is a part of the ICC World Test Championship, where South Africa lead the standings.

De Zorzi paid credit to the innings of Ackerman at stumps on Tuesday, after they combined for a partnership of 74 for the third wicket at SuperSport Park.

"Ackers is very free-flowing and he's in a rich vein of form," de Zorzi noted.

"He's batting incredibly well. He's taking the game forward, he's being himself and he's putting the team in winning positions."

"It's probably similar to batting with Aiden (Markram). Everyone's obviously fixated on them, rightly so. So it's easy. Get them on strike. You just have to make sure you don't get sucked in trying to match them."

Pacer Shoriful Islam picked up two wickets for Bangladesh on the second day, but he rued the efforts of his fellow bowlers as the Proteas increased their lead to 403 runs at the end of the day.

"South Africa's attack bowled really well, but we couldn't bowl in a disciplined way," Shoriful said. 

"I admit that we didn't bowl well enough. I think we bowled boundary balls at the start and end of overs. We gave away some easy boundaries. Their batters took advantage of those (deliveries). We couldn't bowl at a disciplined line and length, especially where there was movement.

"Maybe we bowled too wide, made them play the cut shot more. We bowled more at a back of length. We have to plan how we can do better next time. We couldn't bowl as well we did in the previous matches. I think it is upsetting that almost everyone made the same errors. We have to improve collectively."

Beginning the day on 88/1, just three runs behind the Bangladesh total of 91, the Proteas pressed on the advantage, with Ackerman getting to his fifty in the second over of the day.

After a few watchful overs, Ackerman picked pace and unleashed a flurry of boundaries. Despite losing partner Zubayr Hamza (27) at 145, Ackerman kept at it and got to his hundred off 111 balls. 

With de Zorzi also settled in, Najmul Hossain Shanto brought himself in to change up the proceedings. But Ackerman tore into the part-timer, smashing 20 runs off his over. He attacked Shoriful Islam too and reached his 150 off merely 137 balls. However, he fell off the very next ball to the same bowler, being trapped leg before just when he looked set for a big total.

Bangladesh had more success with wickets of Matthew Breetzke (20) and Ryan Rickelton (20) over the coming overs, but Wiaan Mulder joined de Zorzi and brought the initiatve back to South Africa.

It was Mulder who drove the initiative for the remainder of the day with support from de Zorzi (66) and then Marco Jansen, as South Africa's lead crossed 400 on Day 2 itself. 

Mulder scored his fifth Test ton, but fell in the very last over of the day.

South Africa have a significant advantage in the Test, and Bangladesh would need to be at their very best to get back in the game.

Action resumes on the Day 3 of the Test on 25 November.
