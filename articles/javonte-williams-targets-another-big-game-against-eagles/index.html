Javonte Williams Targets Another Big Game Against Eagles

TheTools · 26.11.2026 21:51 · source · permalink

Dallas Cowboys running back Javonte Williams will be a central part of the offense when the team hosts the Philadelphia Eagles at AT&T Stadium on Thanksgiving. His previous performance against Philadelphia, highlighted by 155 rushing yards and a touchdown, gives Dallas reason to rely heavily on its ground attack again.

The Cowboys enter the matchup at 5-6 following a 51-30 victory over Tennessee. Williams has recorded 10 rushing touchdowns, ranking fourth among running backs according to Pro Football Focus (PFF). Philadelphia arrives with a 3-7 record and a weakened linebacker group.

Williams dominated the previous meeting on Oct. 26, carrying the ball 25 times for 155 yards in Dallas' 38-21 victory. He averaged 6.2 yards per carry, considerably better than his 3.7-yard average across his other 10 appearances, during which he accumulated 604 yards on 163 attempts.

Dallas finished that October contest with 38 rushing attempts for 204 yards. Head coach Brian Schottenheimer had targeted at least 25 carries for the running game, and Williams acknowledged the importance of delivering that performance.

"It was good to give [head coach Brian Schottenheimer] the confidence to keep calling the runs," Williams said after his career day.

Philadelphia's defensive tendencies could provide another opportunity. According to PFF, the Eagles use light defensive boxes at the third-highest rate in the league. Williams also ranks third among running backs in the difference between his yards per carry against light and heavy defensive fronts.

The Eagles surrender an average of 120.8 rushing yards per game. Their situation becomes more challenging without linebacker Zack Baun, who leads the team with 65 tackles and is on injured reserve because of a back injury.

Philadelphia also faces the absence of Arnold Ebiketie, who is sidelined by an oblique injury. Second-year linebacker Jihaad Campbell, who has recorded 56 tackles, will take on additional responsibilities in the defensive running game.

Defensive tackle Jalen Carter remains Philadelphia's most significant obstacle for Dallas. He has recorded five sacks, seven tackles for loss and four forced fumbles this season. However, Carter managed only two tackles and no tackles for loss in the October meeting.

Williams has forced 24 missed tackles this season, according to PFF, although ball security remains a concern. The running back has also recorded three fumbles under the same statistical assessment.

Dallas could also increase Williams' involvement in the passing game. Tight end Jake Ferguson is unavailable because of a knee injury, leaving the Cowboys without one of their regular short-range receiving options.

Against Tennessee, quarterback Dak Prescott directed 22 of his 33 targets toward wide receivers CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens. Williams offers another option underneath, having caught 28 of 30 targets without a drop this season.

PFF gives Williams a receiving grade of 77.8, the sixth-highest among running backs. His receiving production makes three or four catches a reasonable expectation against Philadelphia, particularly if defensive pressure forces Prescott to look for shorter passing options.

Williams has also scored touchdowns in three of his last five games, including a one-yard rushing touchdown against Tennessee. Philadelphia has conceded 10 rushing touchdowns this season, creating another opportunity for the Cowboys running back near the goal line.

The Eagles will also be without quarterback Jalen Hurts, who is injured. Joe Milton is expected to start as Philadelphia attempts to improve an offense averaging 16.7 points per game.

Dallas will look to Williams to maintain its offensive momentum after scoring 51 points against Tennessee. His previous success against Philadelphia, combined with the Eagles' defensive absences, makes him an important component of the Cowboys' Thanksgiving game plan.
