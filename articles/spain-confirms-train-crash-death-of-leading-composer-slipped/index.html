Spain confirms train crash death of leading composer - Slippedisc

TheTools · 31.10.2026 17:08 · source · permalink

norman lebrecht

October 31, 2026

The Spanish authorities have identified the remains of composer María Luisa Manchado Torres in last week’s train disaster. Maria, who was 70, was on her way to attend a university symposium about her works.

Scherzo magazine writes:

María Luisa Manchado Torres, our dear Marisa Manchado, was born in Madrid on July 7, 1956. She trained primarily in her hometown as a composer and pianist, but also as a musicologist, holding a doctorate in that field from the Complutense University. In her compositional training, she often cited Carmelo Bernaola, Antón García Abril, and Luis de Pablo, as well as international figures such as Olivier Messiaen and Brian Ferneyhough. Perhaps the most valuable lesson she learned from them was not to imitate them, but to develop her own musical language based on her personal creative needs. And that is precisely what she did.

Marisa not only developed an interesting personal body of work, but also championed music in every sphere. She was a teacher and for many years served as deputy director of the Teresa Berganza Conservatory. She also participated in the founding of the Madrid Association of Composers, of which she was the first president. And she took part in the ministerial management of music when she was deputy director general of Music and Dance at the INAEM (National Institute of Performing Arts and Music) between 2007 and 2008, during Juan Carlos Marset’s tenure as director of the organization. Beyond all this, her advocacy for the creative role of women composers was reflected in numerous activities and publications.

As a composer, Marisa wrote for all kinds of ensembles and was drawn to opera from a very early age. In fact, in that almost mythical series of operas that premiered at the Sala Olimpia (now the Valle-Inclán Theatre), thanks to the support given to the CDMC by another unforgettable absentee, the then director of the National Center for New Scenic Trends, Guillermo Heras, she was the one who premiered the last opera before the Ministry suppressed the initiative without further explanation. This was El cristal de agua fría (The Glass of Cold Water ), with a libretto by Rosa Montero, which premiered in 1994. A second, smaller-scale opera, Escenas de la vida cotidiana (Scenes from Everyday Life ), premiered at the Teatro de la Abadía in 1997. Then came a long period of work on a libretto by Amelia Valcárcel based on La Regenta by Leopoldo Alas, culminating in the premiere of the opera of the same title, produced by the Teatro Real and the Teatro Español at the Naves del Español in Matadero in 2023. La Regenta not only attracted attention but was also one of the key reasons she was awarded the National Music Prize for Composition in 2024.
