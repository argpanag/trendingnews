On the same day Green Bay Packers' running back Josh Jacobs pleaded no contest to two misdemeanor charges against him, Judge Marc Hammer signed an order temporarily sealing the evidence in the case.

Jacobs took a plea deal Thursday for battery and criminal damage to property. The judge sentenced him to a $1,000 fine on the battery charge. Jacobs entered a deferred judgement for the criminal damage to property charge. The court will dismiss that charge after a year if he does the following:

  • Do no further criminal acts
  • Avoid contacting the victim
  • Submit to CJS for supervision for the next 12 months
  • Complete a course for battery
  • Pay restitution to the victim

In an order signed and dated the same day, one of Jacobs' defense attorneys, Clarence Duhac III, filed a motion to seal the discovery material in the case and the judge signed off on it.

Discovery material, according to the order, may include "...police reports, supplemental reports, dispatch and communications records, recordings, photographs, video recordings, witness statements, investigative notes, forensic materials, medical or treatment-related information, digital evidence, and other documents or media..."

"The Court finds that public disclosure of the Discovery Material would threaten or substantially impair Defendant’s and [the victim's] privacy, dignity, personal security, emotional wellbeing, or ability to participate safely in the criminal-justice process," the order reads. "This protection is not to impede any legitimate law-enforcement function, criminal prosecution judicial proceeding, or disclosure otherwise required by law, but to prevent unnecessary public dissemination of information that identifies [the victim], reveals intimate or sensitive details of [the victim] or the Defendant, or creates a material risk of harm."

"This Order is granted temporarily, pending a further Motion to be filed by [the victim] and/or Defendant," it goes on to say. It does not state a timeframe on how long the temporary seal lasts.

12 News Investigates filed several records requests for documents and video in the case.

Prosecutors relied on video from Jacobs' residence, saying in the criminal complaint it corroborates "...the relevant portions..." of the victim's statement.

Jacobs is accused of throwing his girlfriend to the ground and damaging her phone on May 23 in an incident at his home.

The complaint says Jacobs then took the woman's cellphone and refused to give it back. The two struggled over the phone before leaving the home in separate vehicles.

The woman later followed Jacobs because he still had her phone, and the two eventually returned to the house, according to the criminal complaint.

Prosecutors say the confrontation escalated when the woman later tried to leave again through the garage. Jacobs is accused of grabbing her and throwing her to the ground, causing her to hit her head.

Police later noted a bump on her head and redness on her face and neck, the complaint states.

Investigators also recovered home surveillance video that prosecutors say showed Jacobs taking the phone from the woman while she was on the garage floor and later appearing to damage it.

WISN 12 News has reached out Jacobs' defense team for comment regarding the motionsealing the evidence. Jacobs' remains on the commissioner's exempt list, meaning he cannot practice or play. An NFL spokesperson said they cannot comment on an ongoing investigation. Brown County District Attorney David Lasee says the state is not a party to any motions regarding the video or evidence, so he did not have information about the motion to seal.
