It's the End of An Era for Star Wars Movies After 50 Years

TheTools · 13.10.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

The next Star Wars movie will be hitting theaters 50 years after the beloved sci-fi franchise kicked off, but this release will also bring about huge changes that will mark the end of an era for the franchise. Created by George Lucas, the Star Wars franchise began in 1977 with the release of Star Wars: A New Hope. Audiences have since been immersed in the battle between the good Jedi and the evil Sith, typically from the perspective of Luke Skywalker and his found family.

49 years after the franchise began, nine mainline movies, four standalone movies, seven live-action television series, and 12 animated series have been released, and all of them have had ties to the Skywalker Saga. Star Wars has become one of the most expansive and popular media franchises in history, but the next movie will establish a new status quo for the long-running franchise, as it will separate itself from the Skywalker Saga.

Star Wars: Starfighter Marks 50 Years of the Star Wars Franchise

Following in the footsteps of Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu, which became the lowest-grossing Star Wars movie ever in May 2026, Star Wars: Starfighter will be released as the next adventure in the franchise in May 2027, marking 50 years of the iconic sci-fi franchise. Ryan Gosling and the young Flynn Gray will be teaming up in Star Wars: Starfighter, embarking on a mission completely separated from the Skywalker Saga—the first time for any Star Wars movie or series.

Directed by Shawn Levy and written by Your Friends & Neighbors' Jonathan Tropper, Star Wars: Starfighter will follow starfighter pilot Kade Auberon (Ryan Gosling), who is tasked with protecting a mysterious young boy (Flynn Gray), escorting him across a lawless, fractured galaxy five years after the fall of the First Order and the death of the resurrected Emperor Palpatine (Ian McDiarmid) in 2019's Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker. This will forge a story never before seen in the Star Wars franchise.

As well as Ryan Gosling and young star Flynn Gray, Star Wars: Starfighter features an impressive cast that boasts former Doctor Who star Matt Smith, horror icon Mia Goth, Lanterns' leading man Aaron Pierre, the Academy Award-nominated Amy Adams, and more. Coming fresh off hit roles in Project Hail Mary and Wednesday, however, Gosling and Gray's chemistry will be at the forefront of Star Wars: Starfighter, while Goth and Smith are expected to portray the movie's sinister antagonists.

During 2026's San Diego Comic-Con, Marvel boss Kevin Feige, after announcing Ryan Gosling as the new face of Ghost Rider, confirmed that he had seen Star Wars: Starfighter, and stated that "it's awesome." This urged him to hire Starfighter director Shawn Levy to lead the MCU's upcoming Ghost Rider movie, marking a reunion between Levy and Gosling in another major Disney property. As an entity in and of itself, Star Wars: Starfighter is one of the most exciting movies in the long-running franchise's history.

Star Wars: Starfighter Will Be the Franchise's First Non-Skywalker Story

Ever since the Star Wars franchise kicked off in 1977, the story has centered primarily on the adventures of Luke Skywalker (Mark Hamill) and the unlikely heroes he surrounds himself with. The original Star Wars trilogy explored Skywalker, Han Solo (Harrison Ford), and Princess Leia Organa's (Carrie Fisher) involvement with the Rebel Alliance and efforts to destroy the Galactic Empire's greatest weapon, the Death Star, and its leader, Darth Vader (David Prowse and James Earl Jones), and this informed subsequent stories.

The prequel trilogy explored Anakin Skywalker's (Jake Lloyd and Hayden Christensen) childhood, adolescence, and origins as Darth Vader, while the sequel trilogy caught up with Luke Skywalker and co. decades later, as the Rebel Alliance fought against the new and improved First Order and its twisted leader, Kylo Ren (Adam Driver), the son of Solo and Organa. The ever-growing success of the Star Wars franchise birthed many spinoff television shows, all of which have interconnected with the Skywalker Saga.

Even the franchise's four current standalone movies, the animated Star Wars: The Clone Wars, and the live-action Rogue One, Solo, and The Mandalorian and Grogu all connected to the greater Skywalker story in their own ways, too. Star Wars: Starfighter will establish a new status quo, however, by separating itself from the Skywalker Saga completely, marking the end of a 50-year-long era for the Star Wars franchise, but this could spell good news for the upcoming space opera movie.

Star Wars: Starfighter, despite being set in the same world as the Skywalker Saga projects, will be completely standalone. Rey (Daisy Ridley) was originally supposed to return to mentor Gray's character, but she was later replaced by Kade Auberon, further separating the story. Old Jedi and Sith feuds won't be the focus, Skywalkers and Palpatines might not even be mentioned, and epic galactic battles might be taken back to basics, delivering a unique Star Wars story for the first time in half a century.

Star Wars' Recent Movies Have Suffered in the Skywalker Saga

It spells good news for Star Wars: Starfighter that the movie won't be connected to the wider world of the Skywalker Saga, as many Star Wars movies, especially those released more recently, have crumbled under the weight of the 50-year story. Alongside the lackluster visual effects of the dated Star Wars prequel trilogy and the divisive storylines and character reveals of the more recent sequel trilogy, even the franchise's most popular and critically acclaimed television shows have suffered.

Perhaps most notably, the franchise's most recent movie failed to impress, and while Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu didn't have many ties to the Skywalker Saga, its parent show did. Luke Skywalker himself had started to train the young Grogu in the ways of the Force in Disney+'s The Mandalorian, and Din Djarin's (Pedro Pascal) young companion developed these skills in the movie. Unfortunately, The Mandalorian and Grogu was a failed experiment for the Star Wars franchise.

Despite some praise, especially for Pascal's performance and the action sequences, The Mandalorian and Grogu did not meet Disney's expectations at the worldwide box office, becoming the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars film ever. Spinning off from a television show might have harmed the movie's performance, as well as its connection to the Skywalker Saga, but Star Wars: Starfighter will not be hampered by either, so there are hopes it will perform significantly better in 2027.

Star Wars: Starfighter is perfectly primed to completely avoid any Star Wars fatigue that viewers—even some die-hard fans—might be experiencing after an onslaught of new Star Wars projects in recent years. Starfighter will have its own identity separate from the weight of the behemoth franchise, telling a completely new story that could pave the way forward for the Star Wars franchise. Star Wars: Starfighter is scheduled to premiere on May 28, 2027, as the 13th live-action movie in the Star Wars franchise.
