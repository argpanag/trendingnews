Inside Torreense's key players, new manager and tactic Celtic can exploit

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:46 · source · permalink

In football, or indeed any sport, everyone loves an underdog story. Think Leicester City winning the Premier League in 2016, Greece's victory at the 2004 European Championships or even Cape Verde, an archipelago with a population of around 530,000, almost eliminating holders and eventual finalists Argentina from last summer's World Cup.

There are even more examples closer to home. Back in May, Heart of Midlothian came within minutes of winning their first Scottish top-flight title in 66 years. A week later, Dunfermline Athletic played in only their sixth Scottish Cup final. The common Goliath that shattered both Davids? Celtic.

Martin O'Neill's side will once more look to prove dream crushers on Thursday when they travel to face Portuguese second division side Torreense.

Hailing from the small city of Torres Vedras, 50 kilometres north of Lisbon, Torreense haven't played in their country's top flight since the 1991/92 campaign.

They qualified for the Europa League by winning last season's Taca de Portugal, the Portuguese national cup. It was the first major trophy in the club's 109-year history.

A 2-1 victory over Sporting CP in the final remains their finest hour. It's a day Luis Cristovao, a lifelong Torreense supporter, will never forget.

"It's the first time a second league team won the Portuguese cup," Cristovao tells The Celtic Way. "I think it was the perfect game, because Sporting had lost the league, and they had a fantastic run in the Champions League, but they didn't win anything last season, and I think the Sporting team was not expecting that game to be so hard in the final.

"But Torreense scored in the fourth minute, in a corner, and after that, this competitiveness, this heart that was really there in the team, so in the defence, the team was and is very, very strong.

"It's a bit like a miracle, because even with difficulties, Sporting did have the responsibility to win that match, but Torreense made the perfect game."

Torreense faced Sunderland in the Europa League last month. (Image: Luis Loureiro / Shutterstock)

Cristovao is a prominent Portuguese journalist who's covered Champions League, Europa League, Premier League, La Liga, Ligue 1 and Bundesliga matches. He'll commentate on Thursday's game for Antena 1, Portugal's national radio station.

"I've been a supporter of Torreense since I was five years old," he says. "I went to see the games with my dad. After that my dad was the director of football of the team for ten years, so I lived inside the team a lot.

"I don't go to all the matches now as I work in football and it's not always easy to find the time to go, but I go to every match I can and I watch every match on TV when I'm not able to go. I'm a real supporter of Torreense forever."

As their 2,431-capacity home at Estadio Manuel Marques does not meet UEFA's stadium criteria, Torreense will face Celtic almost 47 miles away at the 23,888-capacity Municipal Stadium in Leiria.

Ironically, they have won as many games in the Portuguese second tier as they have in the Europa League this season.

On matchday one, Torreense defeated Norwegian side Lillestrom 1-2 away from home. That preceded consecutive defeats by Sunderland (1-2) and Ferencvaros (4-1), before they scored twice in stoppage time to beat Ararat Armenia 3-2 last time out.

"For me it was expected," Cristovao says of the decline in performances from European to domestic football. "The team is much stronger in games where we have to defend and play on the counter-attack, and this season we are viewed in the second league as the big team.

"Someone said at the start of the season that we were the Benfica of the second league, everybody wants to beat us, and that turned out to be very difficult for the team to have better results in the second league, and also there's a motivation issue within the team.

"The players are really focused on the Europa League, they want to shine in the Europa League, and you see a heart and motivation and competitiveness in those games that is harder to see in the league games.

"The team is really, really strong in these games that are played like a final, so I think that's the Torreense that Celtic might expect to find."

Unlike Torreense, Celtic have just one Europa League win this season, Camilo Duran's brace breathing fresh life into their campaign in a 3-2 victory over Besiktas on Bonfire Night.

To finish in the top 24 and subsequently reach the knockout stage play-offs, O'Neill's charges need between six and eight more points from their remaining four games against Torreense, Omonia Nicosia, Marseille and Union St-Gilloise.

Thursday's fixture, then, is crucial. But Torreense have ambitions of their own. One more victory puts them within touching distance of the top 24 with three games to spare.

"We have some respect for Celtic because of the titles and always being so strong in the Scottish league and also in Europe," Cristovao explained. "But now seeing the results I think a lot of supporters believe that maybe we can get some points against Celtic, because after two wins in the Europa League, the supporters understand that one more win and we might be in the [knockout stage] play-off.

"Looking at the games that we have, Celtic is a good prospect to get some points. So as things are evolving, we now see Celtic as an opportunity. We'll see what happens."

Despite guiding Torreense to Taca de Portugal glory and their first-ever European victory, manager Luis Tralhao was sacked in October.

"It was unfortunate," Cristovao says. "All the people around the club, all the supporters, really liked Luis, and are very disappointed that he was dismissed from the team.

"But it's the way that the owner works, so for me it was not a surprise, being some points down in the league, to have this kind of change.

"I understand that the owner of the team is always thinking about winning everything. He has this idea that the team, even playing in the Europa League, they have to win also in the league, and try to get promoted this season without other options."

Joao Nuno, 41, replaced Tralhao in the dugout. His first four matches have produced one draw, one win and two losses.

Nuno's only victory so far came against Ararat, where his use of the second 'pivot' in a 4-2-3-1 formation as a box-to-box midfielder worked effectively.

Predecessor Tralhao played the same formation but both players in the '2' sat back as traditional No. 6s, offering more defensive solidity and ball retention.

Cristovao is unsure whether the new coach's tactics will bear fruit with Celtic on the horizon.

"It worked against Ararat, but for the league I don't think that should work, because it puts the team with no reaction to ball loss, so let's see what he does in the next games."

In the win over Ararat, left-back Javi Vazquez scored one goal and set up another. He also provided assists away to Ferencvaros and Lillestrom earlier in the Europa League.

Now 26, Vazquez was capped once by Spain at youth level. In 90 Torreense appearances, he has 32 goal contributions from defence. Cristovao describes Vazquez as Torreense's 'best player'.

Javi Vazquez of Torreense defends against Sunderland's Nilson Angulo. (Image: Luis Loureiro / Shutterstock)

"He has the level to play in the first league, with no doubt. He's our left-back, but he's also our most dangerous offensive weapon, because he does a lot of assists, he scores from dead balls, he's a very good player and I was surprised to see him stay this season, because he has a first league level."

Manu Pozo is another threat. The right-winger converted those chances created by Vazquez against both Ferencvaros and Lillestrom and, per Cristovao, is 'very quick but also very technical and a very interesting player'.

He also mentions Joao Marques, who played 13 times for Portugal's under-21s.

"He's more of an offensive midfielder, but now he's playing a little bit down," Cristovao says. "He's very experienced, he was a player with a big potential, he has a contract with Braga, he's on loan here and he's very good.

"The team expects him to be the leader, but he's still trying to find his place. He's very good, so in any match I think he could pop up and shine."

Suppress Vazquez, Pozo and Marques, along with the infallible zeal that's carried Torreense to this stage, and Celtic stand a good chance of recording three more Europa League points.

That said, they've been in this movie countless times over, faced with a so-called European minnow, only to fall short of the mark à la Lincoln Red Imps and Kairat Almaty.

"Any result is possible," Ricardo Pessoa, who managed against Torreense in the Portuguese second tier last season, told this writer last week, best encapsulating the task at hand.
