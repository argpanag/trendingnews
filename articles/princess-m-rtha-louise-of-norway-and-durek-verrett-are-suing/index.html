Princess Märtha Louise of Norway and Durek Verrett Are Suing Their Former Friends

TheTools · 07.10.2026 15:31 · source · permalink

The couple is alleging a coordinated series of public attacks during an already difficult period for the Norwegian royal family.

Princess Märtha Louise of Norway and her husband, Durek Verrett, have filed a defamation lawsuit in Los Angeles against several former members of their inner circle, seeking more than $80 million in compensation. Court documents filed September 14 in Los Angeles County Superior Court allege a “calculated betrayal of trust” by people who had once been welcomed into the couple’s private lives and business operations.

According to the filings, the defendants include a former intimate partner of Verrett’s, his half-sister Demetria de La Nuit, a former manager and collaborator, and a former friend and collaborator, along with ten currently unidentified individuals. The lawsuit, which also names Verrett’s American company Ohana Productions as a plaintiff, accuses the defendants of spreading false and harmful allegations and, in two cases, violating confidentiality agreements. The documents also state that Verrett has suffered severe financial losses as a result of the allegations against him, including a reported 96% drop in revenue from parts of his business. Two hearings are currently scheduled in Los Angeles for December.

The couple’s American attorney, Richard Roth, who is working with Jamie Campbell, said in a statement to Norwegian outlet VG: “Our clients’ reputations have been judged by the public for years. Now the facts will be tested in a court of law.”

The lawsuit was filed just five days after the state funeral of King Harald V of Norway, Märtha Louise's father, who died in August at the age of 89 and was succeeded by her brother, now King Haakon VIII. The court documents acknowledge the timing, noting that the couple would have preferred to focus on “health, family, grief, and recovery” but were bound by legal deadlines. Three days after the lawsuit was filed, Princess Astrid—King Harald’s sister, and Märtha Louise’s aunt—died at the age of 94, having attended her brother's funeral just days earlier.

The Norwegian royal family has been navigating an unusually difficult period. Queen Mette-Marit, who has pulmonary fibrosis, underwent a lung transplant in June and will not return to official duties until next year at the earliest. Her son from a previous relationship, Marius Borg Høiby, was convicted in June of two counts of rape and sentenced to four years in prison. The family has also faced scrutiny over revelations about Mette-Marit’s past friendship with Jeffrey Epstein.

Princess Märtha Louise herself stepped back from royal duties in 2023 ahead of her marriage to Verrett, who describes himself as a sixth-generation shaman, to focus on the couple’s alternative medicine business. The two were also the subjects of a Netflix documentary, Rebel Royals: An Unlikely Love Story, released last year.
