DFS Tuesday: Ride the Lightning; St. Louis’ Top Spot; Carlson’s Production; Saros’ Value, and More

TheTools · 17.11.2026 22:43 · source · permalink

Andrew Santillo

2026-11-17

Alright, we’re in the midst of another topsy-turvy week when it comes to the schedule, but we have a nice 11-game slate on tap tonight. Break out those wallets, because I think we’re spending up.

Slate Preview

Tampa Bay Lightning

Running on newborn sleep deprivation while onboarding a large client at work has officially turned my brain into mush. I originally wrote “Tampa Bay Buccaneers” here. If you want to throw a future on them to win the NFC South, be my guest, but for tonight we’re talking about a completely different sport and the Tampa Bay Lightning.

If there’s a premium spot to target tonight, this is it. I understand all three members of the top line carry hefty price tags, with Nikita Kucherov costing over $8K, but this is an excellent spot, especially if Brayden Point returns to the lineup. Spending up always carries some risk, but on larger slates we have more opportunities to find affordable secondary stacks to pair with expensive pieces.

The Chicago third line could provide value if Patrick Kane skates with Frank Nazar. Parts of Calgary’s top line are also in play thanks to their shot volume and favorable power-play matchup, and there are a few Islanders worth considering as salary savers.

For single-entry contests, I’m very interested in building around Tampa Bay. Washington has allowed four or more goals in four of its last seven games, and the Capitals are heading into a matchup with a Tampa team that has yet to suffer a regulation loss on home ice.

St. Louis Blues

I caught part of Anaheim’s game on Sunday and Lukas Dostal simply hasn’t looked like the goalie we saw last season. Combine that with Anaheim’s struggling penalty kill, and suddenly I have some interest in St. Louis.

For me, this is a power-play-driven spot. If you want to stack the Blues’ top power-play unit, I’m on board. At even strength, I’d focus primarily on the top two lines, though the third line centered around Dylan Holloway can also work as a value filler.

In single-entry contests, St. Louis may end up on the outside looking in for me. However, in multi-entry builds, this is definitely a team I’ll have exposure to.

Dallas Stars

Last week I mentioned that Dallas was having issues generating offense at even strength. Maybe the Stars were listening, because they’ve now loaded up their top line with Jason Robertson, Wyatt Johnston, and Mikko Rantanen.

This trio logged roughly 12:30 together at even strength in their last game and looked promising. While stacking a line heading into Vegas isn’t always my favorite strategy, I have genuine interest here. You can get this line at a lower combined salary than Tampa Bay’s top unit, and anytime we get full correlation, it’s worth considering.

Vegas isn’t exactly one of the league’s most penalized teams, but I’m always willing to take a hard look at a fully correlated line.

Honestly, sometimes I just want to stack a line I genuinely enjoy watching. I’ve done that with Carolina at various points this season and found success, and I could see myself doing the same with Dallas tonight.

Defense

If you’re spending up on defense tonight, it shouldn’t be a surprise that Evan Bouchard ($7,500) and Matthew Schaefer ($7,200) lead the way. I’m not sure my salary allocations will allow me to pay up at defense, but if I do, those are the two players I’d prioritize.

In the mid-range, I already like Tampa Bay quite a bit, which makes John Carlson at $5,000 stand out as a strong option. Josh Morrissey may be back for Winnipeg, but I still think Neal Pionk is a perfectly viable play at $4,800. Cole Hutson has recorded five points in his last three games and comes at a very reasonable $4,500.

For value, Pavel Mintyukov is firmly on my radar. At $3,600, he has points in consecutive games and contributes enough blocked shots to give himself a realistic path to a block bonus.

Goalies

I have interest in the San Jose crease, but only if Yaroslav Askarov gets the start at $7,800. One amusing note: he scored more fantasy points in a loss to Pittsburgh last week than he did in a win against Utah.

Devon Levi ($7,500) should face strong shot volume against Florida, which always creates upside. For salary relief, I like Juuse Saros at $6,900. He got roughed up on Sunday, but prior to that he had been excellent over his previous five starts.
