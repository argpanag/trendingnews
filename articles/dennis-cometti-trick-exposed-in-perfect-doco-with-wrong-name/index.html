Dennis Cometti ‘trick’ exposed in perfect doco with wrong name

TheTools · 23.09.2026 01:49 · source · permalink

Australian cricket legend Justin Langer isn’t sure the Dennis Cometti documentary Centimetre Perfect has the right name.

While “centimetre perfect” was one of the iconic lines the legendary sports presenter would regularly pull out when calling Australian Football, Langer says Cometti was in fact “millimetre” perfect.

“His magic wasn’t a fluke. Greatness never is,” Langer said.

Centimetre Perfect premieres exclusively on Seven and 7plus tonight (Wednesday) at 8.30pm AEST.

And after watching a sneak peek of the documentary, Langer says he suspected nothing about Cometti’s genius was accidental.

“This week I sat in a pitch-black theatre and listened to a voice I have known my whole life. The late, great Dennis Cometti,” Langer said.

“I walked in expecting a highlights reel. I walked out thinking about friendship, partnership, craft, and the long shadow a good man casts over those who come after him.”

Some of Commetti’s most famous lines are “like a cork in the ocean” and, “he came up behind him like a librarian, he never heard him,” ... the latter a reference to an iconic moment in the replayed grand final of 2010 when Collingwood’s Heath Shaw stopped a certain Nick Riewoldt goal with a desperate dive in the goalsquare.

“Those lines landed in less than a second of live television. They sounded spontaneous. That was the trick,” Langer said.

“In the documentary, Chris Judd tells of having a cup of tea with Dennis and slowly realising he was quietly workshopping one-liners across the table. Testing them. Filing them away.

“I saw the same thing over many coffees with him. I always left entertained, suspecting not one word was accidental.”

Langer said Cometti shared the same traits as many elite sports people, such as Australian cricket legends Shane Warne and Adam Gilchrist.

“It’s the same with every genuinely excellent person I’ve worked with. Gilly’s hands looked effortless because of the thousands of balls nobody watched him take at training,” he said.

“Warney’s theatre looked improvised because he’d been thinking about the batsman for a week. The work is invisible by design. That’s what makes it look like magic.

“Then there was the partnership with Bruce McAvaney. Two men at the top of a brutally competitive profession, who spent decades making each other better and never once appeared to keep score.

“I opened the batting with Matty Hayden for the best part of six years. Ricky Ponting followed at No.3.

“The thing nobody tells you about a great partnership is how little of it is about runs. It’s rhythm. Knowing when your partner needs you to talk, and when he needs you to shut up. Being happier when he gets the hundred than when you do.

“How many of us have found a partnership like that? And how well are we looking after it?

“Legacy isn’t the games called or the awards on the shelf. It’s how people talk about you when you’re no longer in the room, and whether your standards are still being lived by people you may never have met.

“By that measure, Dennis was millimetre, not centimetre, perfect.”

McAvaney said about the exclusive documentary: “It was from laughter to, you know, having a tear in my eye. It’s very uplifting and for me personally, it was cathartic.”

McAvaney said he had “a lot of affection” for Cometti and when he died — in March this year at the age of 76 after battling Alzheimer’s disease and a severe form of dementia — “a little bit of me went with him”.

“That’s the broadcasting bit of me, but also the human bit of me,” McAvaney said.

“So yes, it was profound and perhaps unexpected. It is evidence to me personally of how deep the relationship turned out to be.”

Cometti once said the Cometti and McAvaney partnership had a bit of a “Lennon and McCartney” ring about it.

A humble McAvaney scoffs at that.

“Dennis was both Lennon and McCartney, by the way. I might have been Ringo, but that’ll do,” McAvaney told 7NEWS..

The documentary also reveals that Cometti had a fear of Olympic swimming commentary.

“Because my only qualification really was a pool in the backyard, I wasn’t qualified to do that,” Cometti said.

Despite that fact, some of Cometti’s calls from the pool at the Olympics are immortal.
