Navigating an outbreak requires distinguishing rumour from biological reality. Understanding how the virus behaves, why border checkpoints operate as they do, and why certain ecological zones experience repeated spillovers helps demystify Ebola without resorting to panic.  

In public health governance and corporate risk management, precise terminology is essential to prevent unnecessary panic and ensure proportionate responses.  

Ebola is not a pandemic pathogen. Filoviruses cannot spread through the air, casual breathing, or routine workplace interactions. Transmission requires direct contact with the infectious body fluids of an individual with active symptoms. Consequently, Ebola causes localised outbreaks and regional epidemics. Because every link in the transmission chain requires direct physical exposure, contact tracing, rapid testing, and specialised isolation units are highly effective at halting transmission.  

A central question in regional health security is why the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) accounts for the vast majority of historical Ebola outbreaks, whereas neighbouring East African nations experience only sporadic, imported cases.
