'The gap between Zverev and Shelton is bigger than the score'

TheTools · 18.11.2026 09:01 · source · permalink

Share this article

Jamie Murray told Sky Sports on Tuesday evening that Ben Shelton is some distance from the top three in men’s tennis, after watching Alexander Zverev dismantle the American 6-2, 7-6(4) in the John McEnroe Group at the ATP Finals in Turin. The scoreline was straightforward enough. Murray’s argument was that it still flattered Shelton.

“There is a lot of space between him and those three guys,” Murray said on Sky Sports’ post-match coverage, referring to Shelton’s standing relative to Zverev, Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz. It was a pointed claim from someone who knows this event well. Murray competed at the ATP Finals as a doubles player and understands how performance here differs from the weekly grind of the tour.

Zverev served 14 aces and made zero double faults

The numbers back Murray up. Zverev served 14 aces, made zero double faults and won 88 percent of his first-serve points. Shelton, one of the heaviest servers on tour, hit 15 aces himself but made 24 unforced errors to Zverev’s 10. The world number one won 74 points. Shelton won 55.

The first set took 38 minutes. Zverev broke in the opening game, broke again in the fifth, and barely broke sweat. Shelton held his nerve to force a tiebreak in the second and briefly led 3-1 inside it, but the structural damage was already done. Zverev closed it out 7-4.

Murray pointed to Zverev’s return of serve as the key to beating Shelton. His deep returns pushed the American back and stopped him from taking control with his next shot.

“The depth he was getting on those returns to push Shelton back,” Murray told Sky Sports, “and once you’re in the baseline rally, there is only one winner. Seriously, seriously impressive.”

Shelton usually relies on his serve to gain an early edge. But Zverev’s returns left him on the defensive, unable to dictate play from the positions he prefers.

Seven meetings, same result every time

Zverev has now beaten Shelton seven times without reply. The head-to-head includes their US Open final in September, which Zverev won in four sets to claim his second Grand Slam. At no point in that sequence has Shelton found an answer to what Zverev does in extended rallies: absorb pace, redirect with interest, and outlast.

Against Sinner and Alcaraz, Shelton’s record is similarly lopsided. He has beaten Alcaraz once in five meetings and Sinner less often still. The pattern across all three matchups points to the same structural fault Murray identified: Shelton’s weapons are devastating when he controls the rally from the first ball, but the top three are patient enough to absorb the initial damage and punish him once the exchange extends beyond two or three shots.

Murray’s view that Shelton is still a step behind the top three needs more context than their head-to-head records alone. The American is fourth in the world after a season of clear progress. He reached a Grand Slam final, won titles and qualified for Turin for the second year in a row.

By most measures, it has been the best year of his career. Yet Murray’s point is that a gap remains at the very top. Shelton is close, but taking that final step may prove the hardest part.

Zverev himself was careful not to dismiss the American. “He has so many great weapons,” he told reporters after the match. “If he’s playing with confidence, it’s really, really tough against him.” Shelton showed in his opening win over Arthur Fils that the confidence is there. Against Zverev, it ran out of room.

The question Murray’s verdict raises is not whether Shelton is a top-four player in ranking points. He is. It is whether he can solve one specific problem: what to do when the serve does not win the point outright. Until that answer arrives against Zverev, Sinner and Alcaraz, the gap Murray described will stay exactly where it is.

Shelton must beat Spain’s Rafael Jodar on Thursday to keep his semi-final hopes alive. Zverev, already through to the last four, faces Fils in his final group match.
