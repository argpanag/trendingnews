Marvel's New Rogue Dropped 'X-Men' Casting Hints 1 Month Before the Official Reveal [Exclusive]

TheTools · 21.10.2026 19:49 · source · permalink

Inde Navarrette’s performance in Obsession makes her casting as Rogue one of the most compelling additions to Marvel’s upcoming X-Men. As Nikki, she plays a woman whose control over her own body and relationships is taken away by someone else’s wish. The role demands that she remain recognizable beneath the movie's increasingly disturbing physical horror, giving the audience a reason to fear for her even when Bear fears her.

Rogue presents a different challenge, but her story also places bodily autonomy and intimacy at its center. Her ability to absorb another person’s powers and memories through touch makes physical affection dangerous, complicating the connections she wants to build. For Navarrette, the move from Nikki to Rogue offers another role in which extraordinary circumstances intrude on something as personal as deciding how to be close to someone.

Speaking with Collider’s Maggie Lovitt during the awards campaign for Obsession, Navarrette was asked whether she already knew she had landed Rogue when she publicly discussed wanting to play Mystique in July. “I did know, but that wasn't a lie,” she said. “I love Marvel and specifically X-Men. That is my crack.”

Four people, four completely different ideas of what it means to have somebody in your corner. Here is who picked up, what they would ask of you, and what you would get back.

You want the friend who does not leave. Steve is the one who turns up at the door without being asked, who remembers what you said you were worried about, and who has never once made you audition for his attention.

  • His entire character is built on a single sentence, which is that he will not abandon somebody just because it has become expensive to keep them.
  • He is far funnier than people give him credit for, and the joke is almost always at his own expense rather than yours.
  • The friendship comes with a cost, since being close to Steve means being dragged into fights that were never yours and being glad about it later.
  • You would get a friend who tells you the hard thing to your face and then defends you to everybody else in the room.

You want the friend who is genuinely exhausting and completely worth it. Tony will forget your birthday, then build you something at four in the morning that you did not know you needed, and hand it over as though it were nothing.

  • He is bad at saying the warm thing out loud, so he says it in hardware instead, which is why everybody around him ends up with upgraded everything.
  • Being his friend means putting up with the disappearing, the deflecting and the jokes that arrive about ninety seconds too early.
  • What you get in return is somebody who will spend an unreasonable amount of money and effort on a problem the moment it becomes yours.
  • He is the loudest person in the room and the one most likely to be quietly panicking about all of you.

You want the friend who replies in four seconds and apologises for it. Peter is earnest in a way that is almost unfashionable, and he will take on far more than he can carry rather than admit that he is struggling.

  • He is the only one on this list who needs looking after as much as he looks after you, and that balance is exactly what makes it a real friendship.
  • Nobody in the MCU is better at the small stuff, from remembering the thing you were nervous about to showing up with food you did not ask for.
  • Spider-Man: Brand New Day arrived in July 2026 and put him back where he works best, which is broke, local and carrying the weight on his own.
  • You would get a friend who is sincerely, embarrassingly happy for you when something goes right, with no edge to it at all.

You want the friend who insults your shoes, fixes your phone and then quietly rearranges your entire life for the better. Shuri does not do soft reassurance, and that is the reason you would believe her when she says you are fine.

  • She is the smartest person in almost any room she walks into and treats that as a reason to tease you rather than a reason to lecture you.
  • The teasing is the affection. When Shuri stops making fun of you, something has gone seriously wrong and you should probably ask her about it.
  • Her story is the one on this list that goes somewhere genuinely dark, which is what turned her from a scene stealer into the emotional centre of the films.
  • You would get a friend who never pities you, never softens the truth, and never once lets you take yourself too seriously.

Inde Navarrette Was Dropping Hints About Rogue Before Her ‘X-Men’ Casting Announcement

Mystique is a particularly interesting name to bring up given her relationship with Rogue in the comics. As Rogue’s adoptive mother, she is a major figure in her history, with their relationship extending well beyond which side of a fight they happen to be on. Those family ties give the two characters plenty of shared history for readers to work with. Navarrette’s answer also touched on the earlier X-Men movies and Rebecca Romijn’s portrayal of Mystique. “And so, growing up, I watched a lot of the films, and I have always loved Mystique,” she said. After Romijn’s name came up, she continued:

“The way that that character is, it's so complicated, and it's so nuanced when you have the originality of the story. Her and Rogue are also very similar, you know what I mean, if you've read the comics. So I was not lying. I was dropping hints, and I was like, 'If anybody's picking up what I'm putting down?', but nobody did.”

For now, Navarrette has Obsession behind her and a very different franchise ahead. Barker’s horror movie gives Nikki’s story a devastating physical component, something that also comes with the territory when playing a mutant who absorbs other people’s powers and memories through touch. The circumstances are different, but both characters need an actress who can make the person underneath those frightening abilities matter. “But I love Mystique, dude,” Navarrette added. “Oh my God, love her.” As for those earlier comments, she said, “So it wasn't a lie, but it was a little like a rabbit in the hat situation.”

Marvel’s X-Men is scheduled to arrive in theaters on May 5, 2028. Stay tuned at Collider for the rest of Lovitt's conversation with Navarrette.
