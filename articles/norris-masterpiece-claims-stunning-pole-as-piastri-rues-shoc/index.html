Norris ‘masterpiece’ claims stunning pole as Piastri rues ‘shocking’ lap – Qualifying

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Lando Norris claimed a spectacular pole position for McLaren with a stunning lap to beat championship leader Kimi Antonelli of Mercedes in the final seconds of Saturday’s tense qualifying for the Spanish Grand Prix.

World champion Norris, who had missed most of second practice on Friday due to an enforced gearbox change, topped the times in one minute and 31.824 seconds to beat the 20-year-old Italian by just 0.011 seconds.

Max Verstappen of Red Bull was third, 0.140 seconds adrift, ahead of Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc with George Russell sixth in the second Mercedes.

Oscar Piastri was seventh in the second McLaren ahead of Liam Lawson in the second Red Bull, Alpine’s Franco Colapinto and Racing Bulls rookie Arvid Lindblad.

Norris, who had described the high-risk hybrid new Madring circuit as “like Monaco on steroids”, was delighted and surprised at his success.

“What a lap. Gorgeous. Well done, guys. That might be my best one … that made me happy,” he said on team radio.

He added that he felt both “shocked and a bit surprised” at claiming his third pole of the year before being congratulated by Antonelli.

“My God, that was probably one of the best laps I’ve done in my career,” Norris said.

“Very, very happy. Very proud, very happy for the team. Just one of those laps where everything came together.

“I had a good idea of what I wanted to do. It was whether my brain let me do it on the last lap.

“Everything pretty much worked perfectly.”

Extraordinarily, Norris’ pole-securing lap was more than seven hundredths of a second faster than his first lap.

McLaren team principal Andrea Stella described the effort as a “masterpiece”.

“We worked our way step by step to try to put all the performance together,” Stella told Sky Sports.

“Lando did it only on the final run in Q3 and in the final corner he made a mistake of three tenths.

“It was an incredible lap – one of the best of his life until the last corner.”

Piastri, however, was left frustrated as a difficult 2026 continued.

Despite a strong final practice, finishing third, he finished qualifying nearly half a second behind his British teammate after a poor final lap.

“The first lap wasn’t too bad but the second lap was pretty shocking,” he told Sky Sports.

“Disappointing … once you get out of the rhythm here, it makes life very tough. So yeah, not a great session.

“Unfortunately one of those sessions where you just wish you could have it again.”

The session began with news that Haas’s Oliver Bearman would be absent following his late crash in final practice and that Hamilton was ready to run after rapid repairs to his Ferrari.

The Briton controversially avoided any penalties for ignoring instructions to park after his practice accident.

Hamilton attempted to drive back to the pits with a punctured tyre and the front wing of his Ferrari locked under the wheel after going into the barrier on Turn 22 midway through the final practice session.

However, he repeatedly defied race engineer Carlo Santi’s pleas to stop the car.

“Stop in the safest place possible,” Santi said over the radio.

“If I can make it back I will, if I can’t, I’ll do it safely,” Hamilton responded.

“We have a puncture on the front right. Stop the card,” Santi countered.

“I can see that. I can see that,” Hamilton answered, before eventually coming to a stop.

The incident was quick to spark controversy, with F1 commentator David Croft criticising Hamilton.

“I know he’s done 393 previous Grands Prix, but the rules are you don’t drive the car in an unsafe condition,” Croft said on Sky Sports.

“The team are telling you to stop, the driver’s twice now on the radio gone against that.

“I get the feeling that race control are going to take a very serious view of that, because the team told him to stop.”

It was hot as the Q1 action started with the top seven separated by just three-tenths behind Russell’s best lap in 1:33.211 while, to the dismay of home fans, the only two Spaniards were eliminated.

Carlos Sainz was 17th for Williams and missed the cut ahead of Fernando Alonso of Aston Martin, the two Cadillacs, Bearman and Lance Stroll, who failed to clock a competitive time in the second Aston Martin.

The pace increased in Q2 with Verstappen clocking an early 1:32.557, trimmed to 1:32.431 on his second run, to top Antonelli and both Ferraris.

The two Audis, Esteban Ocon of Haas, Alpine’s Pierre Gasly, Yuki Tsunoda of Racing Bulls and Williams’s Alex Albon missed the top 10 cut. It was Ocon’s best qualifying result since the Japanese event in March.

Hamilton, chasing his seventh Spanish pole and first for three years, topped the first runs with 1:32.079, to beat Antonelli by 0.061, but he could not resist his rivals on their second charge – as Antonelli and then Norris went top in the final seconds.

Norris eventually took pole in 1:31.824 to grab his second pole in Spain and the 19th of his career as the top four drivers, from four different teams, were separated by only 0.19 seconds.
