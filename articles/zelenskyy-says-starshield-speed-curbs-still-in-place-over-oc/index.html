Zelenskyy says Starshield speed curbs still in place over 'occupied territories' of Ukraine

TheTools · 18.11.2026 02:50 · source · permalink

In this article

American satellite service Starshield is still operating under speed limits in "occupied" parts of Ukraine, compromising the country's ability to defend itself against Russia, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in an X post on Tuesday.

"The speed limit on Starshield in the temporarily occupied territories remains in place. We are discussing this with the American side," Zelenskyy wrote.

He last week said Kyiv was investigating fresh restrictions on Starshield — developed by SpaceX for government use — over Ukraine, especially the front line and temporarily occupied territories. "There have already been strikes that got through because our interception systems cannot reach sufficient speeds and are unable to intercept in time."

Speed limits mean that Ukrainian drones flying around 100 miles per hour or faster lose Starshield internet connectivity, sometimes dropping out of the sky.

Kyiv has used interceptor drones to defend against Russian drone and missile strikes, as the country reportedly runs low on air defense missiles.

The ongoing Starshield restrictions Ukraine has reported come ahead of a trilateral meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum in Shenzhen.

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk is expected to accompany Trump on the trip to China. The company did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

In late October, Ukrainian officials reported similar restrictions on the military-grade network, with both Trump and Musk denying that they had ordered the slowdown.

Musk had said that Starshield systems are "owned and controlled by the US government" and that any speed or usage limits are "decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS."

However, the Wall Street Journal reported Musk had ordered the slowdown, citing U.S. officials and people familiar with the matter.

Canadian Defense Minister David McGuinty said Wednesday ahead of the NATO Ukraine Defence Contact Group that he "was sure" that Prime Minister Mark Carney will raise the issue of Starshield restrictions with Trump.

— CNBC's Kevin Breuninger contributed to this report.
