Rolling Stones expand "Foreign Tongues" with five songs

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:29 · source · permalink

On 27 November 2026, The Rolling Stones release "Foreign Tongues, Loose Lips", the deluxe expansion of "Foreign Tongues". It adds five recordings from the same studio sessions, including a vocal duet by Mick Jagger and Keith Richards and a track featuring jazz saxophonist Courtney Pine. Formats include red vinyl with a CD and a deluxe two-CD set.

The Rolling Stones expand the album "Foreign Tongues" with five additional tracks

The Rolling Stones will release an expanded edition of their album "Foreign Tongues" on November 27, 2026, under the title "Foreign Tongues, Loose Lips". It features five additional recordings from the same studio sessions, which the record label presents as material revealing another part of the creative period associated with the album released in July. The five tracks are available on a separate vinyl release, a CD edition and in digital formats. They include a vocal duet between Mick Jagger and Keith Richards, accompanied by Charlie Watts on drums.

Five additional recordings from the same sessions

The expanded tracklist consists of "Where There’s A Will", "Who You Callin’ Crazy", "Human Tide", "Bad Luck Hideaway" and "She Still Comes Around". In its announcement, Universal Music emphasises that the songs collectively reflect the creative momentum and musical connection between the band members while working on the album. However, these are not five completely unknown recordings: the label states that "Bad Luck Hideaway" had already been released as a bonus track on the iTunes-exclusive edition of "Foreign Tongues". It subsequently became more widely available on digital platforms as the B-side of the streaming single "Mr. Charm". The expanded edition therefore brings together five songs from the same period, but does not present every one of them as a premiere of previously unreleased music.
"Who You Callin’ Crazy" features guest appearances by jazz saxophonist Courtney Pine. According to Universal Music's announcement, the song was released digitally on October 20 and served as the first glimpse of previously unreleased material prepared for "Loose Lips". The vocal duet between Jagger and Richards, accompanied by Watts on drums, also stands out in particular. Its presence connects the release with the group's archival legacy: Watts died in 2021, and his performances on later releases are based on recordings made before his death. The record label does not provide detailed descriptions of the sound or lyrics of the individual additional songs, so for now, the clearest context for understanding them is the fact that they originate from the same sessions as the original album.

How the tracks are arranged across the editions

"Foreign Tongues, Loose Lips" has been designed so that the five additional recordings can also function as a standalone collection. The standalone vinyl edition contains all five songs on an LP, and the package also includes a CD featuring the original album. According to Universal Music's technical description, the vinyl is pressed on a heavyweight red record, while the sleeve combines matte and glossy details with a cut-out on the front. This arrangement allows listeners who already own the original album to obtain the additional material on vinyl, together with a CD containing the complete July release. The five songs on the vinyl are divided between two sides: the first two appear on side A, while the remaining three are on side B.
The second physical option is a two-CD deluxe edition. The first disc contains all 14 tracks from the original album, while the second is dedicated to the five additional recordings. Universal Music states that the edition comes in a six-panel digisleeve, accompanied by a 16-page booklet and gold foil details. Digital formats are also included in the offer, meaning the additional material is not restricted to physical collector's editions. The difference between the formats is not in the content of the five additional songs, but in how they are combined with the original album: the vinyl separates them onto an additional record alongside the included CD, while the double CD distributes the entire project across two discs.

The July album as the starting point

The original "Foreign Tongues" was released on July 10, 2026, through the record label Polydor Records. It contained 14 tracks and, according to Universal Music's announcement, reached number one on the charts in 14 countries. The album followed less than three years after "Hackney Diamonds", the band's previous studio release, which won a Grammy and achieved multi-platinum status. The expanded version now adds further recordings to an album that had already made a strong international impact.
The original album features 12 new compositions, alongside a cover of Amy Winehouse's "You Know I’m No Good" and a performance of "Beautiful Delilah", a song associated with Chuck Berry's influence on the band's musical identity. Universal Music describes "Rough and Twisted" as an opening track with a blues-infused sound, while "In The Stars" and "Jealous Lover" were presented as singles associated with the album. The label highlighted "Jealous Lover" as a single with a soul atmosphere and "In The Stars" as a livelier, more rhythm-driven preview. These songs provide the context in which the five additional recordings appear: "Loose Lips" does not replace the original tracklist but supplements it with material from the same period of work.

Recording sessions and collaborators

According to Universal Music, "Foreign Tongues" was created in less than a month at Metropolis Studios in West London. Mick Jagger, Keith Richards and Ronnie Wood once again worked there with producer Andrew Watt, who also produced "Hackney Diamonds". Jagger spoke about the intensive weeks of recording and how the band recorded 14 songs in a short period. Richards highlighted the continuity between the two albums and his satisfaction at returning to work in London, while Wood described the studio atmosphere as creative and said that many performances were successful on the first attempt. These statements offer insight into the working environment from which the additional material also emerged, and the record label connects all five additional songs with the creative period during which the original album was made.
Alongside the three band members, the original album featured longtime collaborators Darryl Jones, Matt Clifford and Steve Jordan. Guest musicians include Steve Winwood, Paul McCartney, Robert Smith from The Cure and Chad Smith from Red Hot Chili Peppers. Charlie Watts appears on the song "Hit Me In The Head", which the label identifies as one of his last recorded sessions before his death in 2021. His contribution is also important in the context of the expanded edition, as the announcement for "Loose Lips" specifically highlights another recording featuring his drumming. This connects the band's more recent studio period with performances recorded before the death of its longtime drummer.

Critical reception and the place of the expanded edition

Universal Music states that "Foreign Tongues" received a positive critical response. According to the label's announcement, The Times awarded it five stars and described it as an exceptionally high achievement in the group's career, while The Telegraph also gave it five stars, highlighting its energy, wit and richness of sound. These reviews provide context for the decision to expand the album with recordings from the same period, but they do not mean that the new editions can automatically be assessed on the basis of reviews of the original album. The five songs on "Loose Lips" are a separate addition, and their significance lies in the fact that they reveal another part of the creative process behind the making of "Foreign Tongues".
The November 27 release brings the album cycle that began in July to a close, while also allowing the songs on "Loose Lips" to be viewed as a separate document of the band's work during that period. For listeners who already own "Foreign Tongues", the standalone vinyl with an included CD provides five additional songs in physical form; the double CD is intended for those who want the original album and its expansion together in one release. Digital availability further removes format restrictions. The new development is not a change to the original album, but the opportunity to consider five recordings from its sessions in a shared context, including the collaboration between Jagger and Richards and Watts's contribution. "Foreign Tongues, Loose Lips" thus expands the already released studio work and brings together additional material in several different formats.
