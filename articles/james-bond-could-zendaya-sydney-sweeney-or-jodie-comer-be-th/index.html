James Bond: Could Zendaya, Sydney Sweeney or Jodie Comer be the next Bond girl opposite Robert Pattinson?

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:57 · source · permalink

After months of speculation, we finally have a new Bond. The question now is - who will play Robert Pattinson’s leading lady?

What’s clear is that Pattinson’s announcement as 007 has completely upped the game.

He's one of Hollywood's biggest stars, so all eyes are on Amazon MGM to see whether they go for an equally big name as a ‘Bond girl’, or if they'll take a chance on someone a little less well known.

Whoever is cast opposite him, though, needs to have a "very strong identity of her own", believes film critic Larushka Ivan-Zadeh - "someone distinctive, sexy, complicated".

Showbiz reporter Catrina Rose agrees: "I'd love to see a Bond girl who's just as interesting and unpredictable as he is."

And then, some say the whole concept of a Bond girl is outdated and the role needs to evolve for a new era. So what can we expect?

Who will the next 'Bond girl' be?

No actress has yet confirmed they're in the running - but stars who have been bandied about the internet include Zendaya, whose name shot to the top of the list after Pattinson's casting.

The duo have already shared three films together this year and make a "really exciting pairing," says Ivan-Zadeh.

Rose agrees that as one of the most recognisable actresses in Hollywood, Zendaya is "definitely someone I'd be thinking about now".

But her Euphoria co-stars Hunter Schafer and Sydney Sweeney could give her a run for her money.

There have been rumours about Sweeney getting the role for months, and you can see why, says Dr Llewella Chapman, a film historian at the University of East Anglia. "She has sex appeal and an ability to take on tougher roles too," she tells the BBC.

Sweeney was given a boost last month by former Bond girl Trina Parks, who told the Telegraph she would fit the role well because Bond needed a woman with "ownership over her sexuality".

Speculation is also growing over Pattinson’s partner, actor and singer Suki Waterhouse. She once said she’d love to play "a bad-ass Bond girl".

But there isn’t a precedent for husband-wife couples in the films, and it may be more likely that she picks up a cameo role, if indeed she wants to.

Meanwhile, TV presenter Maya Jama has picked up new interest following her storming run on Celebrity Traitors. After she quit Love Island, rumours swirled that she could be the next Bond girl - but she’s just signed a Netflix deal, so it’s unclear whether that would still be a possibility.

Another strong contender is Florence Pugh who, like Zendaya, also stars alongside Pattinson in Dune: Part Three. Earlier this week she threw her hat in the ring to be in the new Bond film - although as a villain, rather than his leading lady.

"She ticks quite a few boxes if they're looking for someone bankable," says Rose. "I could absolutely see her as a Bond woman who turns out to be the villain, someone Bond thinks is on his side, but she's actually been playing him the whole time."

Queen's Gambit star Anya Taylor-Joy is another name Amazon MGM could be thinking about if they want a big, recognisable actress, as well as Killing Eve's Jodie Comer (Villanelle meets Bond, anyone?)

Meanwhile, Crazy Rich Asians star Gemma Chan told Variety she was "flattered" by fans speculating she could be a Bond girl. "Absolutely I would," she said.

Other names that have been floated include Saoirse Ronan, Margaret Qualley, Marisa Abela, Emily Blunt, Lily James, Chase Infiniti, Inde Navarrette, Simone Ashley, Deva Cassel and Ambika Mod.

And it could easily be someone totally different. Producers have previously chosen a Bond girl who isn't particularly famous yet - or is on the cusp of stardom.

'I say Bond lady; Bond woman'

But then there’s the wider question of whether the whole idea of a “Bond girl” is now somewhat outdated.

Historically, the character had little agency and existed primarily as Bond's romantic interest - and the term "girl" itself is also criticised for infantilising women.

Monica Bellucci rejected the label. She told the Guardian ahead of her appearance in Spectre in 2015: "I can't say I'm a Bond girl because I'm too mature to be a Bond girl. I say Bond lady; Bond woman."

The way the films have treated Bond girls "hasn't always aged well", says Rose.

Some scenes from 1960s Bond films are "basically rape", and "wouldn't fly today", said No Time To Die director Cary Fukunaga in 2021 - referring to Thunderball and Goldfinger.

In reality, we have come a long way since then.

Over the years, producers have taken steps to usher the franchise into the modern era.

These days, the women of Bond are now much more likely to be sharp, capable equals to 007 - and what's defined as "attractive" has also evolved.

Belluci's inclusion in Spectre as a "Bond woman" aged 50 was widely praised for spotlighting a different type of sex appeal (even though she was only four years older than Daniel Craig in real life).

We've also had other powerful women in Bond, such as Michelle Yeoh's Colonel Wai Lin and Lashana Lynch's MI6 agent Nomi, who also made history as the first black female 007.

Rather than fitting the traditional Bond girl mould, both of them were competent women who could give Bond a run for his money.

"We've moved away from the role being someone with a silly name and for Bond to pat on their bottom," says film critic Ivan-Zadeh. "It's moving in the right direction".

Many people say, however, there is still further to go.

Having more female Bond villains, as well as love interests, could be one way to make it more equal, suggests Rose. "That's what I'd want to see."

"They can be romantic interests, villains, spies, allies or even the person who ultimately outsmarts Bond. There's room for all of those roles,” agrees showbiz journalist Nikki Onafuye.

Another way the roles could evolve is by bringing in "a queer or sexually fluid Bond woman," suggests Ivan-Zadeh. "Someone whose desires don’t simply revolve around Bond. That would create some genuinely interesting chemistry.”

Others are hoping to see more diverse actresses cast from film markets such as China, India or Nigeria.

“There have been a number of women of colour in the past, but I'd like to see more," says Be Manzini, writer and filmmaker.

"I'd like to see an Indian Bond girl," agrees Chapman. "Aishwarya Rai was touted for the follow up to Die Another Day but never got there."

More broadly, there is an appetite to see Bond girls have their own lives.

“For me, it's about giving these women proper storylines,” says Onafuye. "They should have their own motivations and their own ambitions.”

None of this is to say we should do away with the traditional Bond glamour.

"No-one wants to see Bond girls slumping around in their PJs," says Ivan-Zadeh.

But the important distinction is that "being sexy shouldn't be the only thing a female character has to offer,” says Onafuye.

“To those arguing that it's fine for Bond's women just to be sexy and hot, I'd say there's nothing sexier or hotter than a woman (or man) with a brain and a wit,” adds entertainment journalist Caroline Frost.

Ultimately, whatever direction the new producers go in, most agree the "Bond girl" or Bond woman is a key part of the Bond universe.

But continuing to evolve is crucial, says Chapman. "It's about retaining the humour and history of Bond but also recognising - this is 2026."

There are, of course, lots of other women within the films, and for Frost, the key is to have a range of different Bond women on screen, "all well matched with 007 in terms of strength, resources and, above all, wit".

"Bond has always had fascinating women," agrees Rose. "I'd just love the next film to give them the space to be as interesting as the man in the tuxedo."
