Labour approves major solar farm in Robert Jenrick’s constituency

TheTools · 29.10.2026 09:37 · source · permalink

Reform MP says decision is a ‘disgrace’ and a ‘hammer blow’ to Newark and Nottinghamshire

Robert Jenrick has criticised the Energy Secretary for approving a major solar farm in his constituency, calling the plans a “disgrace”.

The Reform MP said the decision to grant approval for the Great North Road Solar scheme in his Newark constituency was a “hammer blow” and meant 10pc of the area would be carpeted with solar panels.

The controversial project, which will cover nearly 2,500 acres of prime farmland, has generated a significant local backlash from objectors increasingly furious at how the Nottinghamshire region is being targeted by solar developers.

On Tuesday, the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, led by Miatta Fahnbulleh, said consent had been granted for the scheme as well as a “biodiversity park” in the constituency.

Mr Jenrick, who is his party’s Treasury spokesman, said: “I am appalled and devastated that the Government has approved this vast solar scheme. This is a hammer blow to Newark and Nottinghamshire.

“The whole area will now be subjected to acre upon acre of solar panels. More than 10pc of my constituency will now be solar. Good farmland ruined. Years of mess and disruption. Views wrecked for generations. Labour clearly have a death wish against our county.

“I am disgusted that the views of local people have been utterly ignored. That was obvious from the beginning as Sherwood’s Labour MP even took a payment from the developer in the general election. The Energy Secretary has refused to meet local residents or answer basic questions. It’s a disgrace.”

Third scheme in Jenrick’s constituency

The scheme is one of the largest solar projects approved anywhere in the UK, generating 800 megawatts when the sun is shining.

It marks the third scheme in Mr Jenrick’s constituency to be approved, after the One Earth and Steeples solar plants.

Other mega-solar projects in the region include the Cottam, West Burton, and Gate Burton plants already slated for the Nottinghamshire and Lincolnshire borders.

Jason Knauf

The Campaign to Protect Rural England and numerous local parish councils highlighted that more than 60pc of the land is top quality farmland currently used for intensive arable crop production for wheat, barley, and oilseed rape.

The developer of the scheme is Elements Green, a private company based in Mayfair, London.

Rasmus Friis, its director, could not be reached for comment, but the project website said that the scheme “would continue the rich history of power generation in this area”.

This is a reference to the power stations that once operated nearby, whose legacy includes the substations providing the connections that now make the region so attractive for solar developers.

Announcing the decision, the Planning Inspectorate said it had considered thousands of local objections but rejected them:

“The Examining Authority listened and gave full consideration to all local views and the evidence gathered during the examination before making its recommendation to the Secretary of State.”
