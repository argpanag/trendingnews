Sydney Southwest Metro set to open after 130yo train line replaced

In short:

An open date has been set for the long-awaited Southwest Metro after a two-year project to replace the 130-year-old line.

The first driverless service is expected to depart early morning on Friday, October 30.

It is hoped the new service will cut travel time with connections including from south-west Sydney to the CBD.

The long-awaited Southwest Metro extension now has an opening date, after extended works to replace the 130-year-old train line.

NSW Premier Chris Minns said the $23 billion project would officially open with the first service departing at 4:31am on Friday, October 30.

The new metro offering will connect south-western suburbs to the Sydney CBD, North Sydney and north-western suburbs.

From next Friday, the NSW government said passengers would have access to trains every four minutes during weekday peak hours in places including Bankstown, Punchbowl, Wiley Park, Lakemba, Dulwich Hill and Marrickville.

"The new Metro will deliver 50 per cent more passenger capacity than the old T3 Bankstown Line it replaces, along with faster journeys and direct connections to more jobs, education and opportunities across Sydney," a spokesperson said.

The opening was delayed until 2026 due to the project's complexity and industrial action. (ABC News: Jak Rowland)

They also said services would run every five minutes during the day, and every 10 minutes in the evenings and on weekends.

On the first weekend, between October 31 and November 1, services will increase to accommodate increased interest.

Mr Minns said it was an investment that would "make a real difference" to communities for "generations to come".

"This is about giving people in south-west Sydney the same fast, reliable public transport connections that other parts of our city enjoy," he said.

Goodbye pink buses

Buses replaced trains on the south-west line for more than two years, and pink-coloured buses became a regular sight on major roads between Sydenham and Bankstown.

It meant some commuters faced lengthier travel times.

Mr Graham says the time for pink buses is over. (ABC News: Declan Bowring)

Transport Minister John Graham said communities "have been very patient" and the wait for the new line to open "will be worthwhile".

"The pink buses have really played a big role moving people around, but the time for them is over," he said.

"We know this has had an impact, though, on communities, on passengers, on businesses as well.

"There's no easy way to upgrade the line in this way; it has taken time. Now it's here, I think people will immediately see the benefits."

Mr Graham said the new line will transform the way people travel around Sydney.

"You won't need to check the timetable, you'll simply be able to turn up at the station and jump on a metro within minutes," he said.

The pink Southwest Link buses will continue to operate on the opening day and weekend.

Station improvements and line upgrades

The new metro line runs from Sydenham to Bankstown. (ABC News: Jak Rowland)

About 48,000 trips are expected to be made across the metro line on a typical weekday during the 8am and 9am peak.

Upgrades include platform screen doors, new lifts and station upgrades, updated customer information systems and enhanced security technology along the line.

Improvements have also been made at stations to improve accessibility for prams and people living with a disability, with accessible lifts at Wiley Park, Dulwich Hill, Hurlstone Park, Punchbowl and Canterbury for the first time.

The T3 Bankstown Line between Sydenham and Bankstown ceased operations in September 2024 to convert the 130-year-old heavy rail line to a metro line.

The Sydney City and Southwest Metro station under construction at Martin Place in 2020. (ABC News: Rachel Pupazzoni)

The conversion deadline was pushed back due to industrial action and complexity issues. It was initially meant to take 12 months.

It is the final stage of Sydney's M1 Metro North West and Bankstown Line, which will connect Bankstown to Tallawong along a 66-kilometre stretch.

The state government has warned passengers there may be "minor teething issues as services get underway".
