Oil is moving, but not always in straight lines. Soaring VLCC freight costs from the U.S. Gulf to Asia and rerouted flows around a war zone are reshaping who pays more for crude and who enjoys a discount. That shift creates winners in crude shipping and logistics, and leaves others squeezed by freight and feedstock. This article walks through three stocks tied to these disruptions and explains why their exposure to the current tanker crunch matters for your portfolio.

Okeanis Eco Tankers (OB:OET)

Overview: Okeanis Eco Tankers is a Greece based owner and operator of modern VLCC and Suezmax crude tankers serving global oil trade routes.

Operations: The business generates about $706 million from tanker vessels, with crude transport revenues largely linked to customers in Europe and Asia.

Market Cap: NOK34.2b

Okeanis Eco Tankers sits at the center of the current crude shipping reshuffle, because its young VLCC and Suezmax fleet is closely linked to long haul flows and spot rate swings that define this screener theme.

"Rising long haul crude trade volumes and rerouted flows, such as Middle East barrels moving toward Asia and shifts away from Russian supply, continue to support higher ton miles for Okeanis Eco Tankers and can keep fleet utilization and revenue at levels that the current valuation already prices in as ongoing."

DHT Holdings (DHT)

Overview: DHT Holdings is a pure play crude tanker owner with a VLCC fleet that moves long haul oil cargoes for global refiners.

Operations: DHT Holdings generates about $799 million from operating its crude oil tanker fleet, with earnings closely tied to VLCC freight markets.

Market Cap: $4.0b

DHT Holdings is one of the cleanest ways to get exposure to the Global Crude Shipping & Logistics Beneficiaries theme because its VLCC heavy fleet is closely linked to the same long haul rate swings that are reshaping how barrels move between the U.S., Middle East, Europe, and Asia.
