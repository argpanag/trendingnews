Parliament Rejects Draft Resolution to Have Council of Ministers Seek Exemption from Ban on Imports of Russian Petroleum Products

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:36 · source · permalink

Parliament rejected on Wednesday a draft resolution submitted by Vazrazhdane, which would task the Council of Ministers with requesting from the European Commission, within one week, an exemption from the ban on the import and purchase of crude oil and petroleum products originating from Russia or exported from Russia. The proposal was rejected by 10 votes to 13, with 115 abstentions.

In its motives, the bill’s sponsor said that an emergency situation had arisen in which an armed conflict between Iran and the United States had led to a drastic increase in fuel prices on global markets. “If our country starts importing cheaper fuels from Russia in this emergency situation, this will have a positive effect on the Bulgarian economy and slow both the increase in the prices of all goods and services and inflation,” the motives for the bill said.

“When Prime Minister Rumen Radev says that he will work to restore relations with Russia, this carries a lot of weight with voters. As of today, the Government is doing exactly the opposite - it is neither working to restore relations with Russia nor changing the Euro-Atlantic policies pursued for 37 years,” said Vazrazhdane MP Tsoncho Ganev. "Fuel prices directly affect what happens in our economic life. We are paying high fuel prices even though they could be lower if we had a guaranteed supplier several hundred kilometres away that would provide guaranteed quantities that we need,” Ganev said. He argued that these sanctions were a burden on Bulgarian society and on all consumers of goods and services.

“Fuel prices have increased, and this is a major component of the prices of goods and services. So far, the Government has not introduced a single comprehensive measure that would allow citizens and businesses to benefit from lower fuel prices, while other EU Member States have taken such measures over the past three or four months,” said Vazrazhdane Deputy Floor Leader Peter Petrov.
