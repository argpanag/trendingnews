Channel Islands red wind warning as Storm Austen approaches

TheTools · 23.10.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

A red weather warning for wind has been issued in the Channel Islands ahead of Storm Austen.

Jersey Met has forecast very strong winds with gusts reaching 75mph (120km/h) and potentially up to 85mph, with the strongest winds expected early on Friday.

All Government of Jersey schools would be closed on Friday and islanders were advised to avoid outdoor activity where possible, officials said.

Nikki de Gruchy, a farmer whose property was badly damaged in previous storms, said she was facing an anxious wait.

La Croix Regeneration Centre was damaged in storms Ciarán and Goretti, with several trees brought down and a cattle shelter flipped over.

De Gruchy said she was preparing for Storm Austen by putting in new tree stakes and weighing down trampolines used by chickens for shelter.

"Trees are such a big part of what we're doing here... I'm not going to be getting much sleep," she said.

"[We're] making sure that the animals are in a place which is open and there aren't any big trees which could potentially fall on them."

Jersey Met said it was briefing authorities and the situation was being "closely monitored".

"Islanders are being advised to avoid outdoor activity where possible," said officials.

They said the decision to close government schools on Friday was made "as early as possible to give families time to organise childcare arrangements".

Among the other services closed include the Child and Adolescent Mental Health Service at Bermuda House, but two nurses and a duty doctor will monitor the duty phone remotely.

All youth clubs will be closed on Thursday night and on Friday morning Jersey Library and Western Library will be shut.

Winds were expected to gradually ease on Friday afternoon, forecasters said.

Senior forecaster Matt Winter said there could be some damage to trees and travel disruption.

Scientists have said this year's El Niño, a climate phenomenon that can greatly change weather patterns, was the strongest on record.

Winter said: "It's a bit uncertain to say how severe they'll be and how much of an influence El Niño has on the severity of those storms, but we are likely to see more.

"The chances are that one or two of them could be on the higher, more impactful side."

Renouard Le Maistre, who runs Jonathan Le Maistre Tree Surgery, advised people to contact professionals if they were worried about trees.

He said people could get seriously hurt without the proper tools or training.

"The most important thing is make sure you're not out in those conditions because it is really dangerous," he said.
