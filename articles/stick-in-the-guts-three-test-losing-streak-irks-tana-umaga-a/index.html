'Stick in the guts': Three-test losing streak irks Tana Umaga and All Blacks

TheTools · 07.10.2026 20:34 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Sport

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/sport/1761799%2Fstick-in-the-guts-three-test-losing-streak-irks-tana-umaga-and-all-blacks

One of the reasons Dave Rennie asked Tana Umaga to assist the team on defence was his ability to speak to the players as a former All Blacks captain - a man who just about saw it all as a leader, a man too who has the mana to connect on a different level.

So, there would have been a different edge to Umaga's presentation to the All Blacks this week about the importance of the Bledisloe Cup - to him, to the team and to the nation - following three successive defeats to South Africa.

And while those losses at the end of a recent arduous tour would have provided valuable data to the All Blacks' high-performance staff, Umaga, who played 74 tests, stated on Tuesday that they were hard to stomach and must be immediately put right.

"It's probably the one stat we looked at most coming back - that three in the red that we lost," Umaga said after the All Blacks trained next to North Harbour Stadium ahead of Saturday's first test against the Wallabies at Eden Park.

"I can't remember the last time that happened as an All Black. For us it does stick in the guts a bit."

The last time it happened was in fact not long ago - 2022, when the All Blacks lost two to Ireland in Dunedin and Wellington and one to the Springboks in Nelspruit.

They also lost three times to South Africa under head coach (and now selector) Sir Graham Henry in 2009 - in a sequence of two defeats broken by a 19-18 victory over Australia in Sydney.

Incidentally, the last time the All Blacks lost four in a row was in 1998 under head coach John Hart; an historically bad season which finished in a streak of five defeats - three to Australia and two to South Africa.

Tana Umaga guides All Blacks Peter Lakai, left, and Tupou Vaa'i through a defensive drill at training on Tuesday.

Brett Phibbs / www.photosport.nz

"We've got to come out with a response after that - to look at who we're playing, where we're playing and who we're playing for," Umaga added of the challenge presented by the Wallabies who have not won at Eden Park since 1986.

"If that's not enough to get us up I don't know what will be.

"We've had those conversations... there should be not hesitation around understanding what this weekend means to us.

"I was part of the team that won it [Bledisloe Cup] back [in 2003] - it took us a few goes. Some were quite heart-breaking, the ones we lost. I've shared that with the group and what the Cup means to us, the people who have played for it and coached for it.

"It means a lot personally to me... it's up to them now to stake their claim as to how we get it back."

Umaga's "heart-break" at a few losses to Australia will be genuine, but one wonders about how much the other side hurts because the Wallabies have not held the Bledisloe since they lost it 23 years ago to Umaga and company.

Saturday's test will be Umaga's first Bledisloe Cup experience as a coach, but it's not for Rennie, who coached the Wallabies for three years before being controversially sacked in favour of Australian Eddie Jones just months out from the 2023 World Cup.

The All Blacks enjoyed three weeks off after returning from "Rugby's Greatest Rivalry" tour, and there will be a sense of excitement not only at the prospect of playing in front of a home crowd at a place they have not lost at since 1994, but also at the way the opposition will likely play.

The Wallabies' recent victory over a weakened Boks in Perth suggests their set piece has improved significantly, while their work under the high ball is superior to the All Blacks.

All Blacks fullback Damian McKenzie sparks an attack at training.

Brett Phibbs / www.photosport.nz

However, they will play with far more width and ambition than the South Africans - a strategy that carries the risk of being exposed to a counter-attack, and this is one area where the All Blacks showed promise, especially during their first test victory over the Boks at Ellis Park.

And there are few better than Damian McKenzie and Will Jordan, likely to start at fullback and right wing respectively, at seeing and exploiting space.

"Australia bring a different set of issues for us," Umaga said. "They have a mindset about wanting to play so that's great. Yet I'm sure they would have seen and learned some things [about us] in South Africa where we struggled.

"They are playing with a lot more width and with numbers across the field. You can definitely see [new coach] Les Kiss' influence on the side. You have to think that's a positive with the way they've been playing."

Meanwhile, lock or flanker Tupou Vaa'i, who is likely to start in the No.6 jersey on Saturday, said his side would attack to mount a successful defence. An All Blacks victory would lock up the Cup for another year no matter the result a week later in Sydney.

"You've always got to go into a game with that attacking mindset," Vaa'i said.

"We want to get two hands on that trophy and it starts this weekend at Eden Park."
