This Cosmic Retreat in Bali is Unlike Anything You’ve Ever Seen

TheTools · 12.11.2026 02:39 · source · permalink

Wish you were here

Wish you were here

Words by Nadia Bailey · Published on 12 Nov 2026

At Moon Gang, a recently opened boutique stay in Ubud, there are plants that sing to you. The plants grow on an airy terrace overlooking the retreat’s serene infinity pool and soften the interior of the communal lounge. Hooked up to specially designed machines that detect subtle shifts in the plants’ bio-activity – such as electrical fluctuations in their leaves, roots or stem – the plants’ energy is translated into synthesised notes, forming a drifting, ambient soundscape that sets the tone of the resort.

Photo: Courtesy of Moon Gang / Tommaso Riva

These singing plants are a neat encapsulation of the Moon Gang ethos: playful, nature-centric, a little trippy. Born of the collaboration between three women – owner and operator Kirsten Millbank of Adelaide-based Firstbase Studio, designer Yasmine Ghoniem of YSG Studio and creative consultant Tory Bauer of Dance Card – Moon Gang is the kind of place that takes the best parts of luxury accommodation and tilts it into something far more interesting.

Take, for example, the interior design of each of the five suites. Characterised by high ceilings, some with airy mezzanine sleeping spaces, the suites feature cascading linen curtains custom-dyed in kaleidoscopic shades, cloud-like lightshades crafted from banana leaf paper and eclectic artworks sourced at local markets. The custom-designed sits alongside decor and furnishings happened upon by chance, reflecting Millbank’s interest in serendipity and the belief things happen for a reason (another example of this: all three women who worked on Moon Gang have a background in the music industry; Millbank and Bauer also discovered that they’d grown up in the same city and even attended the same high school. They took this as a sign that they were meant to collaborate: “In 30 years of working, I don’t think I’ve ever had such a creative connection with anybody that I have with Kirsten,” says Bauer).

Photo: Courtesy of Moon Gang / Tommaso Riva

To create Moon Gang’s aesthetic, the team scoured old books and tapped into creative networks, relying on word of mouth, personal introductions and chance encounters, rather than algorithmically generated suggestions (the word Bauer likes to use for this process is “foraging”). This approach put them into contact with an eclectic array of collaborators, including Balinese natural dyeing studio Pagi Motley, Indonesian design agency Copyright Reserved and Javanese electronic duo Bottlesmoker, who are the artists behind the singing plants.

“Working really closely with the Indonesian creative community was absolutely key from the beginning,” notes Bauer. “Anything we did, we discussed with them from a cultural point of view and from a creative point of view, so nothing got lost in translation.”

Photo: Courtesy of Moon Gang / Tommaso Riva

Creativity and connection are central here. More than just a place to relax, Moon Gang is calibrated to gently encourage artistic exploration. Rooms are kitted out with film cameras, telescopes and Walkman cassette players, with typewriters available on request. The communal Nongkrong Lounge, centrally located with pool views and a DIY cocktail bar, has a turntable and vinyl library, curated by DJ Munir Harry Septiandry (also known as Midnight Runners), where you’ll find everything from familiar Western classics to Indonesian rarities and underground Japanese pop.

“Music plays such an important part in Indonesia, it’s an undercurrent through their rituals and celebrations,” says Millbank. “We wanted to honour that as a thread throughout this space.” For her, music is a powerful tool for cross-cultural connection. “You might not be able to speak the same language, but you can all listen to a song. It’s something that automatically resets everyone’s nervous systems.”

Photo: Courtesy of Moon Gang / Tommaso Riva

Though the property is intimate in scale, Moon Gang ticks off all the essentials for a luxurious stay. There’s an expansive pool fringed with tropical greenery and electric green recycled plastic lounges, ideal for balmy afternoons; a comprehensive wellness offering including facials and massage with plant-based products by Balinese brand Fields of Yarrow, as well as a sauna, guided meditation, breathwork, yoga and intravenous vitamin infusions; and a genuinely interesting hotel gift shop called Cosmic Kiosk, where you can pick up Moon Gang-branded motocross shirts, zines made by a local artist or a vintage record or two.

In keeping with the esoteric party vibe, an eclectic array of events will be held throughout the year – from DJ sets to natural-dyeing workshops to live performances (the Australian singer-songwriter Mylee Grace recently played a set, for example, with the resort’s multitalented staff moonlighting as back-up musicians). Other paths to enlightenment on offer include a silent disco, moon-bathing and stargazing on the terrace and a midnight running club, with a curated playlist to keep you moving.

Photo: Courtesy of Moon Gang / Tommaso Riva

For Millbank, the aim is to do more than provide a beautiful place to stay – it’s to encourage people to engage with Ubud on a more intentional level. Nods to local tradition are incorporated throughout the space, from the newly blessed timber shrines adorning the suites’ walls to the black and white checkered tile surrounding the pool’s edge, a nod to the Balinese Hindu philosophy of Saput Poleng, or universal balance. As Bauer notes, Moon Gang is about “the luxury of connection, the luxury of learning about Balinese culture”.

It’s also about embracing a sense of cosmic possibility and magical thinking. As Millbank explains, Moon Gang’s name came to her via a phrase she heard by chance on the radio that conjured up visions of “that feeling of still being up and partying, of having the moon above you and living life to its fullest”.
