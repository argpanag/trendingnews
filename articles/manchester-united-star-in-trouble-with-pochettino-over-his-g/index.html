Manchester United star in trouble with Pochettino over his GTA 6 'addiction'

TheTools · 20.11.2026 09:35 · source · permalink

Bryan Mbeumo (Manchester United) was caught playing GTA 6 in the early hours before going to his first training session under the Argentine coach following his unveiling

The world has been waiting 13 years for the realase of the famous video game GTA 6, and sports stars are not immune to the appeal and addictive nature of this game that threatens to bring offices to a standstill (it is expected to be the day with the most workplace absences of the entire year).

Alexander Isak, Liverpool forward, admitted that "yes, I think it's going to be difficult to reach me from the 19th onwards, when GTA 6 comes out. I’ll be completely shut in at home. That’s the plan".

Steven Adams

Conor McGregor, a UFC legend, announced that he would give 400 employees across his various businesses the day off so they could play GTA 6.

Thibaut Courtois, Real Madrid's goalkeeper, shared an image of himself piloting a helicopter in GTA 6.

Various studies maintain that athletes' performance could "collapse" for weeks after the release of GTA 6.

"Pay attention to what happens in November, to performance in the league, in all leagues... GTA 6 comes out. Look at the NFL, look at all the leagues and you’ll see how performance drops, buddy. During the month of November, performance is going to go down," warned Steven Adams, center for the Houston Rockets in the NBA.

Bryan Mbeumo was caught playing GTA 6 in the early hours before his first training session with Pochettino

Bryan Mbeumo a footballer for whom Manchester United paid 82 million euros in 2025, expressed his enthusiasm for the release of GTA 6.

The 27-year-old Franco-Cameroonian forward couldn't resist a late-night session and stayed up late playing GTA 6, while in the morning Mauricio Pochettino, his new coach at United, was beginning work.

Bryan Mbeumo shared the Rockstar game's loading screen on his Instagram "stories" along with the word "historic", just as the clock struck the start of Thursday.

"Now all that's left is to wait; the game will make me stay home every day... after training," Mbeumo confessed before the release of GTA 6.

It’s not realistic to expect a player to stop playing after two hours when they’re completely immersed in the game

Nick Littlehales, a renowned sleep expert for elite athletes, explains in "Ozoon" the effects that playing video games until the early hours of the morning can have: "It completely absorbs you; it is a very compelling distraction that satisfies the competitive spirit. But, just as happens when we mindlessly browse YouTube or social media, it is necessary to establish your own filters and blocks. Otherwise, you can end up doing something truly absurd: playing for hours and hours, which will ultimately harm your performance," he told Ozoon.

Declan Rice

"We know it’s not realistic to expect a player to stop playing exactly at two hours when they’re completely absorbed in the game. You have to accept that and raise the question with them: "Are you willing to sacrifice your nighttime sleep to stay awake and stimulated? Is it worth it?". If they insist, we try to plan for it; for example, allowing them to play late on Tuesdays or Thursdays... It’s better to keep these kinds of conversations open rather than flatly banning nighttime gaming. If you simply impose strict rules on them, they often end up turning to sleeping pills or unregulated supplements they don’t even know, just to be able to keep up the pace," he clarified in statements reproduced by the "Daily Mail".

Declan Rice, Arsenal star, admits in "GQ" that "I can never sleep after night matches. It's impossible; I stay too wound up. I play Call of Duty, Grand Theft Auto, FIFA... anything. I play with a group of friends. It's the adrenaline, all of it... you just can't calm down".
