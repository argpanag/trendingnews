Mildura-Swan Hill and Wimmera local news briefing

Topic:Regional Communities

Here's what's making news in the Mildura-Swan Hill and Wimmera regions today.

Robinvale-raised man jailed in Tonga

A Robinvale-raised man deported from Australia has been sentenced to 12 years' jail over his role in setting up a branch of the Comancheros outlaw motorcycle gang in Tonga.

Eneasi Taumoefolau was found guilty of participating in an organised criminal group.

Justice Elisapeti Langi said his actions showed the threat from "foreign criminal syndicates seeking to use the Pacific nations as transit hubs for organised criminal activities".

The sentence will be served at the same time as a life sentence he is already serving for importing more than two kilograms of methamphetamine into Tonga.

Nick Stevens' fraud conviction stands

Former Carlton and Port Adelaide midfielder Nick Stevens has failed to have his fraud conviction set aside.

The 46-year-old was jailed in June this year after defrauding six Mildura families of $170,000 in 2017 using an unlicensed pool installation business.

He was sentenced to nine months in prison and a two-year order on release.

A jury found him guilty of 12 counts of obtaining financial advantage by deception and acquitted him of one identical charge.

Three appeal judges in the Supreme Court ruled this week that the jury had a rational basis for its verdict.

The judges agreed that the jury considered the charges individually and that the timing of Stevens' initial interaction with one of the victims explained his acquittal.

Paramedics help girl at Halls Gap

Advanced life support paramedics were called to help a high-school-aged girl at Halls Gap on Thursday afternoon.

She was taken to Ararat Hospital in a stable condition.

The day before, VIC SES volunteers from Stawell, Horsham and Ararat were called to help ambulance crews retrieve an injured hiker near Roses Gap.

Crews had to walk to the hiker's location, load her onto a stretcher, and take her to paramedics.

Algae warning for Lake Boga

Visitors to Lake Boga are being told to avoid contact with the water after Goulburn-Murray Water (GMW) detected high levels of blue-green algae.

The lake will remain open to the public, with warning signs in place while the algae is present.

Blue-green algae occurs naturally but produces toxins harmful to people and animals.

Anyone who comes into contact with the water should immediately wash the affected area in clean, cold water.

GMW said it is continuing to monitor algae levels and will remove the warning signs once it is safe to do so.

Gannawarra council elects new mayor

The Gannawarra Shire Council has elected Councillor Ross Stanton as mayor for the remainder of the council term, which is only a month.

Garner Smith resigned at the end of September to stand as the One Nation candidate for the Murray Plains electorate.

Daniel Bolitho is now the deputy mayor.

A new election will be called on November 18.
